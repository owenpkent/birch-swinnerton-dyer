# Reading note: B. Mazur, J. Tate, J. Teitelbaum, "On $p$-adic analogues of the conjectures of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer," Inventiones Mathematicae 84 (1986), 1-48.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the founding statement of the $p$-adic mirror of BSD. It formulates a $p$-adic Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for the $p$-adic $L$-function $L_p(E, s)$ attached to a modular elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$: a predicted order of vanishing at the central point $s = 1$, a predicted leading term involving a $p$-adic regulator and the algebraic data of strong BSD, and (the paper's most consequential discovery) the **exceptional-zero** phenomenon. When $E$ has split multiplicative reduction at $p$, the interpolation factor kills the $p$-adic $L$-value where the complex $L$-value is nonzero, forcing $L_p$ to vanish to one extra order. The corrective factor is the **$\mathcal{L}$-invariant**. The paper conjectures the exceptional-zero order and the value of the derivative; that conjecture was later **proven by Greenberg-Stevens (1993)** in the split multiplicative case. The rest (the higher-rank $p$-adic order-of-vanishing statement) remains a conjecture, exactly as the archimedean BSD does.
>

## One-line takeaway

The $p$-adic $L$-function $L_p(E, s)$ is a genuinely different object from the complex $L(E, s)$: it agrees in order of vanishing at $s = 1$ with the conjectural Mordell-Weil rank in the good-reduction case, but in the **split multiplicative** case it acquires an **extra zero** (the interpolation factor $1 - a_p^{-1}$ degenerates to $1 - 1 = 0$ since $a_p = 1$), so $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L_p = \operatorname{rank} + 1$, with the leading derivative corrected by the **$\mathcal{L}$-invariant** $\mathcal{L}_p(E) = \log_p(q_E)/\operatorname{ord}_p(q_E)$ built from the Tate period $q_E$; the order statement is now a theorem (Greenberg-Stevens), but the higher-rank $p$-adic BSD leading-term formula is, like archimedean BSD, a conjecture.

## Technical content (section by section)

**The $p$-adic $L$-function (Chapter I).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be modular of conductor $N$, $f = \sum a_n q^n$ the associated weight-2 newform, and fix a prime $p$. Using the modular symbols of $E$ (the periods $\{ \lambda(a/m) \}$ obtained by integrating $\omega_E$ along geodesics, normalized by the real period $\Omega_E^{+}$), one forms a measure on $\mathbb{Z}_p^{\times}$ and defines the **$p$-adic $L$-function** $L_p(E, s)$ as the Mellin-Mazur transform of that measure, for $s$ in a $p$-adic neighborhood of the central point. The construction requires choosing a **$p$-stabilization**: a root $\alpha$ of the Hecke polynomial $X^2 - a_p X + p$ (good reduction) with $\operatorname{ord}_p(\alpha)$ controlled so the measure is bounded or admissible.

- **Ordinary case** ($p \nmid N$, $p \nmid a_p$): take $\alpha$ the unit root, $v_p(\alpha) = 0$. The measure is bounded; $L_p(E, s)$ is an Iwasawa function (a power series in $s - 1$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, up to the cyclotomic variable).
- **Supersingular case** ($p \nmid N$, $p \mid a_p$): both roots have positive valuation; the measure is only *admissible* of growth $h$, and one gets two $p$-adic $L$-functions (one per root). MTT handle this with a two-variable / $h$-admissible refinement; Pollack later split these into $\pm$ functions.
- **Multiplicative case** ($p \mid\mid N$): $a_p = +1$ (split) or $a_p = -1$ (nonsplit); the $p$-stabilization is forced, $\alpha = a_p$.

**The interpolation property (Chapter I).** The defining property pins $L_p$ to the complex $L$ at finite-order characters. At the central point the comparison reads, schematically,
$$L_p(E, 1) \;=\; \Bigl(1 - \tfrac{1}{\alpha}\Bigr)^{2} \cdot \frac{L(E, 1)}{\Omega_E^{+}} \quad\text{(good ordinary; one Euler-type factor per place)},$$
where the Euler-like factor $(1 - 1/\alpha)$ is the removed $p$-Euler factor of the complex $L$-function evaluated at the unit root. This factor is the entire source of the exceptional-zero discrepancy: it is a unit in the good case but **vanishes** precisely when $\alpha = 1$.

**The $p$-adic BSD conjecture (the main conjecture of the paper).** Write $r = \operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})$. MTT conjecture an order of vanishing and a leading coefficient for $L_p$ at $s = 1$:

- **Generic (non-exceptional) order.** $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L_p(E, s) = r$ is conjectured, where $r = \operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})$. This is the $p$-adic order predicted to equal the Mordell-Weil rank; tying it to the analytic rank $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E, s)$ requires archimedean BSD, which is itself proven only in analytic rank 0 and 1.
- **Leading-coefficient formula.** The leading term is conjectured to equal
$$\frac{L_p^{(r)}(E, 1)}{r!} \;=\; \Bigl(\textstyle\prod \text{Euler factors at } p\Bigr) \cdot \frac{\operatorname{Reg}_p(E) \cdot \#\operatorname{Sha}(E) \cdot \prod_\ell c_\ell}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}})^2},$$
the $p$-adic analogue of strong BSD, with the **$p$-adic regulator** $\operatorname{Reg}_p(E)$ of the **$p$-adic height pairing** $\langle\,,\rangle_p$ on $E(\mathbb{Q})$ replacing the archimedean Neron-Tate regulator, and the real period $\Omega_E$ absorbed into the modular-symbol normalization. This formula is **conjectural in every rank**, and it is non-degenerate only if the $p$-adic height pairing is non-degenerate, which is itself an open conjecture.

**The exceptional zero and the $\mathcal{L}$-invariant (the paper's signature discovery).** Suppose $p \mid\mid N$ with **split multiplicative** reduction, so $a_p = 1$ and the forced root is $\alpha = 1$. Then the interpolation factor $(1 - 1/\alpha) = 0$, so $L_p(E, 1) = 0$ even when $L(E, 1) \neq 0$ (rank-0 case). The complex and $p$-adic orders no longer agree: the $p$-adic $L$-function picks up **one extra zero**. MTT conjecture the **exceptional-zero order** and the precise derivative:
$$\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L_p(E, s) \;=\; r + 1 \qquad (p \mid\mid N,\ \text{split}),$$
and, in the rank-0 exceptional case, the leading (first) derivative satisfies
$$L_p'(E, 1) \;=\; \mathcal{L}_p(E) \cdot \frac{L(E, 1)}{\Omega_E^{+}} \cdot (\text{Euler factors}),$$
where the **$\mathcal{L}$-invariant** is defined from the **Tate parametrization** $E(\mathbb{Q}_p) \cong \mathbb{Q}_p^{\times}/q_E^{\mathbb{Z}}$ by
$$\mathcal{L}_p(E) \;=\; \frac{\log_p(q_E)}{\operatorname{ord}_p(q_E)},$$
with $q_E \in p\mathbb{Z}_p$ the Tate period of $E$ at $p$, $\log_p$ Iwasawa's $p$-adic logarithm, and $\operatorname{ord}_p$ the normalized valuation. This $\mathcal{L}$-invariant is the precise "defect" measuring how much the $p$-adic world deviates from the complex world at a split multiplicative prime. The general statement (all ranks, split multiplicative) is that the order rises by 1 and the leading $p$-adic term equals $\mathcal{L}_p(E)$ times what strong-BSD would otherwise predict.

**Status of the exceptional-zero conjecture.** The **order** statement $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L_p = r + 1$ in the split multiplicative rank-0 case, together with the $\mathcal{L}$-invariant value of the derivative, was **proven by R. Greenberg and G. Stevens, "$p$-adic $L$-functions and $p$-adic periods of modular forms," Inventiones 111 (1993)**, using Hida families and a two-variable $p$-adic $L$-function (they show $\mathcal{L}_p = -2\, d\log a_p/dk$ along the family, matching the Tate-period definition). Independent / related proofs and reinterpretations were given by Kato-Kurihara-Tsuji, Colmez, and others. So the exceptional-zero piece is a **theorem**; the **higher-rank** $p$-adic BSD leading-term formula remains **open**, as does non-degeneracy of the $p$-adic height pairing.

**Nonsplit multiplicative and additive cases.** For $p \mid\mid N$ **nonsplit** ($a_p = -1$), the factor $(1 - 1/\alpha) = (1 - 1/(-1)) = 2$ is a $p$-adic unit (for $p$ odd), so there is **no** exceptional zero and the generic order conjecture applies. For additive reduction the construction is more delicate and MTT restrict accordingly. The exceptional zero is therefore a **split-multiplicative-only** phenomenon, a sharp local condition at the single prime $p$.

**The supersingular refinement (Chapter II).** When $p \mid a_p$ the measure is unbounded; MTT work with admissible distributions of bounded growth and obtain a $p$-adic $L$-function for each root $\alpha$, with a corresponding pair of $p$-adic BSD conjectures. This is the historical seed of the $\pm$ $p$-adic $L$-functions (Pollack) and the signed Iwasawa theory (Kobayashi) used later in Iwasawa-theoretic proofs of the $p$-part of BSD in the supersingular case.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN here vs the GAP.** PROVEN (by Greenberg-Stevens 1993, downstream of this paper's conjecture): the **exceptional-zero order** $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L_p = r + 1$ and the $\mathcal{L}$-invariant value of the leading $p$-adic derivative, in the **split multiplicative rank-0** case (and the order in general split-mult cases via the same machinery). GAP / still CONJECTURAL: the **higher-rank** $p$-adic BSD order $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L_p = r$ for $r \geq 2$, the **$p$-adic leading-coefficient formula** with $\operatorname{Reg}_p$ in every rank, and **non-degeneracy of the $p$-adic height pairing**. Do not upgrade these: the paper's title says "conjectures" for a reason.

2. **EXACT regime.** This note is **entirely $p$-adic**, not archimedean. The order-of-vanishing statement that is *proven* lives in **analytic rank 0** (the exceptional zero is detected against a nonzero complex $L(E, 1)$). The conjectural part spans all ranks, including the open **rank $\geq 2$** regime. Critically, the object whose order is computed is $L_p$, **not** $L$: the $p$-adic and complex orders **coincide in good reduction** but **differ by 1 in the split multiplicative case**. Conflating the two would be a category error, which is the detector point below.

3. **Detector 1 (parity-only): touched but PASSES with a caveat.** The exceptional zero shifts the order by an *odd* amount ($+1$), so it directly interacts with parity: a $p$-adic root-number / sign argument that did not account for the exceptional zero would miscount the $p$-adic rank's parity. This is the precise reason Research Direction 03 lists a Detector-1 check (the analysis "must yield the order, not just its parity"). The paper itself yields the full order in the proven case, not just parity, so it is not parity-only. Flag: any *downstream* method that reads off "$p$-adic rank parity" must subtract the exceptional contribution first.

4. **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): TRIGGERED in the leading-term conjecture.** The conjectural $p$-adic strong-BSD formula contains $\#\operatorname{Sha}(E)$ explicitly. In rank $\geq 2$ that is the open finiteness assumption, exactly as in archimedean strong BSD. The $p$-adic framing does **not** evade Detector 2: it relocates the Sha-finiteness assumption into the $p$-adic leading term. (The order-of-vanishing exceptional-zero theorem, being rank-0, sits in the proven regime where $\operatorname{Sha}$ finiteness is a theorem; the rank $\geq 2$ leading-term conjecture does not.)

5. **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): NEAR-MISS, a clean PASS but instructive.** The $\mathcal{L}$-invariant and the Tate parametrization $\mathbb{Q}_p^{\times}/q_E^{\mathbb{Z}}$ are *local-at-$p$* analytic-geometry inputs that have a transparent analogue in the function-field / rigid-analytic setting (Tate curves are the same object there). One must be careful: a "$p$-adic proof" that leans entirely on the multiplicative Tate uniformization is using local geometry available at split multiplicative primes, not the missing **global** geometric Frobenius over $\mathbb{Q}$. This paper does **not** claim a global BSD proof, so it passes Detector 3, but it is a useful reminder of where the genuine geometric input lives and where it does not.

6. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** The proven content (exceptional zero) sits firmly on the **rank-0 (proven) side** of the control pair `37a1` / `389a1`. The paper does **nothing new on the rank $\geq 2$ side**: its rank $\geq 2$ statements are conjectural and inherit the same wall as archimedean BSD. So on the project's central line ("proven in rank $\leq 1$" vs "open in rank $\geq 2$"), this paper extends the *vocabulary* (a $p$-adic $L$, a $p$-adic regulator, an $\mathcal{L}$-invariant) but **not the frontier**.

7. **Which direction / experiment it feeds.** Primary feed: **Research Direction 03** ([`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md)). This note pins exactly which factor of strong BSD the $p$-adic side controls: MTT (with Greenberg-Stevens) controls $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L_p$ and, at split multiplicative primes, supplies the $\mathcal{L}$-invariant correction; it does **not** control the archimedean period $\Omega_E$ or the real regulator $\operatorname{Reg}_E$. The missing comparison that Direction 03 names is precisely the $p$-adic-to-archimedean period/regulator bridge (Beilinson-Bloch-Kato), and the $\mathcal{L}$-invariant is a concrete instance of how the two leading terms differ. Secondary feed: it sharpens the parity touchpoint in [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md) (the exceptional zero is an order shift, not a rank shift) and sets up the signed/Euler-system machinery referenced in [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md) (supersingular $\pm$ functions descend from this paper's admissible-measure construction).
