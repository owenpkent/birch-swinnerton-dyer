# Reading note: K. Kato, "p-adic Hodge theory and values of zeta functions of modular forms," Asterisque 295 (2004), 117-290

> **Role in the BSD program.** Kato constructs an Euler system for the Galois representation attached to a modular form (in particular for $E/\mathbb{Q}$) out of Beilinson elements in $K_2$ of modular curves (Siegel units). This Euler system bounds the Selmer group by the $p$-adic $L$-function and proves ONE divisibility of the Iwasawa main conjecture for $GL_2/\mathbb{Q}$. The headline arithmetic consequence: if $L(E,1) \neq 0$ then $E(\mathbb{Q})$ is finite (rank $0$) and the $p$-part of $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$ is finite, for almost all $p$, WITHOUT a CM hypothesis. This is the non-CM generalization of Coates-Wiles / Rubin and the analytic-side companion to Skinner-Urban.

## One-line takeaway

Kato builds a norm-compatible system of Galois cohomology classes (the "zeta elements") from Beilinson elements in $K_2$ of modular curves, computes their image under the $p$-adic dual exponential / regulator map to be the special values of the $L$-function (an explicit reciprocity law), and runs the Euler-system machine to bound the Selmer group above by the $p$-adic $L$-function. This yields one divisibility of the $GL_2$ main conjecture in general, hence Kato's arithmetic corollary: $L(E,1)\neq 0 \Rightarrow \operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q}) = 0$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]$ finite for almost all $p$, with no CM hypothesis. The decisive arithmetic reach of Kato's Euler system alone is analytic rank $0$. The zeta element is a first-order object, so it is the analytic-side twin of the structural ceiling that confines the proven regime to analytic rank $\leq 1$ (the rank-$1$ half of that proven regime is supplied by the Heegner/Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin Euler system, not by Kato).

## Technical content (section by section)

The paper is long (it occupies the whole of Asterisque 295, the bibliographic extent quoted in the title above). The logical spine has four movements: (A) construct the zeta elements from Beilinson / Siegel units; (B) prove they form an Euler system with the right norm relations; (C) the explicit reciprocity law identifying their localization at $p$ with $p$-adic $L$-values; (D) the Euler-system descent giving the Selmer bound, the main-conjecture divisibility, and the BSD-type corollaries.

**Setup and the Galois representation.** For a normalized newform $f$ of weight $k\geq 2$, level $N$, and nebentypus, let $V = V_f$ be the $2$-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_p$-representation of $G_\mathbb{Q} = \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ attached to $f$ (Deligne), realized in the $p$-adic etale cohomology of a Kuga-Sato variety over the modular curve. For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ this specializes to $k=2$ and $V = V_p(E) = T_p(E)\otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$, the rational $p$-adic Tate module. Kato works with the cohomology of the modular curve $Y(N)$ (and its tower), with coefficients, so that twisting by powers and Dirichlet characters is built in.

**(A) Siegel units and Beilinson elements (the source of the Euler system).** The construction begins with *Siegel units* $g_a$ (theta functions / modular units) on modular curves, whose divisors are supported on cusps. Kato forms *Beilinson elements*: cup products of two Siegel units,
$$\{g_a, g_b\} \in K_2(Y(N)) = H^2_{\mathcal{M}}(Y(N), \mathbb{Q}(2)),$$
elements of motivic cohomology ($K_2$ of the open modular curve). Their images under the etale Chern / regulator map land in $H^1$ of the relevant Galois representation. The key formal point: Siegel units satisfy *distribution / norm relations* in the modular tower, which the Beilinson elements inherit. These are the "zeta elements" $z_\gamma$ (Kato's notation in the paper's first part), the input to the Euler system.

**(B) The Euler system and its norm relations.** Pushing the Beilinson elements through etale realization produces a family of classes
$$z_m \in H^1(\mathbb{Z}[1/mp, \mu_m], T)$$
(an integral lattice $T \subset V$), indexed by $m$, satisfying the Euler-system norm-compatibility: the corestriction from level $m\ell$ to level $m$ multiplies $z_{m\ell}$ by the Euler factor $P_\ell(\ell^{-1}\operatorname{Fr}_\ell^{-1})$ of $f$ at $\ell$. This is exactly the Kolyvagin / Rubin axiom for an Euler system, but the input is the Siegel-unit family rather than Heegner points. Kato also produces the variant indexed by Dirichlet characters / the cyclotomic tower, which feeds the Iwasawa-theoretic statement over $\mathbb{Z}_p[[\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q})]]$.

**(C) The explicit reciprocity law (the heart).** This is the technical engine and where "$p$-adic Hodge theory" enters the title. Kato computes the image of the zeta element under the localization-at-$p$ map followed by the *dual exponential map* $\exp^*$ (Bloch-Kato), respectively the Perrin-Riou big logarithm / regulator in the Iwasawa-theoretic version. The result (Kato's explicit reciprocity law, the statement customarily referred to by that name and globalized later) states that
$$\exp^*\big(\operatorname{loc}_p(z_m)\big) = (\text{explicit } L\text{-value of } f, \text{ twisted}) \cdot \omega_f,$$
i.e. the dual exponential of the localized zeta element equals the critical $L$-value (up to explicit period and Euler-factor corrections) times the differential $\omega_f$. In the cyclotomic-deformation form this becomes the statement that the image of the Iwasawa zeta element under the Coleman / Perrin-Riou map is the $p$-adic $L$-function $L_p(f)$ of $f$. This identity converts an arithmetic object (the cohomology class) into the analytic object (the $L$-value), and it is the only place the special values appear. Proving it requires comparing the syntomic / $p$-adic-Hodge realization of the Siegel units with their de Rham / explicit modular description; this is the comparison that the $p$-adic Hodge theory of the title supplies.

**(D) The Euler-system bound, the main-conjecture divisibility, and the corollaries.** With an Euler system in hand whose bottom class localizes to the $p$-adic $L$-function, Kato runs the Euler-system / Kolyvagin-Rubin descent to bound the Selmer group. The Iwasawa-theoretic output is one divisibility of the main conjecture. In the standard formulation for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ good ordinary at $p$, writing $X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)$ for the Pontryagin dual of the $p$-primary Selmer group over the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension and $L_p(E)$ for the $p$-adic $L$-function in $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]]$:
$$\big(\text{char}_\Lambda\, X(E/\mathbb{Q}_\infty)\big) \ \big|\ \big(L_p(E)\big) \qquad \text{(equivalently } L_p(E) \in \text{char}\, X\text{), one inclusion only.}$$
Kato proves the divisibility in the direction "the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer divides (is bounded by) the analytic $p$-adic $L$-function" (the inclusion that an Euler system always gives: the analytic side annihilates / bounds the algebraic side). The reverse divisibility is NOT in this paper; it is Skinner-Urban (via Eisenstein congruences), under hypotheses.

From this Kato extracts the arithmetic theorems. Paraphrasing the central applications (the statements customarily quoted as "Kato's theorem," in the applications part of the paper; I do not have the exact theorem numbers and do not invent them):

1. **Finiteness in analytic rank $0$ (no CM).** If $f$ corresponds to $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and $L(E,1) \neq 0$, then $E(\mathbb{Q})$ is finite and $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]$ is finite for all primes $p$ outside an explicit finite/controlled set (in particular for almost all $p$, and for good ordinary non-anomalous $p$ under the usual hypotheses, with effective control). This is BSD's rank-equality direction "$L(E,1)\neq 0 \Rightarrow \operatorname{rank}=0$" for general (non-CM) $E/\mathbb{Q}$, the case left open by Coates-Wiles/Rubin which required CM.

2. **Mordell-Weil/Selmer bound from $L$-vanishing order.** More generally, the order of vanishing of $L(f,s)$ at the near-central / critical points controls the corank of the relevant Selmer group from above: Kato's Selmer bound says $\operatorname{cork} \operatorname{Sel} \leq \operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L$. Combined with the (modularity-given) functional equation this is consistent with BSD but is an inequality, sharp only when the analytic order is $0$ (or, with the complementary Heegner input, $1$).

3. **The $p$-part of the BSD leading-term formula (rank $0$).** Together with Skinner-Urban's reverse divisibility (separate paper), Kato's divisibility yields the exact $p$-part of the strong-BSD formula for many $E$ in analytic rank $0$: $v_p(\#\operatorname{Sha})$ matches the formula's prediction. Kato alone gives the upper bound (one divisibility); the equality needs the second divisibility.

**Where "p-adic Hodge theory" is doing the work.** The dual exponential map $\exp^*$, the Bloch-Kato exponential, comparison isomorphisms between etale and de Rham/syntomic cohomology, and Perrin-Riou's theory of the big logarithm are the tools that make the explicit reciprocity law (C) provable. The phrase in the title is not decoration: the identification "localized zeta element $=$ $L$-value" is a $p$-adic Hodge comparison.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN here.** (a) An Euler system for $V_f$ from Beilinson/Siegel-unit elements with the correct norm relations. (b) An explicit reciprocity law identifying its $p$-localization with $L$-values / the $p$-adic $L$-function. (c) ONE divisibility of the $GL_2$ main conjecture (analytic side bounds algebraic side). (d) For general $E/\mathbb{Q}$ (no CM): $L(E,1)\neq 0 \Rightarrow \operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q}) = 0$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]$ finite for almost all $p$. This is a genuine THEOREM, not a conjecture.

2. **What is the GAP.** Three layers. (i) Kato gives only one divisibility; the main conjecture as an equality needs Skinner-Urban's reverse divisibility, which carries hypotheses (good ordinary at $p$, irreducibility/ramification conditions). (ii) The Selmer bound is an INEQUALITY $\operatorname{cork}\operatorname{Sel} \leq \operatorname{ord} L$; it is sharp and conclusive only when $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L = 0$ (rank $0$). It does not certify rank equality, nor finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$, once $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L \geq 2$. (iii) Even in rank $0$/$1$ the result is $p$-adic and per-prime: concluding global finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ needs all $p$ together plus a controlled bound on the bad primes; the archimedean leading term ($\Omega_E$, $\operatorname{Reg}_E$) is untouched.

3. **EXACT regime.** Analytic rank $0$ for the clean arithmetic corollary (finite $E(\mathbb{Q})$, finite $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$). The main-conjecture divisibility is a statement over the cyclotomic tower valid in all ranks, but it bounds, it does not equate. Everything is $p$-adic ($p$-adic $L$-functions, $p$-adic Hodge theory, Selmer groups), NOT archimedean: the real period and regulator side of strong BSD are outside the method. So this note sits at: analytic rank $0$ decisive, rank $\geq 2$ untouched, $p$-adic only.

4. **Detector scores.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES.** This is not a parity argument. The Selmer bound and the $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finiteness are honest rank/Sha statements, strictly stronger than rank mod $2$. (Where it stops is an inequality in higher rank, not a parity collapse.)
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES in the regime it covers, and it is exactly the boundary case.** Kato PROVES $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite (for almost all $p$) when $L(E,1)\neq 0$; it does not assume it. Critically, this finiteness is delivered ONLY where the $L$-value forces analytic rank $0$. In the rank $\geq 2$ open regime the method gives no finiteness, so it neither assumes nor proves Sha-finiteness there. It is clean on the detector precisely because it respects the rank wall.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): PASSES.** The construction is intrinsically a number-field / cyclotomic object: Siegel units on modular curves over $\mathbb{Q}$, the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension, Bloch-Kato/Perrin-Riou $p$-adic Hodge theory. There is no geometric Frobenius over a finite field smuggled in; the Euler factors come from Hecke eigenvalues, not from a surface over $\mathbb{F}_q$. No mirage.

5. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** Kato lands squarely on the proven side and shows WHY the wall is where it is from the analytic-side Euler system. The zeta element is a first-order object: the explicit reciprocity law ties it to the value (and first derivative regime) of $L$, so the derived bound is sharp at $\operatorname{ord} L = 0$ and, paired with Heegner input, at $\operatorname{ord} L = 1$, but degenerates to a non-sharp inequality at $\operatorname{ord} L \geq 2$. This is the same structural ceiling that confines Heegner/Kolyvagin to rank $\leq 1$, seen from the Beilinson-element side rather than the Heegner-point side. Two different rank-$1$-input Euler systems, one wall.

6. **Which direction / experiment it feeds.** Primary: Research Direction 02 ([`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md)). Kato is the canonical "rank-input ceiling" the missing rank-$\geq 2$ Euler system must beat: a genuine higher-rank system needs norm relations sensitive to a second-order phenomenon ($\operatorname{ord} L \geq 2$), whereas Kato's Siegel-unit relations only see the value/first-order behavior. The note pins what to beat: a system whose explicit reciprocity law reaches the second derivative, not just the value. Secondary: Direction 03 ([`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md)), since Kato controls $v_p(\#\operatorname{Sha})$ and the $p$-adic $L$-function but not $\Omega_E$ or $\operatorname{Reg}_E$; bridging the $p$-adic bound to the archimedean leading term is the Beilinson-Bloch-Kato gap. Also tightens the atlas rows 3 and 4 (separating "Kato = one divisibility" from "Skinner-Urban = reverse divisibility").
