# Reading note: J. Tate, "On the conjectures of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer and a geometric analog," Seminaire Bourbaki 9, Exp. 306 (1964-1966), 415-440

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the paper that first states the (strong) Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture in its modern, invariant form (rank equality plus the leading-coefficient formula with $\Omega$, $\operatorname{Reg}$, $\prod c_p$, $\#\operatorname{Sha}$, torsion) and, in the same breath, builds the *geometric analog*: for an elliptic surface (or abelian variety) over a global function field $K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$, BSD is reformulated cohomologically and shown to be **equivalent to finiteness of the Brauer group** of the surface. Tate's analog is the canonical source of **Detector 3 (function-field mirage)**: the function-field case is tractable precisely because of geometric Frobenius eigenvalues on $H^2_{\mathrm{et}}$ and the Tate conjecture, structures the number field does not possess. This note is the open-source anchor (numdam.org) for that detector.

## One-line takeaway

Tate's 1966 Bourbaki report gives the first clean, invariant statement of strong BSD over a number field and shows that its geometric analog over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ collapses to a *single finiteness statement*, finiteness of the Brauer group of the associated surface, because over a finite field the $L$-function is a rational function of $q^{-s}$ whose order of vanishing at the center is governed by Frobenius eigenvalues on $\ell$-adic $H^2$; the equality the number field must prove by transcendental and Euler-system methods is, over a function field, a statement about cycles and the Tate conjecture, and that asymmetry is the whole reason the function field is solved and $\mathbb{Q}$ is not.

## Technical content (section by section)

**The arithmetic conjecture (statement of BSD).** Tate states the conjecture for an abelian variety $A$ over a global field $K$, specialized to an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$. The $L$-function $L(A, s) = \prod_v L_v(A, s)$ is the Hasse-Weil product over places, conjecturally continued to $s = 1$. Two assertions:

- **Weak BSD (rank).** $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(A, s) = \operatorname{rank} A(K)$, the Mordell-Weil rank.
- **Strong BSD (leading coefficient).** Writing $r = \operatorname{rank}$, the leading Taylor coefficient is
$$\lim_{s \to 1} \frac{L(A, s)}{(s-1)^r} = \frac{\#\operatorname{Sha}(A) \cdot \operatorname{Reg}(A) \cdot \prod_v c_v \cdot \Omega_A}{(\#A(K)_{\mathrm{tors}})^2}$$
(for the elliptic-curve form, $\Omega_E$ the real period, $\operatorname{Reg}$ the Neron-Tate height regulator, $c_v$ the Tamagawa numbers, $\operatorname{Sha}$ the Tate-Shafarevich group). Tate is explicit that $\#\operatorname{Sha}$ appearing here *presupposes $\operatorname{Sha}$ is finite*: finiteness is part of the conjecture, not a theorem. This invariant packaging (periods, regulator, Tamagawa factors, $\operatorname{Sha}$) is, historically, this paper's contribution; the original Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer numerical conjecture did not isolate all these factors.

**The Selmer and Tate-Shafarevich groups.** Tate recalls the descent exact sequence
$$0 \to A(K)/nA(K) \to \operatorname{Sel}^{(n)}(A) \to \operatorname{Sha}(A)[n] \to 0,$$
the $\operatorname{Sha}$ being the obstruction to computing the rank from local data alone, and notes the conjectural finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ together with the existence of the alternating (Cassels-Tate) pairing on $\operatorname{Sha}$, forcing $\#\operatorname{Sha}$ to be a perfect square when finite (a point Tate had established and which the leading-term formula tacitly uses).

**The geometric setup (function-field analog).** Now let $K = k(C) = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$ be the function field of a smooth projective curve $C/\mathbb{F}_q$, and let $A/K$ be an abelian variety. Tate passes to a *model*: for an elliptic curve $E/K$ one takes the associated **elliptic surface** $\pi: \mathcal{E} \to C$, a smooth projective surface over $\mathbb{F}_q$ whose generic fiber is $E$. The arithmetic of $E/K$ becomes the geometry of the surface $\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_q$. Key dictionary entries:

- The Hasse-Weil $L$-function $L(E/K, s)$ equals (up to the contribution of bad fibers and the trivial factors) the relevant factor of the zeta function of the surface $\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_q$, and is therefore a **rational function of $t = q^{-s}$** by Grothendieck's cohomological formula. Continuation and the functional equation are automatic; there is nothing to prove there.
- By Grothendieck-Lefschetz, $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E/K, s)$ is read off from the multiplicity of the eigenvalue $q$ of geometric Frobenius acting on $H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(\overline{\mathcal{E}}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$.

**The central equivalence (BSD $\Leftrightarrow$ Tate conjecture $\Leftrightarrow$ finiteness of Brauer).** This is the heart of the analog. Tate shows that, in the geometric case, the BSD assertions are equivalent to a statement about algebraic cycles on the surface:

- The **Tate conjecture** $T(\mathcal{E})$ for divisors on the surface $\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_q$ asserts that the $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-span of the cycle classes of divisors equals the Frobenius-invariant ($\times q$ eigenvalue) subspace of $H^2_{\mathrm{et}}$, i.e. the order of the pole of the zeta factor at $t = q^{-1}$ equals the rank of the Neron-Severi group.
- Combining the divisor classes coming from the section group (Mordell-Weil of $E/K$) and from fiber components, the gap between $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L$ and $\operatorname{rank} E(K)$ is exactly controlled by the **Brauer group** $\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal{E})$ of the surface.

Tate's conclusion (made fully precise in his companion Inventiones paper, and in Artin-Tate and Milne 1975): for the elliptic surface $\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_q$,
$$\text{(weak BSD for } E/K) \iff T(\mathcal{E}) \text{ for divisors} \iff \operatorname{Br}(\mathcal{E}) \text{ is finite} \iff \operatorname{Sha}(E/K) \text{ is finite}.$$
Moreover, *when* these finiteness statements hold, the strong-BSD leading-coefficient formula holds as a theorem (the Artin-Tate conjecture, proved by Milne 1975 up to the $p$-part in characteristic $p$, with the $p$-part settled later, including Kato-Trihan 2003): $\#\operatorname{Sha}$, the regulator, the period, and the Tamagawa factors are all read off from the étale cohomology of the surface, and there is a canonical identification $\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal{E}) \cong \operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ (finite together), with a nondegenerate alternating pairing. The function-field strong BSD is thus **conditional only on one finiteness**, and that finiteness is equivalent to the Tate conjecture for divisors on the surface.

**What makes the geometric side tractable.** Two ingredients have no number-field counterpart:

1. **Geometric Frobenius.** Over $\mathbb{F}_q$ the $L$-function is a polynomial in $q^{-s}$ with zeros the Frobenius eigenvalues on $H^2$, of absolute value $q$ (Weil/Deligne purity). The order of vanishing at $s = 1$ is a *cohomological multiplicity*, computable in principle.
2. **Cycles on a surface.** The Mordell-Weil group is realized inside $\operatorname{NS}(\mathcal{E})$ by the Shioda-Tate relation; rank questions become Neron-Severi questions, accessible by the Tate conjecture (known in many cases for surfaces: e.g. for many elliptic surfaces, products, etc.).

Tate emphasizes that the analog is *not a proof of the number-field conjecture*; it is a model that says what a proof would have to deliver. Over $\mathbb{Q} = $ "$\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$" there is no proper curve $C$, no surface $\mathcal{E}$, and no Frobenius acting on a finite-dimensional $H^2$; $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ is not proper and the analog breaks at exactly the geometric input.

**Status of the analog in 1966 and after.** Tate presents the function-field BSD as still *conditional* on finiteness of $\operatorname{Br}$ (equivalently the Tate conjecture for the surface). It is not proven outright in this paper. Subsequent work (Tate's own results, Artin-Tate, Milne 1975, and in the higher-dimensional and characteristic-$p$ settings, Kato-Trihan 2003 for the $p$-part) established BSD over function fields *whenever* $\operatorname{Sha}$/$\operatorname{Br}$ is known finite, and finiteness is known in many families (constant curves, low genus, many elliptic surfaces) but is still **not known in general**. The honest reading: function-field BSD is a theorem *conditional on a finiteness that is itself frequently a theorem*, which is a far stronger position than the number field, where the analogous finiteness ($\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ finite) is known *only* in analytic rank $\leq 1$.

## Project mapping

1. **Proven vs gap.** Over $\mathbb{Q}$ this paper proves *nothing* unconditionally; it states the conjecture. Over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ it reduces BSD to one finiteness (Brauer/$\operatorname{Sha}$ $\Leftrightarrow$ Tate conjecture). The GAP it makes visible is structural: the reduction uses geometric Frobenius and surface cycles, neither available over a number field. The atlas should record function-field BSD as "theorem conditional on finite $\operatorname{Br}$" and number-field BSD as "open, proven only in analytic rank $\leq 1$."

2. **Exact regime.** The function-field equivalences are *rank-agnostic*: they hold for all $r$, including $r \geq 2$, because the order of vanishing is a Frobenius multiplicity, not a Heegner-point count. This is the seductive part. It might suggest the rank-$\geq 2$ wall is an artifact, but the equivalence is powered entirely by geometry the number field lacks. Regime: archimedean (the $L$-function here is genuinely the Hasse-Weil $L$, not a $p$-adic one), but the "leading term" is an algebraic quantity in $q^{-s}$, not a transcendental period, which is itself the tell.

3. **Detector triggers.**
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): THIS IS THE SOURCE.** Tate's analog is the precise template the detector guards against. Any proposed number-field argument that "proves BSD" by a route that runs verbatim over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ has silently imported Grothendieck-Lefschetz / Frobenius on $H^2$ and the Tate conjecture. `function_field_template` in `controls.py` documents exactly this gap; this note is its citation.
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): TRIGGERED (and openly).** Even the function-field strong-BSD formula is *conditional on $\operatorname{Br}(\mathcal{E})$ finite* ($\Leftrightarrow \operatorname{Sha}$ finite). The note must not present function-field BSD as unconditional. Over $\mathbb{Q}$, finiteness is the wall.
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES.** Tate's analog delivers the *full* order of vanishing (a Frobenius multiplicity), not merely its parity, so it is not a parity-only method. But that full order is bought with the geometric input; the modularity root number over $\mathbb{Q}$ gives only parity, and nothing in this paper closes that gap over $\mathbb{Q}$.

4. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** The function-field equivalence treats all ranks uniformly, which is exactly why it is a *control* and not a *method*: it does nothing genuinely new in the number-field rank-$\geq 2$ case because it never touches the number field. It is the boundary marker that tells you when an argument has left the number-field problem entirely.

This is the object a rank-$\geq 2$ proof must construct.
