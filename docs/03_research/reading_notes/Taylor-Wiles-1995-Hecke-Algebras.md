# Reading note: R. Taylor and A. Wiles, "Ring-theoretic properties of certain Hecke algebras," Annals of Mathematics 141 (1995), 553-572

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the patching engine that completes Wiles' proof of modularity for semistable $E/\mathbb{Q}$ (and, downstream, the full BCDT modularity theorem). Wiles reduced the modularity of $E$ to a numerical criterion: that a certain minimal Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}$ is a complete intersection, equivalently that the deformation ring equals the Hecke algebra ($R = \mathbb{T}$) with the rings being complete intersections. Wiles' own argument supplied the Gorenstein property (after Mazur) but not the complete-intersection property in the form needed. This paper supplies it, by the Taylor-Wiles patching method: add auxiliary primes $Q_m$ to relax the deformation problem, prove $\mathbb{T}_Q$ is free over a group-ring $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$, then take a limit over a tower of such $Q_m$ to force the deformation ring onto a power-series ring and conclude $R \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{T}$. The payoff for BSD is exactly the payoff of modularity: $L(E,s)$ acquires analytic continuation, a functional equation, and a root number $w$. Root number gives the **parity** of the analytic rank, nothing more.
>

## One-line takeaway

This paper finishes the modularity proof by proving the minimal Hecke algebra is a complete intersection: it adds auxiliary "Taylor-Wiles primes" $Q_m$ (with $q \equiv 1 \bmod p^m$, $\overline{\rho}$ unramified at $q$, $\overline{\rho}(\mathrm{Frob}_q)$ with distinct eigenvalues) to relax the deformation problem so the enlarged Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}_Q$ is free over a group ring $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$, then patches a tower of these to identify the deformation ring $R$ with $\mathbb{T}$ as a power-series-ring quotient, giving $R \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{T}$ complete intersection; the entire output for BSD is the existence of the Hasse-Weil $L$-function with its functional equation and root number, hence only the **parity** of the analytic rank, never the rank itself and never anything about $\mathrm{Sha}$ in rank $\geq 2$.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Introduction.** States the purpose precisely: "to provide the missing ingredient in [W2] by establishing that the Hecke algebras considered there are complete intersections." Records that Mazur's method [M] gives only that the algebras are *Gorenstein*, which is "too weak for the purposes of that paper." The methods here are "related to those of chapter 3 of [W2]." Acknowledges that Faltings suggested a simplification, reproduced in the appendix.

**Section 1 (Notation).** Fixes an odd prime $p$, the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}$ of a finite extension $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ with maximal ideal $\lambda$ and residue field $k = \mathcal{O}/\lambda$. Fixes a continuous representation
$$\overline{\rho} : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \longrightarrow GL_2(k)$$
satisfying the standard hypotheses that make the Wiles machine run:
- $\overline{\rho}$ is **modular** (a $\bmod p$ representation of some modular newform);
- the restriction of $\overline{\rho}$ to $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p}))$ is **absolutely irreducible** (this is the crucial irreducibility hypothesis that powers the whole patching);
- $\det \overline{\rho}(c) = -1$ for complex conjugation $c$ (odd);
- prescribed local behavior at $p$ (ordinary/flat dichotomy: either $\overline{\rho}|_{G_p}$ is $\begin{pmatrix}\psi_1 & * \\ 0 & \psi_2\end{pmatrix}$ with $\psi_1, \psi_2$ distinct and $\psi_2$ unramified, or induced from a character of the unramified quadratic extension whose inertia restriction is the level-2 fundamental character), and prescribed behavior at $\ell \neq p$.

The **auxiliary prime sets** $Q$ are finite sets of primes $q$ with: $\overline{\rho}$ unramified at $q$; $q \equiv 1 \bmod p$; and $\overline{\rho}(\mathrm{Frob}_q)$ having distinct eigenvalues $\alpha_q, \beta_q$. For $q \in Q$, $\Delta_q$ is the Sylow $p$-subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$ and $\Delta_Q = \prod_{q \in Q}\Delta_q$. The point of $q \equiv 1 \bmod p$ is precisely that $\Delta_q$ is nontrivial, giving room for the group-ring action.

$\mathbb{T}(\Gamma_Q)$ is the Hecke algebra on weight-2 cusp forms for $\Gamma_Q$; $\mathfrak{m}$ is the maximal ideal cut out by $\overline{\rho}$ (via $T_\ell - \overline{\rho}(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell)$ data and the $U_q - \alpha_q$ conditions); $\mathbb{T}_Q$ is the localization at $\mathfrak{m}$, which is **reduced**. There is a natural map $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q] \to \mathbb{T}_Q$, and a modular Galois representation $\rho_Q^{\mathrm{mod}}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(\mathbb{T}_Q)$ lifting $\overline{\rho}$, normalized to a twist $\rho_Q'$ with $\det \rho_Q' \in \mathcal{O}^\times$.

The **main theorem** is stated (with $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{T}_\emptyset$):

> **Theorem 1.** *The ring $\mathbb{T}$ is a complete intersection.*

The strategy is recorded immediately: one writes $\wp_Q = \ker(\pi_Q : \mathbb{T}_Q \to \mathcal{O})$ and the congruence ideal $\eta_Q = \pi_Q(\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathbb{T}_Q}(\wp_Q))$, with the numerical criterion that $\#(\wp_Q/\wp_Q^2) \geq \#(\mathcal{O}/\eta_Q)$ always, **with equality iff $\mathbb{T}_Q$ is a complete intersection** (appendix of [W2] or Lenstra [L], using reducedness).

**Section 2 (Generalisation of a result of de Shalit).** The freeness theorem, the technical heart:

> **Theorem 2.** *The ring $\mathbb{T}_Q$ is a free $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$-module of $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$-rank equal to the $\mathcal{O}$-rank of $\mathbb{T}$.*

Proof method: introduce a single auxiliary prime $R$ (Diamond-Taylor [DT] lemma 3) chosen so that the modified groups $\Gamma_Q'$, $\Gamma_{Q-}'$ act **freely** on the upper half plane, making them free groups and the relevant cohomology computable. Lemma 1 gives $\mathbb{T}_Q' \cong \mathbb{T}_Q$ and $\mathbb{T}_{Q-}' \cong \mathbb{T}$. Proposition 1 then proves $H^1(Y_Q', \mathcal{O})^-$ is a free $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$-module of rank equal to the $\mathcal{O}$-rank of $H^1(Y_{Q-}', \mathcal{O})^-$, using that the cocycles $Z^1(\Gamma_{Q-}', \mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q])$ are free over $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$ (free group) and Shapiro's lemma. Irreducibility of $\overline{\rho}$ identifies the minus cohomology with $\mathbb{T}_Q'$ itself.

The corollaries propagate the consequences:
- **Corollary 1.** $\mathbb{T}_{Q\cup\{q\}}/(\delta_q - 1) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{T}_Q$, with $(\delta_q - 1)$ and $(1 + \delta_q + \dots + \delta_q^{\#\Delta_q - 1})$ mutual annihilators.
- **Corollary 2.** *If for some $Q$ the ring $\mathbb{T}_Q$ is a complete intersection then so is $\mathbb{T}$.* (Via Kunz [K1]: adjoining $f = \delta_q - 1$ to a complete intersection $S$ with $S/f$ free gives $S/f$ a complete intersection.)
- **Corollary 3.** $\eta_Q = \eta \cdot \#\Delta_Q$.
- **Corollary 4.** $\#(\mathfrak{a}_Q\mathbb{T}_Q / \wp_Q \mathfrak{a}_Q \mathbb{T}_Q)\cdot\#(\mathcal{O}/\eta) = \#(\mathcal{O}/\eta_Q)$.

**Section 3 (Some Algebra).** Establishes the commutative-algebra criterion for being a complete intersection, generalizing the appendix of [W2] by working with Lenstra [L] so the Gorenstein hypothesis is dropped. For a finite flat reduced local $\mathcal{O}$-algebra $T$ with section $\pi: T \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}$, and $R \twoheadrightarrow T$ with kernel $J_R$, $\wp_R = \ker \pi_R$, $\eta_R = \pi_R(\mathrm{Ann}_R(\wp_R))$, and $\Psi_R = (\wp_R^2 \cap J_R)/\wp_R J_R$, there is an exact sequence
$$(0) \to \Psi_R \to J_R/\wp_R J_R \to \wp_R/\wp_R^2 \to \wp_T/\wp_T^2 \to (0),$$
with $\#\Psi_R < \infty$.

> **Lemma 2.** *Suppose $\#(\wp_T/\wp_T^2) \leq \#(\mathcal{O}/\eta_T)\#\Psi_R$ and $\#(\mathcal{O}/\eta_T)\#(J_R/\wp_R J_R) \leq \#(\mathcal{O}/\eta_R) < \infty$, and $R$ is a finite flat $\mathcal{O}$-algebra. Then $R$ is a complete intersection.*

Lemma 3 gives $\#(\mathcal{O}/\eta_R) \leq \#(J_R/\wp_R J_R)\#(\mathcal{O}/\eta_T)$ via Fitting ideals. Lemma 4 gives, for $R$ a complete intersection finite flat over $\mathcal{O}$ with $\eta_R \neq 0$, $\#(\wp_T/\wp_T^2) \leq \#(\mathcal{O}/\eta_T)\#\Psi_R$. The patching proposition assembles these into a limit statement:

> **Proposition 2.** *Suppose for a series of sets $Q_n$ there are ideals $I_n$ in $\mathbb{T}_{Q_n}$ with: (1) $I_n \subset \mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{T}_{Q_n}}^2$ and $\mathbb{T}_{Q_n}/I_n$ finite; (2) $I_{n+1}\mathbb{T} \subset I_n \mathbb{T}$ and $\bigcap_n I_n \mathbb{T} = (0)$; (3) compatible surjections $\mathbb{T}_{Q_{n+1}}/I_{n+1} \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{T}_{Q_n}/I_n$ over the tower; (4) $\varprojlim \mathbb{T}_{Q_n}/I_n$ is a power series ring. Then for $n$ sufficiently large $\mathbb{T}_{Q_n}$ is a complete intersection, and hence $\mathbb{T}$ is a complete intersection (by corollary 2 of theorem 2).*

The proof patches: $P = \varprojlim \mathbb{T}_{Q_n}/I_n$, with $\Psi_P \xrightarrow{\sim} \Psi_{Q_n}$ for large $n$, yielding the chain of inequalities $\#(\wp/\wp^2) \leq \#(\mathcal{O}/\eta)\#\Psi_P = \#(\mathcal{O}/\eta)\#\Psi_{Q_n}$, which forces equality in the complete-intersection criterion via Corollary 4 and Lemma 2. Corollary 1 of Proposition 2 packages the input one actually verifies: given an integer $r$ and sets $Q_m$ with $q \equiv 1 \bmod p^m$ for $q \in Q_m$, $\overline{\rho}$ unramified with distinct Frobenius eigenvalues at each $q$, $\#Q_m = r$ (fixed), and $\mathbb{T}_{Q_m}$ generated as an $\mathcal{O}$-algebra by $r$ elements, then $\mathbb{T}$ is a complete intersection. The "level $n$ structure" bookkeeping shows the inverse limit is a finite free $\mathcal{O}[[S_1,\dots,S_r]]$-module of Krull dimension $r+1$ and a quotient of $\mathcal{O}[[T_1,\dots,T_r]]$, hence equal to it: a power series ring.

**Section 4 (Galois Cohomology).** Produces the required sets $Q_m$. Defines the Selmer-type groups $H^1_Q(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho})$ and the dual $H^1_{Q^*}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho}(1))$ via local conditions $H^1_f$ at each place (the flat/ordinary conditions at $p$, unramified-up-to-$I_\ell$ at $\ell \neq p$). The two key cohomological inputs:

> **Lemma 5.** $\dim_k H^1_Q(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho}) \leq \dim_k H^1_{Q^*}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho}(1)) + \#Q.$

(Proved via Wiles' formula, proposition 1.6 of [W2], computing local terms $h_\ell$; the Fontaine-Laffaille category $\mathcal{M}$ of [FL] is invoked to control the local term at $p$, including the dichotomy $\dim_k \mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathcal{M}}(M(\overline{\rho}), M(\overline{\rho})) = 2$ if $\overline{\rho}|_{G_p}$ is indecomposable and $3$ otherwise.)

> **Lemma 6.** $\mathbb{T}_Q$ can be generated as an $\mathcal{O}$-algebra by $\dim_k H^1_Q(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho})$ elements.

(Via an embedding $\kappa: \mathrm{Hom}_k(\mathfrak{m}_Q/(\mathfrak{m}_Q^2, \lambda), k) \hookrightarrow H^1_Q(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho})$, the tangent-space-to-Selmer map.) Combining: by shrinking $Q_m$ one arranges $\#Q_m = \dim_k H^1_\emptyset(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho}(1))$ exactly (a fixed number $r$ independent of $m$), with $H^1_{Q_m}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho}(1))$ killed, so $\dim_k H^1_{Q_m}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho}) \leq \#Q_m$ and $\mathbb{T}_{Q_m}$ is generated by $\#Q_m = r$ elements. Chebotarev produces the actual primes $q$ realizing the required local classes. The main theorem follows from Corollary 1 of Proposition 2.

**Appendix (Faltings' simplification).** Reproves the result in the cleaner deformation-ring language and yields the explicit $R = \mathbb{T}$ statement. Defines deformations of type $Q$: complete noetherian local $\mathcal{O}$-algebras $A$ with a lift $\rho: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(A)$ of $\overline{\rho}$ with the prescribed local conditions, giving a universal deformation $\rho_Q^{\mathrm{univ}}: G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to GL_2(R_Q)$ with $\mathrm{Hom}_k(\mathfrak{m}_{R_Q}/(\lambda, \mathfrak{m}_{R_Q}^2), k) \cong H^1_Q(\mathbb{Q}, \mathrm{ad}^0\overline{\rho})$ and a natural surjection $R_Q \to \mathbb{T}_Q$.

> **Theorem 3.** $R \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{T}$ *and these rings are complete intersections.*

Lemma 7 shows that for $q \in Q$, $\rho_Q^{\mathrm{univ}}|_{G_q} \sim \begin{pmatrix}\phi_1 & 0 \\ 0 & \phi_2\end{pmatrix}$ with $\phi_1|_{I_q} = \phi_2^{-1}|_{I_q}$ factoring through $\chi_q: I_q \twoheadrightarrow \Delta_q$, which makes $R_Q$ an $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$-algebra with $R_Q/\mathfrak{a}_Q \xrightarrow{\sim} R$. Proposition 3 is the clean patching statement: given $R \twoheadrightarrow T$ ($T$ finite flat over $\mathcal{O}$) and, for each $n$, compatible squares with $\mathcal{O}[[X_1,\dots,X_r]] \twoheadrightarrow R_n$, $(S_1,\dots,S_r)R_n \subset \ker(R_n \twoheadrightarrow R)$, $(S_1,\dots,S_r)T_n = \ker(T_n \twoheadrightarrow T)$, and $T_n$ a finite free $\mathcal{O}[[S_1,\dots,S_r]]/\mathfrak{b}_n$-module with $\mathfrak{b}_n \subset ((1+S_1)^{p^n}-1, \dots)$, then **$R \xrightarrow{\sim} T$ and both are complete intersections.** The proof builds infinitely many "$n$-structures," picks a compatible cofinal system (pigeonhole, finitely many iso classes at each level), and passes to a limit $R_\infty' \twoheadrightarrow T_\infty'$ where $T_\infty'$ is finite free over $k[[S_1,\dots,S_r]]$ of Krull dimension $r$, forcing $k[[X_1,\dots,X_r]] \xrightarrow{\sim} R_\infty' \xrightarrow{\sim} T_\infty'$, hence $R \xrightarrow{\sim} T$ and $T \cong k[[X_1,\dots,X_r]]/(S_1,\dots,S_r)$, a complete intersection of dimension 0.

A scope caveat stated explicitly in the appendix: these arguments (and chapter 3 of [W2]) prove conjecture 2.16 of [W2] **for the minimal Hecke ring and minimal deformation problem only**. The full theorem 3.3 of [W2] additionally needs theorem 2.17 and chapter 2 of [W2] (the level-raising / "induction on the level" that moves from the minimal case to the general one). This note's result is the minimal-case engine, not the entire modularity lifting theorem by itself.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN here.** A pure commutative-algebra and Galois-deformation result: the minimal Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}$ is a complete intersection, and (Faltings appendix) the minimal deformation ring equals it, $R \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{T}$, both complete intersections. Logically this is the missing numerical criterion of [W2]. Composed with [W2]'s chapters 2 and the $3$-$5$ switch, it yields: every semistable $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is modular (and downstream, with BCDT, every $E/\mathbb{Q}$).

2. **The GAP relative to BSD.** Modularity delivers the analytic object: $L(E,s)$ extends to an entire function with functional equation $s \leftrightarrow 2-s$ and root number $w = \pm 1$. That is the *existence* of the left-hand side of BSD and the *parity* $w = (-1)^{\text{analytic rank}}$. It delivers **nothing** about the Mordell-Weil rank as an integer, nothing about rational points, and nothing about $\mathrm{Sha}$. The whole content of BSD (rank equality, finiteness and order of $\mathrm{Sha}$) is downstream and untouched.

3. **EXACT regime.** Rank-agnostic on the analytic side: it makes the $L$-function exist for every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ regardless of rank, but the consequence one can extract (parity) is only a congruence on the rank, not the rank. **Archimedean** (it is the classical Hasse-Weil $L$-function and its functional equation that result). The internal machinery is $p$-adic (deformations of $\bmod p$ Galois representations, $p$-adic Hecke algebras), but the BSD-facing output is the archimedean $L$-function. It does not by itself produce a $p$-adic $L$-function or a $p$-adic BSD statement (that is the Iwasawa thread, [`iwasawa_skinner_urban.md`](iwasawa_skinner_urban.md), which reuses this technology).

4. **Detectors.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): TRIGGERED, by design and by lineage.** This is the canonical parity-level result. The only rank information modularity yields is $w$, i.e. rank mod 2. A method whose BSD payoff factors through "modularity, hence root number" cannot close the rank equality. This note is the reference instance of that detector firing cleanly. Flag: anything in the program that claims to get the rank "from modularity" is at best getting parity.
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES (not applicable).** The paper makes no use of and asserts nothing about $\mathrm{Sha}$. It does not assume Sha-finiteness anywhere; it is a statement about Hecke algebras and deformation rings.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): PASSES (not applicable).** The argument is intrinsically over $\mathbb{Q}$ (modular curves, $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ deformations, $q \equiv 1 \bmod p^m$ primes from Chebotarev over $\mathbb{Q}$). The Frobenius elements $\mathrm{Frob}_q$ used here are arithmetic Frobenii at finite primes of $\mathbb{Q}$, supplied by Chebotarev, not the geometric Frobenius of a curve over $\mathbb{F}_q$ that the detector watches for. Nothing in the proof relies on a base-field Frobenius acting on the whole geometry, so the result is genuinely a number-field theorem. (The *technique* of Taylor-Wiles patching does have function-field analogues, but that is a statement about the method, not an import of function-field structure into this theorem.)

5. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This note sits *before* the control pair: it is the precondition (existence of the $L$-function) that both arms of the pair presuppose. It does nothing to distinguish rank $\leq 1$ from rank $\geq 2$, and crucially the patching engine here is **not** the rank-$\geq 2$ object the program needs. Patching constructs an isomorphism of rings; it does not construct rational points. So it cannot be the "one missing object" of the atlas.

6. **Research direction / experiment fed.** Primarily a **boundary marker** for [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md) and the parity discussion in [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md): it pins down exactly where the parity ceiling sits and why "use modularity" is not a route past it. Its *methodology* (the $R = \mathbb{T}$ / Taylor-Wiles patching system, freeness over $\mathcal{O}[\Delta_Q]$, the numerical complete-intersection criterion) is the direct ancestor of the Euler-system and main-conjecture technology in [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md) and the Skinner-Urban work in [`iwasawa_skinner_urban.md`](iwasawa_skinner_urban.md). On the experimental side it underwrites the substrate: that $L(E,s)$ and its analytic continuation exist at all (used by `experiments/l_function_rank/` and the root-number/parity controls) is a consequence of the modularity this paper completes.
