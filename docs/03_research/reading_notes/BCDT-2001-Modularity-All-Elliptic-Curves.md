# Reading note: C. Breuil, B. Conrad, F. Diamond, R. Taylor, "On the modularity of elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$: wild 3-adic exercises," Journal of the AMS 14 (2001), 843-939

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the paper that closes modularity for EVERY $E/\mathbb{Q}$, removing the semistability hypothesis that Wiles (1995) and Taylor-Wiles (1995) had required. The payoff for BSD is structural and universal but strictly upstream of the rank equality: once every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is modular, $L(E, s)$ equals $L(f, s)$ for a weight-2 newform $f$ of level $N(E)$, hence $L(E, s)$ has analytic continuation to all of $\mathbb{C}$ and a functional equation $s \leftrightarrow 2 - s$. The sign of that functional equation (the root number $w$) gives the parity of the analytic rank for ALL $E/\mathbb{Q}$, unconditionally. This is the floor BSD now stands on: the left-hand side of $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E,s) = \operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})$ is now a well-defined integer for every curve, and its parity is pinned. The order of vanishing itself is not.

## One-line takeaway

Every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ is modular (Theorem 2.2.2, p. 19), proved by completing the residually-irreducible 3-adic modularity-lifting machinery of Wiles and Taylor-Wiles through the hardest wildly ramified cases at the prime 3 (conductor-at-3 types $f = 27, 81, 243$), with the residually-reducible-at-3 cases handled via the prime 5 (Theorem B / Theorem 2.2.1) and a residual fallback to a finite explicit list. The output for BSD is universal analytic continuation, a functional equation, and a well-defined root number giving the parity of the analytic rank for ALL $E/\mathbb{Q}$. It is a parity-level, not a rank-level, input: it triggers Detector 1.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Introduction. The two headline theorems (p. 1).** The paper states two theorems up front.

- **Theorem A.** If $E_{/\mathbb{Q}}$ is an elliptic curve, then $E$ is modular.
- **Theorem B.** If $\overline{\rho} : \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_5)$ is an irreducible continuous representation with cyclotomic determinant, then $\overline{\rho}$ is modular.

Theorem A is the goal; Theorem B is the engine for the residually-reducible-at-3 cases. Both are proved in section 2.2.

**The meaning of "modular" (pp. 1-3).** For an eigenform $f \in S_k(N)$ there is a unique continuous irreducible $\rho_{f,\lambda} : \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\lambda)$ with $\operatorname{tr} \rho_{f,\lambda}(\operatorname{Frob}_p) = a_p(f)$ for $p \nmid N\ell$. For an elliptic curve $E_{/\mathbb{Q}}$, with $\rho_{E,\ell}$ the Galois action on the $\ell$-adic Tate module and $\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}$ on the $\ell$-torsion, the paper records six equivalent formulations of "$E$ is modular" (p. 3), including: (1) $L(E,s) = L(f,s)$ for some eigenform $f$; (2) $L(E,s) = L(f,s)$ for some eigenform $f$ of weight 2 and level $N(E)$; (3) $\rho_{E,\ell}$ is modular for some $\ell$; (4) $\rho_{E,\ell}$ is modular for all $\ell$; (5) there is a non-constant holomorphic map $X_1(N)(\mathbb{C}) \to E(\mathbb{C})$ for some $N$; (6) there is a non-constant morphism $X_1(N(E)) \to E$ defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. The equivalences cite Carayol, Faltings, Shimura, and Mazur. This is the precise sense in which modularity yields the analytic continuation and functional equation of $L(E,s)$: it is identified with the $L$-series of a weight-2 newform.

**The three-case division of the proof of Theorem A (p. 3).** The strategy splits on the residual behavior at 3 and 5.

1. If $\overline{\rho}_{E,5}|_{\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 5))}$ is irreducible, show $\rho_{E,5}$ is modular.
2. If that is reducible but $\overline{\rho}_{E,3}|_{\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}))}$ is absolutely irreducible, show $\rho_{E,3}$ is modular.
3. If both are reducible, then $E$ is isogenous to a curve with $j$-invariant $0$, $(11/2)^3$, or $-5(29)^3/2^5$, and these specific low-conductor curves are checked directly against tables (with E. Kani / Elkies' help, in [CDT]).

In cases 1 and 2 one first proves $\overline{\rho}_{E,\ell}$ is modular (via the Langlands-Tunnell theorem applied to the residual mod-3 picture, the classical starting point also used by Wiles), then lifts to $\rho_{E,\ell}$. The genuinely new content is case 2 in the wild 3-adic situation.

**The six $\overline{\rho}_{E,3}$ cases (pp. 3-4).** After twisting by a quadratic character, $\overline{\rho}|_{\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_3/\mathbb{Q}_3)}$ falls into one of six types, labeled by the conductor at 3, the cases $f = 1, 3, 9, 27, 81, 243$. The first three ($f = 1, 3, 9$) were handled in earlier work ([CDT], [Di2]) where $E$ has good or semistable reduction at 3. The three new cases $f = 27, 81, 243$ are the "wild" ones: $E$ acquires good reduction only over a wildly ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_3$, and the local condition to impose on the deformations is potentially Barsotti-Tate of a specified type, including in $f = 243$ a full specification of the restriction of the Weil-group representation (not just its inertial restriction). The paper notes this completeness of specification is "a rare phenomenon in the wild case" and is what makes the Taylor-Wiles patching argument go through.

**The technical engine, sections 3-9 (Contents, p. 6).** The body of the paper develops the tools the wild cases need:
- section 3 (Admittance, p. 20): when a type $\tau$ "admits" a residual $\overline{\rho}$, the matching condition between local types and modular forms.
- section 4 (New deformation problems, p. 22): the deformation rings and their reduction steps, generalities on group schemes and deformation theory, and Galois cohomology.
- section 5 (Breuil modules, p. 34): a new description (due to Breuil) of finite flat group schemes over the ring of integers of a $p$-adic field in terms of semilinear-algebra data. This is the technical innovation that makes the local deformation-ring computations tractable in the wild case.
- section 6 (Some local fields, p. 47): the explicit local field calculations for the types $\tau_1, \tau_{-1}, \tau_3, \tau_{-3}, \tau_i'$.
- sections 7, 8, 9 (Proofs, including the rank-one calculations and the finite-flat-group-scheme computations): the long, delicate computations, "particularly in the case $f = 243$," that produce local deformation rings in accord with the predictions of the local Langlands correspondence and the modular representation theory of $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_3)$.

**The main modularity-lifting theorems (section 1.4, p. 12).** These are the abstract statements that drive cases 1 and 2.

- **Theorem 1.4.1.** Let $\ell$ be an odd prime, $K$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ with residue field $k$. Let $\rho : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \operatorname{GL}_2(K)$ be an odd continuous representation ramified at only finitely many primes. Assume its reduction $\overline{\rho} : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \operatorname{GL}_2(k)$ is absolutely irreducible after restriction to $\mathbb{Q}\big(\sqrt{(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\,\ell}\big)$ and is modular. Suppose further that $\overline{\rho}|_{G_\ell}$ has centralizer $k$, that $\rho|_{G_\ell}$ is potentially Barsotti-Tate with $\ell$-type $\tau$, that $\tau$ admits $\overline{\rho}$, and that $\tau$ is weakly acceptable for $\overline{\rho}$. Then $\rho$ is modular.
- **Theorem 1.4.2.** The same statement with "extended $\ell$-type $\tau'$" in place of $\ell$-type $\tau$ (this is the version needed for the $f = 243$ extended-type case).

The proofs run "verbatim" along the proof of Theorem 7.1.1 of [CDT], with an explicit list of substitutions (the note on pp. 12-14 spells out which lemmas and pages of [CDT] are modified, e.g. on pp. 539, 541, 546, 547, 549-551). The logical shape is the standard $R = \mathbb{T}$ identification of a universal deformation ring with a Hecke algebra.

**Conjecture 1.3.1 (p. 12) and its verified special cases.** The starting point is a conjecture about when a type $\tau$ (or extended type $\tau'$) "admits" / "simply admits" / is "acceptable" for $\overline{\rho}$, phrased in terms of nonvanishing of a deformation invariant $R^D_{V,0}$. The paper does not prove the conjecture in general; it verifies exactly the special cases it needs, stated as Theorem 2.1.2, Theorem 2.1.4, Theorem 2.1.6 (weak acceptability for the relevant $\overline{\rho}|_{I_3}$ shapes) plus the supporting Lemmas 2.1.1, 2.1.3, 2.1.5 (the "simply admits" statements). These are the inputs that sections 3-9 establish by direct computation.

**Section 2.1, the 3-types (pp. 15-16).** With $\ell = 3$ fixed and $\zeta \in \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_3)$ a primitive cube root of 1, the paper defines the specific types $\tau_1, \tau_{-1}, \tau_3, \tau_{-3}$ (order-3 and order-6 characters of explicit local units) and the extended types $\tau_i'$ (eq. 2.1.1). These are "up to twist, a complete list of the wildly ramified types which can arise from elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}_3$." This is the precise local-representation-theoretic inventory the wild cases must cover.

**Theorem B as Theorem 2.2.1 (p. 17).** Any continuous absolutely irreducible $\overline{\rho} : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_5)$ with cyclotomic determinant is modular. The proof classifies $\overline{\rho}|_{G_3}$ into six explicit local cases (characters of $\mathbb{Q}_3^\times$, $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{-1})^\times$, $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt 3)^\times$, $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{-3})^\times$, pp. 17-18), then for each case uses Ekedahl's Hilbert Irreducibility Theorem and the Manoharmayum surface $X_{\overline{\rho}}$ (from [SBT], the Shepherd-Barron-Taylor circle of ideas) to find an elliptic curve $E_1/\mathbb{Q}$ with $\overline{\rho}_{E_1,5} \cong \overline{\rho}$ and $\overline{\rho}_{E_1,3}$ of a controlled très ramifié shape, surjective onto $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)$. Proposition B.4.2 of [CDT] then makes $\rho_{E_1,3}$ potentially Barsotti-Tate of one of the six types, $\rho_{E_1,3}$ is modular by Theorems 1.4.1/1.4.2, the Langlands-Tunnell input makes $\overline{\rho}_{E_1,3}$ modular, and $\overline{\rho} = \overline{\rho}_{E_1,5}$ is therefore modular.

**The headline theorem, Theorem 2.2.2 (p. 19).** Combining Theorem 2.2.1 with Theorem 7.2.4 of [CDT]:

> **Theorem 2.2.2.** Every elliptic curve defined over the rational numbers is modular.

This is Theorem A. It is the universal statement: no semistability, no conductor restriction, every $E/\mathbb{Q}$.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN.** Every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is modular (Theorem 2.2.2). Concretely: $L(E,s) = L(f,s)$ for a weight-2 newform $f$ of level $N(E)$, so $L(E,s)$ extends to an entire function with a functional equation relating $s$ and $2 - s$, and the root number $w = \pm 1$ in that functional equation is defined for every curve. By the analytic side of BSD, $w = (-1)^{\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E,s)}$, so the parity of the analytic rank is pinned for ALL $E/\mathbb{Q}$.

2. **The GAP.** Modularity gives the parity of $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E,s)$, not the integer itself, and says nothing about $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})$. The rank equality $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E,s) = \operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})$ is not touched here. (The companion parity result, that the Mordell-Weil rank mod 2 also matches, is a separate theorem of Nekovar and the Dokchitsers, and is itself still only parity.) The leading coefficient, the regulator, the period, and $\#\mathrm{Sha}$ are all outside this paper.

3. **EXACT regime.** Archimedean: this is about the complex analytic $L$-function $L(E,s)$ and its continuation/functional equation, not a $p$-adic $L$-function. Rank regime: it provides a universal input valid for all curves regardless of rank (rank 0, 1, and $\geq 2$ alike), but the input is only parity-level. It does NOT close any rank. In particular it does not advance the open rank $\geq 2$ regime beyond pinning a parity.

4. **Detector 1 (PARITY-ONLY): TRIGGERED, by design.** This is the textbook trigger for Detector 1 in [`controls.py`](../../../experiments/_shared/controls.py): the entire BSD-relevant output is "the root number gives the parity of the analytic rank." The paper itself makes no full-rank claim, so it does not overstate. The detector models the misuse: anyone who tried to read a full rank off this paper's output would be using only the sign $w$, and `parity_detector(claims_full_rank=True, uses_only_root_number=True)` returns a verdict beginning "PARITY-ONLY: the root number gives rank mod 2 only." The paper is correctly classified as a modularity-level (parity-level) input, not a rank-equality input. This is not a defect of the paper; it is the precise statement of its strength and its ceiling for BSD.

5. **Detector 2 (SHA-FINITENESS ASSUMED): NOT triggered.** The paper never uses or asserts finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$. It makes no claim about $\mathrm{Sha}$ at all. `sha_finiteness_flag(E, analytic_rank, method_assumes_finite=False)` returns "Sha finiteness not used." for any curve, regardless of rank. Clean.

6. **Detector 3 (FUNCTION-FIELD MIRAGE): NOT triggered.** The result is genuinely over $\mathbb{Q}$ (over the number field, not a function field), and uses no geometric Frobenius shortcut. The deep work is precisely the number-field, mixed-characteristic local theory at the prime 3 (potentially Barsotti-Tate deformations, Breuil modules over $\mathbb{Z}_3$-integers). `function_field_mirage(method_uses_geometric_frobenius=False, method_needs_base_curve=False)` returns "OK: method does not silently import the function-field geometry." Clean.

7. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This paper is symmetric across the control pair: it gives the same parity-level input on a rank-0, rank-1, or rank-2 curve. It does NOT do "something genuinely new in the open regime" in the BSD sense, because it does not produce points or bound $\mathrm{Sha}$. It is a prerequisite that makes the analytic side well-defined everywhere, which is what lets Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin even be stated and run in rank $\leq 1$ ([`gross_zagier_kolyvagin.md`](gross_zagier_kolyvagin.md)). It is the floor, not the frontier.

8. **What it feeds.** It feeds the analytic-rank substrate used throughout the experiments: the smoothed approximate functional equation that evaluates $L(E,s)$ at $s = 1$ (see [`experiments/PLAN.md`](../../../experiments/PLAN.md), the $L$-function / analytic-rank thread) is only legitimate because modularity guarantees the continuation and functional equation this paper certifies for every curve. For the research directions it is upstream of all three: Direction 01 (the rank-2 object) and Direction 02 (the higher-rank Euler system) must construct rank-$\geq 2$ data that this paper does NOT provide; Direction 03 (p-adic to archimedean) relies on the archimedean $L$-function being defined, which is exactly this paper's deliverable. The honest reading: BCDT removes the last "is the left-hand side even defined?" caveat from BSD and leaves the entire difficulty concentrated in the rank, not the parity.
