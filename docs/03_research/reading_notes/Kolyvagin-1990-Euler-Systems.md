# Reading note: V. A. Kolyvagin, "Euler systems," in The Grothendieck Festschrift Vol. II, Progress in Mathematics 87, Birkhauser (1990), 435-483

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the paper that closes the analytic rank $\leq 1$ case of the rank part of BSD and proves $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ finite there. Gross-Zagier supplies one non-torsion Heegner point $y_K \in E(K)$ exactly when $L'(E/K,1) \neq 0$. Kolyvagin promotes that single point into an Euler system: a norm-compatible family of Heegner points $y_n \in E(K_n)$ over ring class fields, whose Kolyvagin-derivative cohomology classes annihilate the $p$-Selmer group. The output is a bound on $\operatorname{Sel}_p$ and $\operatorname{Sha}_p$ tight enough to force $\operatorname{rank} E(K) = 1$ and $\#\operatorname{Sha}(E/K) < \infty$ when $y_K$ is non-torsion (and $\operatorname{rank} 0$, finite $\operatorname{Sha}$, when $y_K$ is torsion and $L(E/K,1) \neq 0$).

## One-line takeaway

Starting from ONE non-torsion Heegner point, Kolyvagin builds a norm-compatible Euler system of Heegner points over ring class fields, applies group-ring derivative operators to manufacture Galois-cohomology classes $c(n) \in H^1(K, E_p)$, shows these classes are unramified away from $n$ (hence Selmer-like), and runs a Chebotarev-plus-Tate-local-duality recursion in which the classes for varying $n$ annihilate each other's contributions to the dual Selmer group. The result is that $\operatorname{Sel}_p(E/K)$ is bounded by the divisibility of $y_K$, giving $\operatorname{Sel}_p$ cyclic generated by the image of $y_K$, hence $\operatorname{rank} E(K) = 1$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)_p$ trivial, for almost all $p$. This is a theorem ONLY in analytic rank $\leq 1$, and the restriction is intrinsic: the system has exactly one degree of freedom because it is built from one point.

## Technical content (section by section)

Notation throughout: $E/\mathbb{Q}$ a (modular) elliptic curve of conductor $N$; $p$ an odd prime; $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})$ an imaginary quadratic field with $D \neq -1,-3$ satisfying the Heegner hypothesis (every prime $\ell \mid N$ splits in $K$); $E_p = E[p]$ the $p$-torsion as a $G_K = \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)$-module; $K_n$ the ring class field of conductor $n$, with $K_1 = H$ the Hilbert class field; $G_n = \operatorname{Gal}(K_n/K_1)$, $\mathcal{G}_n = \operatorname{Gal}(K_n/K)$; $\tau$ complex conjugation. Heegner points $y_n \in E(K_n)$ are the images under the modular parameterization $\Phi: X_0(N) \to E$ of CM points of conductor $n$; $y_K = \operatorname{Tr}_{K_1/K}\, y_1 \in E(K)$.

**The main theorem (expository thesis sec. 4.1).** *If $y_K$ has infinite order in $E(K)$, then (1) $E(K)$ has rank $1$, and (2) $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite.* This is the statement Kolyvagin proves. Note both conclusions are conditional on $y_K$ being non-torsion, which by Gross-Zagier is equivalent to $L'(E/K,1) \neq 0$, i.e. analytic rank $1$ over $K$. The clean working version proved in the expository text fixes one odd prime $p$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E_p)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})$ (a big-image hypothesis, true for all but finitely many $p$ when $E$ has no CM, by Serre) and with $p \nmid [y_K]$ in $E(K)$, and reduces everything to:

$$\textbf{Sel}_p(E/K) \text{ is cyclic, generated by } \delta(y_K).$$

The reduction uses the descent exact sequence
$$0 \longrightarrow E(K)/pE(K) \xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} \operatorname{Sel}_p(E/K) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Sha}(E/K)_p \longrightarrow 0.$$
If $\operatorname{Sel}_p$ is cyclic and $\delta(y_K) \neq 0$ generates it, then $E(K)/pE(K)$ has dimension $1$ (rank $1$, since the GL$_2$-image kills $p$-torsion) and the cokernel $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)_p$ is trivial. Doing this for almost all $p$ gives finite $\operatorname{Sha}$.

**Galois action and the Frobenius congruence (thesis sec. 4.2).** Restrict to Heegner conductors $n$ that are squarefree and prime to $N D p$, with each prime factor $\ell$ inert in $K$. Two facts power everything.
- *(Unramified torsion field.)* $K(E_p)/K$ is unramified outside primes dividing $Np$, via good reduction and the injection $E_p \hookrightarrow \tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\lambda)$.
- *(Kolyvagin primes.)* One selects primes $\ell$ with $\operatorname{Frob}(\ell) = \operatorname{Frob}(\infty) = [\tau]$ in $\operatorname{Gal}(K(E_p)/\mathbb{Q})$. Chebotarev gives infinitely many. Such $\ell$ are inert in $K$ and satisfy the congruence
$$a_\ell \equiv \ell + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod p,$$
so that $p \mid \#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ and BOTH eigenspaces $\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\lambda)_p^{\pm} \cong \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ are nontrivial, with $\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\lambda)_p \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^2$. This is the engine: at a Kolyvagin prime the local reduction sees full $p$-torsion, split by the $\pm$ eigenspaces of $\tau$/Frobenius. The group $G_\ell = \operatorname{Gal}(K_n/K_{n/\ell})$ is cyclic of order $\ell + 1$.

**The Euler system axioms (thesis sec. 4.3, the heart).** For $n = \ell \cdot m$ with $\ell \nmid m$ a prime inert in $K$, the Heegner points satisfy:
1. **Norm relation:** $\operatorname{Tr}_{K_n/K_m}\, y_n \;=\; a_\ell \cdot y_m$ in $E(K_m)$.
2. **Congruence (reduction) relation:** each prime $\lambda_n$ of $K_n$ above $\ell$ lies over a unique prime $\lambda_m$ of $K_m$ (totally ramified, $\lambda_m = (\lambda_n)^{\ell+1}$), with residue field $\mathbb{F}_{\ell^2}$, and
$$y_n \equiv \operatorname{Frob}(\lambda_m)\, y_m \pmod{\lambda_n}, \qquad \text{equivalently} \quad \operatorname{red}_{\lambda_n}(y_n) = \operatorname{Frob}(\lambda_m)\,\operatorname{red}_{\lambda_m}(y_m).$$
The norm relation comes from the Hecke relation $T_\ell f_E = a_\ell f_E$ pushed through $\Phi$ (the divisor identity $\Phi(\ell\tau) + \sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\Phi((\tau+k)/\ell) = a_\ell \Phi(\tau)$). The congruence relation comes from the Eichler-Shimura relation $T_\ell = \operatorname{Fr}_\ell + \widehat{\operatorname{Fr}}_\ell$ on $Y_0(N)/\mathbb{F}_\ell$ together with total ramification. These two relations ARE the Euler-system axioms in this setting: norm-compatibility downward, and a Frobenius-controlled congruence linking the $\ell$-layer to its reduction.

**The derivative operator and the classes $c(n)$ (thesis sec. 4.4).** For each prime $\ell \mid n$ fix a generator $\sigma_\ell$ of the cyclic $G_\ell$. The augmentation ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[G_\ell]$ is principal, generated by $\sigma_\ell - 1$. Define $\operatorname{Tr}_\ell = \sum_{\sigma \in G_\ell}\sigma$ and the **Kolyvagin derivative** $D_\ell \in \mathbb{Z}[G_\ell]$ as a solution of
$$(\sigma_\ell - 1)\,D_\ell \;=\; \ell + 1 - \operatorname{Tr}_\ell,$$
Kolyvagin's explicit choice being $D_\ell = \sum_{i=1}^{\ell} i\,\sigma_\ell^i$ (telescoping identity $(\sigma_\ell-1)D_\ell = (\ell+1) - \operatorname{Tr}_\ell$). Set $D_n = \prod_{\ell \mid n} D_\ell \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{G}_n]$. The point $D_n y_n$ is the derived Heegner point. Key facts:
- $D_n y_n$ gives a class $[D_n y_n] \in E(K_n)/pE(K_n)$ that is $G_n$-fixed: one checks $(\sigma_\ell - 1)D_n y_n \in pE(K_n)$ using the norm relation $\operatorname{Tr}_\ell y_n = a_\ell y_m$ together with $a_\ell \equiv 0 \pmod p$ and $\ell + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod p$ at Kolyvagin primes.
- Averaging over coset representatives $S$ of $G_n$ in $\mathcal{G}_n$ defines $P_n := \sum_{\sigma \in S}\sigma(D_n y_n) \in E(K_n)$, whose class $[P_n] \in (E(K_n)/pE(K_n))^{\mathcal{G}_n}$ is now $\mathcal{G}_n$-fixed. Note $P_1 = y_K$.
- The big-image hypothesis forces $E$ to have NO $p$-torsion rational over $K_n$ ($E_p(K_n) = 0$, because $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/p)$ is non-solvable for $p \geq 5$ while $\operatorname{Gal}(K_n/\mathbb{Q})$ is dihedral hence solvable). Therefore restriction is an isomorphism $H^1(K, E_p) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^1(K_n, E_p)^{\mathcal{G}_n}$ (Hochschild-Serre, vanishing inflation/cokernel).

**Definition of the cohomology class (thesis sec. 4.4).** Using the descent diagram over $K$ and $K_n$, define $c(n) \in H^1(K, E_p)$ as the UNIQUE class with
$$\operatorname{res}\, c(n) = \delta_n(P_n) \in H^1(K_n, E_p)^{\mathcal{G}_n}.$$
Let $d(n)$ be the image of $c(n)$ in $H^1(K, E)_p$ (the "$E$-coefficient" reduction). Then $\operatorname{res}\, d(n) = 0$ globally, so $d(n)$ inflates from a class $\bar d(n) \in H^1(\mathcal{G}_n, E)_p$. Triviality criteria:
- $c(n) = 0 \iff P_n \in pE(K_n)$.
- $d(n) = 0 \iff P_n \in pE(K_n) + E(K)$.
- $c(1) = 0 \iff y_K$ is $p$-divisible in $E(K)$; $d(1)$ is always globally trivial.
McCallum's explicit cocycle: $c(n)$ is represented by $f(\sigma) = \sigma(\tfrac1p P_n) - \tfrac1p P_n - \tfrac{(\sigma-1)P_n}{p}$, and $\bar d(n)$ by $\bar f(\sigma) = -\tfrac{(\sigma-1)P_n}{p}$.

**The eigenspace sign (thesis sec. 4.5).** Let $\epsilon = \pm 1$ be the eigenvalue of the Atkin-Lehner $w_N$ on the newform $f$ (so the sign of the functional equation of $L(E/\mathbb{Q},s)$ is $-\epsilon$). Complex conjugation acts on $y_n$ by $y_n^\tau = \epsilon\, y_n^{\sigma'} + (\text{torsion})$ for some $\sigma' \in \mathcal{G}_n$ (using $x_n^\tau = w_N x_n^{\sigma'}$ and Manin-Drinfeld for the cusps). Setting $\epsilon_n := \epsilon \cdot (-1)^{f_n}$ where $f_n$ is the number of prime divisors of $n$, the class $c(n)$ and the point class $[P_n]$ lie in the $\epsilon_n$-eigenspace for $\tau$:
$$\tau P_n \equiv \epsilon_n\, P_n \pmod{pE(K_n)}, \qquad c(n) \in H^1(K, E_p)^{\epsilon_n}.$$
This sign is what alternates as one adds prime factors to $n$, and it is the mechanism that lets the classes for $n$ and $n\ell$ probe OPPOSITE eigenspaces of the (self-dual under Tate pairing) local cohomology, which is exactly what makes the annihilation recursion work.

**Local triviality, i.e. $c(n)$ is "almost Selmer" (thesis sec. 4.6).** Recall $\operatorname{Sel}_p \subseteq H^1(K,E_p)$ cuts out classes locally in the image of $E(K_\nu)/p$ at every place $\nu$, and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)_p = \ker(H^1(K,E)_p \to \prod_\nu H^1(K_\nu, E)_p)$. The class $c(n)$ is genuinely Selmer at every place EXCEPT possibly the primes dividing $n$:
- At $\nu = \infty$: $H^1(\mathbb{C}, E) = 0$, so $d(n)_\infty = 0$. (This is where the *imaginary* quadratic field matters: $K_\infty = \mathbb{C}$.)
- At finite $\nu \nmid n$: $\bar d(n)$ is inflated from the unramified-at-$\nu$ extension $K_n/K$, so $d(n)_\nu$ lands in $H^1(K_\nu^{\mathrm{ur}}/K_\nu, E)$, which vanishes at good-reduction $\nu \nmid N$; the bad primes $\nu \mid N$ are handled by a Neron-model analysis (referenced to Gross's Heegner-points paper). So $d(n)_\nu = 0$ for $\nu \nmid n$.
- At $\nu = \lambda \mid \ell \mid n$ (the Kolyvagin primes, the ONLY place $c(n)$ can fail to be Selmer): the local class $d(n)_\lambda \in H^1(K_\lambda, E)_p$ is computed via the finite/singular decomposition. It equals zero iff the derived point reduces into $pE$ locally; in general its "residue" is governed by the $Q_n$ computation
$$Q_n := \frac{(\sigma_\ell - 1)P_n}{p}, \qquad Q_n \equiv \frac{(\ell+1)\operatorname{Frob}(\lambda_m) - a_\ell}{p}\, P_m \pmod{\lambda_n}.$$
On the $\epsilon_m$-eigenspace $\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\lambda)^{\pm}$, $\operatorname{Frob}(\lambda_m)$ acts as $\pm 1$, so $(\ell+1)\operatorname{Frob}(\lambda_m) - a_\ell$ becomes multiplication by $\ell+1-a_\ell = \#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\lambda)^+$ or by $\ell+1+a_\ell = \#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_\lambda)^-$. The upshot, which is the structural identity of the whole method: **the local singular residue of $c(n)$ at $\ell$ equals (up to unit) the local finite part = reduction of $P_m$, i.e. the value of the PREVIOUS class $c(m)$.** The ramified local condition at $\ell$ for $c(n)$ is dual to the unramified local condition at $\ell$ for $c(m)$.

**Tate local duality and the Selmer bound (Kolyvagin's endgame; thesis sec. 4.7-4.10).** *Written from authoritative knowledge; not read from the PDF.* At each Kolyvagin prime $\ell$, the local cohomology $H^1(K_\lambda, E_p)$ is a $2$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-space, self-dual under the Tate local pairing $\langle\,,\,\rangle_\lambda$, and splits as (finite/unramified) $\oplus$ (singular/ramified), the two summands being EXACT annihilators of each other. Write $\operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}$ and $\operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{s}}$ for the two projections. The local computation above says:
- $\operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}(c(n)) = $ reduction of $P_n$ (a finite/unramified class), nonzero iff $P_n \not\equiv 0$;
- $\operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{s}}(c(n\ell)) = $ (unit) $\cdot \operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}(c(n))$ (the singular part of the bigger class equals the finite part of the smaller one).

Now run the global reciprocity (sum-of-local-invariants $= 0$) of the Tate-Poitou pairing against any class $s$ in the $p$-Selmer group. Because $s$ is Selmer, $s$ is unramified (finite) at $\ell$, so the pairing $\langle s, c(n\ell)\rangle$ collapses to the single ramified place $\ell$:
$$0 = \sum_{\nu}\langle s_\nu, c(n\ell)_\nu\rangle = \langle s_\lambda, c(n\ell)_\lambda\rangle = \langle \operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}(s),\, \operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{s}}(c(n\ell))\rangle = (\text{unit})\cdot\langle \operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}(s),\, \operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}(c(n))\rangle.$$
Choosing, by Chebotarev, a Kolyvagin prime $\ell$ at which $\operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}(c(n))$ is a prescribed nonzero vector lets one read off $\operatorname{loc}_\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}(s)$, i.e. test $s$ against the reduction of the derived point. Running this for a finite set of primes $\ell$ (the global duality / Chebotarev existence of primes realizing any element of the relevant Galois module) shows that any $s$ in the part of $\operatorname{Sel}_p$ orthogonal to the system must be zero, EXCEPT for the contribution of $c(1) = c(y_K)$ itself. The induction on the number of prime factors of $n$, alternating eigenspaces via $\epsilon_n$, gives the bound
$$\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} \operatorname{Sel}_p(E/K) \;\leq\; 1 + 2\,\operatorname{ord}_p\big(\text{index of } \mathbb{Z}y_K \text{ in } E(K)\big),$$
and in the clean case $p \nmid [y_K]$ it collapses to $\operatorname{Sel}_p(E/K)$ cyclic, generated by $\delta(y_K)$. Feeding this back through the descent sequence gives $\operatorname{rank} E(K) = 1$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)_p = 0$. Over varying $p$ this yields finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$. Descent from $K$ to $\mathbb{Q}$ uses the eigenspace sign: with a suitable choice of $K$ (nonvanishing of the twisted $L$-value, Bump-Friedberg-Hoffstein / Murty-Murty / Waldspurger) the $\mathbb{Q}$-rank is recovered, giving analytic rank $0 \Rightarrow \operatorname{rank} 0$ and analytic rank $1 \Rightarrow \operatorname{rank} 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, with finite $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ in both.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN vs the GAP.** PROVEN: for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ modular with $y_K$ non-torsion, $\operatorname{rank} E(K) = 1$ and $\operatorname{Sha}(E/K)$ finite; combined with Gross-Zagier and the nonvanishing results, BSD's rank statement and $\operatorname{Sha}$-finiteness for analytic rank $0$ and $1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. The GAP: the method produces a system with exactly ONE generator ($P_1 = y_K$), so it can certify rank $\leq 1$ and never exhibit two independent points. Nothing here touches analytic rank $\geq 2$, where even the finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ remains OPEN.

2. **EXACT regime.** Analytic rank $0$ and $1$ (equivalently $y_K$ torsion / non-torsion). Archimedean in ORIGIN: the Heegner points come from the complex-analytic modular parameterization and the height formula is archimedean (Gross-Zagier). The Selmer-bounding machinery is mod-$p$ Galois cohomology, but it is driven by a point manufactured archimedean-analytically, not by a $p$-adic $L$-function. Contrast `iwasawa_skinner_urban.md`, which is the $p$-adic lift.

3. **Three detectors.**
   - *Parity-only:* **PASSES (does NOT trigger).** This is not a root-number argument. It produces the actual rank ($=1$) and the actual triviality of $\operatorname{Sha}_p$, not merely the rank mod $2$. It delivers genuine BSD content in its regime, the strongest thing in the proven zone.
   - *Sha-finiteness-assumed:* **PASSES, and is the SOURCE of the theorem.** Kolyvagin does not assume $\operatorname{Sha}$ finite; he PROVES it. But the detector's lesson lives here: finiteness is a conclusion ONLY because the system starts from one non-torsion point (rank $1$). The bound $\dim \operatorname{Sel}_p \leq 1 + 2\operatorname{ord}_p[\,\cdots]$ is finite precisely because there is a single seed. Any method that wants finite $\operatorname{Sha}$ in rank $\geq 2$ cannot borrow this conclusion; it must build a system with $\geq 2$ seeds.
   - *Function-field-mirage:* **PASSES (clean number-field argument).** The inputs are genuinely arithmetic over a number field: Chebotarev over $K(E_p)/\mathbb{Q}$, Tate local duality at finite places of a number field, the imaginary-quadratic condition $K_\infty = \mathbb{C}$ killing $H^1(\infty)$, and the Eichler-Shimura relation on $X_0(N)$. There is no geometric Frobenius shortcut; the method does not "work verbatim" over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ by importing global Frobenius. This is one of the few constructions in the corpus that is honestly number-field-native.

4. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This note IS the proven half of the control pair. It defines the wall precisely: the construction has a single degree of freedom ($D_n y_n$ derives from the one tower of Heegner points seeded by $y_1$). The Euler-system axioms (norm relation $\operatorname{Tr}_\ell y_n = a_\ell y_m$, congruence relation) are statements about that one family. A rank-$2$ analog would need a second, independent norm-compatible family whose derived classes pin down a second Selmer dimension, and no such family is known to exist over $\mathbb{Q}$.

5. **Feeds.** Research Direction 01 (`01_rank_two_object.md`): this note fixes the exact target an alternative must beat: replace $y_K$ by an object whose derived classes bound $\operatorname{Sel}_p$ by $2$ from below and above (a $2\times 2$ nondegenerate analog of the single-class duality recursion). Research Direction 02 (`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`): the precise axioms above (norm-compatibility + Frobenius congruence + eigenspace sign $\epsilon_n$) are the structure a "higher-rank Euler system" must reproduce; this note is the specification document for that search, and flags that the obstruction is the single-seed nature, not the cohomological formalism. Research Direction 03 (`03_padic_archimedean.md`): contrast partner for the Kato / Iwasawa $p$-adic Euler system, clarifying that THIS system is archimedean-seeded and self-dual-at-$\ell$, whereas the $p$-adic main conjecture replaces the seed with a $p$-adic $L$-function. Atlas row: Euler systems, proven regime, all three detectors PASS, structural ceiling = one point.
