# Reading note: D. Ulmer, "Elliptic curves with large rank over function fields," Annals of Mathematics 155 (2002), 295-315

> **Role in the BSD program.** Ulmer constructs explicit, NON-isotrivial elliptic curves over $K = \mathbb{F}_q(t)$ whose Mordell-Weil groups have ARBITRARILY large rank, and for these curves he PROVES the full Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture (rank equality, not merely parity, not merely a bound). The proof routes BSD through the Tate conjecture for the associated elliptic surface $\mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{P}^1$, which is known because $\mathcal{E}$ is dominated by a Fermat surface, and then reads off the rank as the order of the pole of the zeta function at $s=1$, computed via Gauss and Jacobi sums. This is the starkest possible function-field-mirage warning in the corpus: the exact statement that is OPEN over $\mathbb{Q}$ (full BSD in arbitrary rank, hence rank $\geq 2$) is here a THEOREM for all ranks, and the entire reason it is a theorem is the geometric Frobenius / Tate-conjecture input that the number-field case structurally lacks.

## One-line takeaway

For $p$ prime and $d = p^n + 1$, the explicit curve $E: y^2 + xy = x^3 - t^d$ over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ is non-isotrivial ($j(E) \notin \mathbb{F}_q$), satisfies the full Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, and has $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{F}_q(t)) \geq (p^n - 1)/2n$, which is unbounded as $n \to \infty$ (Theorem 1.5, p. 296). The proof never produces a point: it constructs the minimal elliptic surface $\mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ with generic fiber $E$, dominates $\mathcal{E}$ by a Fermat surface $F_d$ so that the Tate conjecture (known for $F_d$ via Shioda-Katsura) carries over to $\mathcal{E}$, and over an elliptic surface the Tate conjecture is EQUIVALENT to BSD for $E$ (Tate). The rank then equals the order of the pole of $\zeta(\mathcal{E}, s)$ at $s=1$ (Shioda-Tate plus the equality of the Tate inequality), and that pole order is computed exactly by counting Jacobi sums equal to $q$ on a set of characters. The whole argument runs on geometric Frobenius acting on $H^2$ of a surface over a finite field, which is precisely the structure the number-field case does not have.

## Technical content (section by section)

The logical spine: (1) write down $E$ and its reduction data; (2) build the minimal regular elliptic surface $\mathcal{E}$ and relate $\operatorname{rank} E(K)$ to $\operatorname{rank} NS(\mathcal{E})$ via Shioda-Tate; (3) dominate $\mathcal{E}$ by a Fermat surface to import the Tate conjecture; (4) over an elliptic surface, Tate $\Leftrightarrow$ BSD, so BSD holds and $\operatorname{rank} E(K) = -\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\zeta$; (5) compute that pole order exactly with Gauss/Jacobi sums.

**Section 1 (Introduction, pp. 295-297). The theorem and its place.** Ulmer states the open landscape honestly (p. 296): over $\mathbb{Q}$ the largest KNOWN rank at writing is 24 (Martin-McMillen) and the largest PROVEN analytic rank is 3 (Gross-Zagier), and the existence of unbounded rank over $\mathbb{Q}$ is open. Over $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$, Shafarevich-Tate (1967) had already produced unbounded rank, but their curves are ISOTRIVIAL ($j \in \mathbb{F}_p$), which has no number-field analog, so they give weak evidence for $\mathbb{Q}$. Ulmer's curves are non-isotrivial, which is the point.

> **Theorem 1.5 (p. 296).** Let $p$ be a prime, $\mathbb{F}_p$ the field of $p$ elements, $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ the rational function field. Let $E/K = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$ be defined by
> $$y^2 + xy = x^3 - t^d, \qquad d = p^n + 1, \ n \geq 1.$$
> Then $j(E) \notin \mathbb{F}_p$, the conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer holds for $E$ over $K$, and $\operatorname{rank} E(K) \geq (p^n - 1)/2n$.

Remarks 1.6 (p. 296) record the refinements: the EXACT rank is in Theorem 9.2; the curve makes sense over any $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ with $q$ a power of $p$ and BSD holds there too; the rank stabilizes already at the constant field $\mathbb{F}_{p^{2n}}$, with $\operatorname{Rank} E(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2n}}(t)) = \operatorname{Rank} E(\mathbb{F}_p(t)) = p^n$ when $6 \nmid d$ and $p^n - 2$ when $6 \mid d$ (remark 2, p. 296); the same Weierstrass equation over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ has rank BOUNDED independently of $d$, even over $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ itself, not just after passing to a constant field (remark 4, p. 296). Crucially, Ulmer states (1.9, p. 297): "the proof does not explicitly produce any points." The high rank is detected, not exhibited.

**Section 2 (Invariants of $E$, pp. 297-298).** For $E_d: y^2 + xy = x^3 - t^d$ over $k(t)$, $p \nmid d$: discriminant $\Delta = t^d(1 - 2^4 3^3 t^d)$, $j(E) = 1/\Delta$ (p. 298). Reduction: split multiplicative $I_d$ at $t=0$; multiplicative $I_1$ at the zeros of $(1 - 2^4 3^3 t^d)$; and a $d \bmod 6$-dependent type at $t = \infty$ tabulated via Tate's algorithm. These local data feed the Shioda-Tate count.

**Section 3 (Construction of $\mathcal{E}$, pp. 298-300).** Ulmer builds the unique regular, proper, flat, relatively minimal elliptic surface $\pi: \mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ over $k$ with generic fiber $E$, by gluing two affine Weierstrass models and resolving singularities (blowing up $\lfloor d/2 \rfloor$ times over $t=0$). The key arithmetic-geometry bridge is the **Shioda-Tate formula** (Tate, Shioda; p. 299):
$$\operatorname{Rank} E(K) = \operatorname{Rank} NS(\mathcal{E}) - 2 - \sum_v (n_v - 1),$$
where $NS(\mathcal{E})$ is the Neron-Severi group, the sum runs over closed points $v$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, and $n_v$ is the number of irreducible components of the fiber at $v$. There is an exact sequence $0 \to L \to NS(\mathcal{E}) \to E(K) \to 0$ where $L$ is generated by the zero-section and fiber components (p. 300). This reduces computing $\operatorname{rank} E(K)$ to computing $\operatorname{rank} NS(\mathcal{E})$.

**Section 4 (Fermat surfaces, pp. 300-301).** Let $F_d \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be the Fermat surface $x_0^d + x_1^d + x_2^d + x_3^d = 0$. Ulmer defines explicit dominant rational maps $F_d \dashrightarrow W_d \dashrightarrow \mathcal{E}_d$ and a finite quotient $F_d/\Gamma$ (for an explicit subgroup $\Gamma$ of the automorphism group $G \subset \operatorname{Aut}(F_d)$, p. 301) so that $\mathcal{E}_d$ is birational to $F_d/\Gamma$. The map idea is due to Shioda (1986), who related similar elliptic curves $E_n$ over $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ (with $p \equiv 3 \bmod 4$) to Fermat surfaces (1.8, p. 297).

**Section 5 (Analysis of $\varphi_d$, pp. 301-302).** A geometric lemma (Lemma 5.2, p. 302: "a rational map from a normal variety is defined at a point iff its composition with a finite map is") and its Corollary 5.3 let Ulmer pin down explicit open sets on which the birational map $\varphi_d: W_d \dashrightarrow F_d/\Gamma$ is a biregular isomorphism. This bookkeeping is needed so that the zeta functions of $\mathcal{E}_d$ and $F_d/\Gamma$ differ only by explicit, computable factors.

**Section 6 (Tate conjecture, BSD, and rank via zeta, pp. 304-306). The conceptual core.** For a surface $X/\mathbb{F}_q$ there is the chain of inequalities (6.2.1, p. 305):
$$\operatorname{Rank} NS(X) \ \leq\ \dim_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} H^2(X)^{Fr = q} \ \leq\ -\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\zeta(X, s).$$
The **Tate conjecture** "(T) for $X$" asserts these are all EQUALITIES (Tate 1965). Then (Section 6.3, p. 305):

> When $X = \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{C}$ is an elliptic surface with a section, the Tate conjecture for $\mathcal{E}$ is EQUIVALENT to the Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for $E/K$, and (T) implies $\operatorname{Rank} E(K) = \operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E/K, s)$. Moreover Tate (1966) proved that when (T) holds, the refined leading-coefficient BSD formula is true up to a power of $p$, and Milne (1975) proved the full refined formula.

This is the load-bearing equivalence. It means: to prove BSD for $E$, prove the Tate conjecture for $\mathcal{E}$. And the Tate conjecture is known for $\mathcal{E}$ because:

> **Proposition 6.4 (p. 305).** The Tate conjecture holds for $\mathcal{E}$; equivalently, BSD holds for $E$.

The proof (p. 306): the Tate conjecture is known for Fermat varieties because a Fermat variety is dominated by a product of curves (Shioda-Katsura 1979), (T) is trivial for curves, (T) propagates under dominant rational maps and to products, and $\mathcal{E}$ is dominated by $F_d$. Combining the Shioda-Tate formula with the now-established equality $\operatorname{Rank} NS(\mathcal{E}) = -\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\zeta(\mathcal{E}, s)$ gives (Proposition 6.4, part 2, p. 306) a formula
$$\operatorname{Rank} E(\mathbb{F}_q(t)) = -\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}\zeta(F_d/\Gamma, s) - 1 + \varepsilon,$$
with an explicit correction $\varepsilon \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ that is the sum of a $\{0,1\}$ term ($1$ exactly when $2 \mid d$ and $4 \mid q-1$) and a $\{0,1,2\}$ term ($1$ when $3 \mid d$ and $3 \nmid q-1$, $2$ when $3 \mid d$ and $3 \mid q-1$). Per remark 6.5 (p. 306) this $\varepsilon$ accounts for the points on $E$ with $x=0$ or $y=0$. Note (6.6, p. 306): the local component numbers $n_v$ cancel out of the final rank, though they are recorded for height computations.

**Sections 7-8 (Zeta of the Fermat surface via Gauss and Jacobi sums, pp. 307-312).** This is the explicit-computation engine. Using the cohomological description $\zeta(X,s) = \prod_i P_i(X, q^{-s})^{(-1)^{i+1}}$ with $P_i(X,T) = \det(1 - Fr^* T \mid H^i(X))$ (p. 307), only $P_2$ can contribute a pole at $s=1$ (Deligne purity: eigenvalues of $Fr$ on $H^i$ have absolute value $q^{i/2}$, so only $H^2$ gives the weight matching a pole at $s=1$). The pole order of $\zeta(F_d/\Gamma, s)$ at $s=1$ equals the number of Frobenius eigenvalues on $H^2(F_d)^\Gamma$ that equal $q$.

> **Proposition 7.6 (Shioda, p. 309).** Decomposing $H^2(F_d) = \bigoplus_a H^2(F_d)(a)$ over characters $a \in \hat{G}$, each piece $H^2(F_d)(a)$ is $0$ or $1$-dimensional, it is nonzero iff a Jacobi sum $J(a) \neq 0$, and $(Fr^{u(a)})^*$ acts on it by multiplication by $J(a)$. Consequently (Corollary 7.7, p. 310) $P_2(F_d/\Gamma, T) = \prod_i (1 - J(a_i) T^{u(a_i)})$ over orbit representatives.

So a Frobenius eigenvalue equals $q$ exactly when the corresponding Jacobi sum $J(a) = q$. Ulmer then proves the decisive arithmetic input in the "supersingular" case $d \mid p^n + 1$:

> **Proposition 8.1 (p. 310).** If some power of $p$ is congruent to $-1$ modulo $d$, then for all $a \in \hat{G}'$, $J(a) = q^{u(a)}$.

The proof (pp. 310-312) reduces Gauss sums via the Hasse-Davenport relation and a Shafarevich-Tate-style evaluation of Gauss sums that are trivial on a subfield (Lemma 8.3, p. 310: $g(\chi, \psi) = -\chi(x) p^f$ for trivial-on-$\mathbb{F}_{p^f}$ characters). The condition $d \mid p^n + 1$ is exactly the "supersingular" condition that forces every relevant Jacobi sum to hit $q^{u(a)}$, hence to contribute a pole, hence to contribute to the rank. This is where the unbounded rank comes from: as $d = p^n+1$ grows, the number of characters with $J(a) = q^{u(a)}$ grows.

**Section 9 (Exact rank, pp. 312-313).** Assembling Proposition 6.4, Corollary 7.7, and Proposition 8.1:

> **Theorem 9.2 (p. 312).** Let $p$ be prime, $n \geq 1$, $d \mid p^n + 1$, $q$ a power of $p$, and $E/\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ defined by $y^2 + xy = x^3 - t^d$. Then $j(E) \notin \mathbb{F}_q$, BSD holds for $E$, and
> $$\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{F}_q(t)) = \sum_{\substack{e \mid d \\ e \nmid 6}} \frac{\phi(e)}{o_e(q)} \ +\ \begin{cases} 0 & 2\nmid d \text{ or } 4 \nmid q-1 \\ 1 & 2\mid d \text{ and } 4 \mid q-1 \end{cases} \ +\ \begin{cases} 0 & 3 \nmid d \\ 1 & 3\mid d,\ 3\nmid q-1 \\ 2 & 3 \mid d,\ 3 \mid q-1 \end{cases}$$
> where $\phi$ is Euler's function and $o_e(q)$ is the multiplicative order of $q$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z})^\times$.

Specializing $d = p^n+1$, $q = p$ gives $o_e(p) \leq 2n$ for all $e \mid d$, hence $\operatorname{rank} \geq (p^n-1)/2n$ (Theorem 1.5). Taking $q = p^{2n}$ gives $o_e(q) = 1$ for all $e \mid d$, so the rank reaches its maximum $d - 1 = p^n$ (for $6 \nmid d$); the rank STABILIZES over the larger constant field (p. 313). This explicit dependence of the rank on the size of the constant field $\mathbb{F}_q$ is the mirage signature: the rank jumps when you enlarge $\mathbb{F}_q$, a move with no analog over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Section 10 (Rank bounds and the number-field conjecture, pp. 313-314).** Ulmer compares his ranks to the upper bounds. The GEOMETRIC bound $\operatorname{Rank} E(K) \leq \operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L \leq 4g - 4 + \deg(\mathfrak{n})$ is insensitive to $\mathbb{F}_q$ (p. 313). Brumer's ARITHMETIC bound (1992) is sensitive to the constant field:
$$\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E/K, s) \leq \frac{4g - 4 + \deg(\mathfrak{n})}{2 \log_q \deg(\mathfrak{n})} + C \frac{\deg(\mathfrak{n})}{(\log_q \deg(\mathfrak{n}))^2},$$
the function-field analog of Mestre's GRH bound $\operatorname{ord} L(E/\mathbb{Q}, s) = O(\log N / \log\log N)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Ulmer's curves show the main term of Brumer's bound is SHARP (p. 313). Motivated by this, he conjectures the analogous sharpness over number fields:

> **Conjecture 10.5 (p. 314).** With $r_K(N)$ the max rank over elliptic curves over a number field $K$ of conductor norm $N$,
> $$\limsup_N \frac{r_K(N)}{\log N / \log\log N} > 0.$$

This is the note's payload for the number-field world: Ulmer is explicit that the unbounded-rank phenomenon he proves over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ is a THEOREM there but only a CONJECTURE over $\mathbb{Q}$ (the analogous abelian-variety statement was proven unconditionally by Kowalski-Michel, remark 10.7, p. 314, but NOT for elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$).

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN here.** For the explicit family $y^2 + xy = x^3 - t^d$ over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ with $d \mid p^n + 1$: (a) non-isotriviality, (b) the FULL Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture (rank equality $\operatorname{rank} E(K) = \operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E/K,s)$, AND, via Tate 1966 / Milne 1975, the refined leading-coefficient formula, not just parity, not just a bound), (c) an exact closed-form rank (Theorem 9.2), (d) unbounded rank as $n \to \infty$. These are genuine THEOREMS over the function field, in ALL ranks including rank $\geq 2$.

2. **What is the GAP.** The theorems are over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$, and the number-field analog of every step fails or is open: (i) BSD over $\mathbb{Q}$ is proven only for analytic rank $\leq 1$; here it is proven for unbounded rank, but only because (ii) the Tate conjecture for the surface is available, and (iii) the Tate conjecture is available because the surface lives over a FINITE field, where geometric Frobenius acts on $H^2$ and the Fermat-surface domination plus Deligne purity pin the pole order. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ there is no surface over $\mathbb{F}_q$, no geometric Frobenius, no Tate conjecture of this kind, and no Jacobi-sum computation. Ulmer himself flags (1.9) that the proof produces NO points, so even here it is a rank/Selmer-rank result detected cohomologically, not a Mordell-Weil generator construction. The number-field unbounded-rank statement remains Conjecture 10.5.

3. **EXACT regime.** Function field $K = \mathbb{F}_q(t)$ (genus $0$ base), characteristic $p$. Rank UNBOUNDED (the whole point), so this is decisively a rank-$\geq 2$ result, in fact rank $\to \infty$. The relevant $L$-function is the geometric $L$-function $L(E/K, s)$ whose order of vanishing equals the pole order of $\zeta(\mathcal{E}, s)$ at $s=1$; the "archimedean vs $p$-adic" axis does not apply in the number-field sense, because the analytic object is a polynomial in $q^{-s}$ (a zeta/$L$-function of a variety over $\mathbb{F}_q$), and the leading-term BSD is proven via Tate/Milne, not via a complex or $p$-adic period in the number-field sense. So: function-field regime, unbounded rank, full BSD proven.

4. **Detector scores.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES (over-delivers).** This is the opposite of a parity result. Ulmer computes the EXACT rank in closed form (Theorem 9.2) and proves the full rank equality plus the refined formula. There is no collapse to rank mod $2$. (The lesson for $\mathbb{Q}$ is therefore NOT "parity is the ceiling" but "the missing ingredient is the geometric Tate input.")
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES.** Over function fields, BSD is known to be equivalent to finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ (Tate, Artin-Tate, Milne). Ulmer does not ASSUME finite $\operatorname{Sha}$ as a hypothesis: he PROVES the Tate conjecture for $\mathcal{E}$ outright (via Fermat domination), which yields BSD and hence the finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ for these curves as a consequence. So finiteness is an output, not an input. (Contrast: a method that merely invoked "finite $\operatorname{Sha}$ over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$" as a black box would trip this detector; Ulmer earns it.)
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): TRIGGERS, maximally, BY DESIGN.** This is the canonical mirage exhibit. The full BSD in unbounded rank is delivered by: a surface over a FINITE field, geometric Frobenius acting on $H^2$, the Tate conjecture (a finite-field / motivic-weight statement proven through Fermat surfaces and Deligne purity), and an explicit Gauss/Jacobi-sum count. Every one of these is a structure the number-field case lacks. The rank even DEPENDS on the size of $\mathbb{F}_q$ (it jumps when the constant field is enlarged, Theorem 9.2 / remark 1.6), a phenomenon with no number-field analog. This note is the reference point for the mirage detector: "proves BSD in all ranks" here means "imports geometric Frobenius," exactly the move forbidden over $\mathbb{Q}$.

5. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This source sits ENTIRELY in the rank-$\geq 2$ (indeed rank $\to \infty$) regime that is open over $\mathbb{Q}$, and it shows that the wall is NOT intrinsic to high rank: in the geometric world the rank-$\geq 2$ object is fully constructed, fully understood, and BSD-certified. The wall over $\mathbb{Q}$ is therefore not "rank $\geq 2$ is impossible" but "rank $\geq 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ lacks the geometric Frobenius / Tate-conjecture engine that makes it tractable over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$." The control pair is satisfied in the cleanest way: a method that does something genuinely new in the open regime, but only by using the finite-field structure, which is precisely why it does not transfer.

6. **Which direction / experiment it feeds.** Primary: Research Direction 01 ([`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md)). Ulmer is the existence proof that a fully-understood rank-$\geq 2$ (indeed unbounded-rank) BSD object EXISTS, over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$, and the note pins down the single transferable-versus-non-transferable line: the Tate conjecture via geometric Frobenius. Any number-field attack on the rank-$\geq 2$ object must supply a replacement for that engine, because the Heegner/Euler-system machinery cannot reach rank $\geq 2$ and Ulmer's machinery cannot leave the finite field. Secondary: Direction 02 ([`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md)) as a CONTRAST, Ulmer needs NO Euler system at all: the Tate conjecture replaces the descent wholesale, which is itself a data point about what a higher-rank method could look like if a non-Euler-system mechanism (some cohomological cycle-class input) could be found over $\mathbb{Q}$. Feeds the atlas function-field row and is the worked example behind the function-field-mirage detector in [`controls.py`](../../../experiments/_shared/controls.py).
