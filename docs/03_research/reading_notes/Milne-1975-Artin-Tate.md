# Reading note: J. S. Milne, "On a conjecture of Artin and Tate," Annals of Mathematics 102 (1975), 517-533.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the sharpest form of the function-field template. For a projective smooth surface $X$ over a finite field $k = \mathbb{F}_q$, Tate had conjectured (T) that $\rho(X)$ (the rank of the Neron-Severi group) equals the multiplicity of $q$ as a reciprocal root of $P_2(X,t)$, and Artin-Tate had refined this to (A-T): the Brauer group $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ is finite and the leading coefficient of $P_2(X,t)$ at $t = q^{-1}$ is the explicit Brauer-Neron-Severi quotient. Milne proves: **(T) implies (A-T) in full, including the $p$-part, for $p \neq 2$.** Via the dictionary between a minimal elliptic surface $\mathcal{E} \to C$ and an elliptic curve $E/K$, $K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$ (Section 8), this is exactly **BSD over the function field $K$, proven under finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich (equivalently Brauer) group.** The engine is pure geometry: etale, crystalline, flat, and Witt-vector cohomology of the surface, Poincare duality making $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ self-dual, and the geometric Frobenius $\Phi$ acting on these cohomology groups. That Frobenius is precisely the object the number-field case lacks.

## One-line takeaway

Over a function field $K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$ the full Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer formula (rank equality *and* the leading-coefficient identity with $\#\mathrm{Sha}$, regulator, and torsion) is a **theorem**, equivalent to Tate's conjecture (T) and provable in full including the $p$-part, the entire weight of the proof carried by Poincare duality and the geometric Frobenius on the etale / crystalline / flat / Witt-vector cohomology of the associated elliptic surface; none of that geometry exists over $\mathbb{Q}$, so the result is a template and a mirror, not a transferable proof, and it remains conditional on the finiteness of $\mathrm{Br}(X) = \mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Introduction (pp. 517-519): the two conjectures.** $X$ is a projective smooth surface over $k = \mathbb{F}_q$, $q = p^s$. The zeta function factors as
$$Z(X,t) = \frac{P_1(X,t)\,P_3(X,t)}{(1-t)\,P_2(X,t)\,(1-q^2 t)},$$
with $P_r(X,t) \in \mathbb{Z}[t]$ whose reciprocal roots have absolute value $q^{r/2}$. Let $\rho(X)$ be the rank of $\mathrm{NS}(X)$.

- **(T) (Tate):** $\rho(X)$ equals the multiplicity of $q$ as a reciprocal root of $P_2(X,t)$.
- **(A-T) (Artin-Tate):** $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ is finite, and
$$P_2(X, q^{-s}) \;\sim\; \frac{(-1)^{\rho(X)-1}\,[\mathrm{Br}(X)]\,\det(D_i \cdot D_j)}{q^{\alpha(X)}\,(\mathrm{NS}(X):B)^2}\,(1 - q^{1-s})^{\rho(X)} \quad \text{as } s \to 1,$$
where $[S]$ is the order of a finite set $S$, $\alpha(X) = \chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X) - 1 + \dim(\mathrm{PicVar}(X))$, the $D_i$ are independent elements of $\mathrm{NS}(X)$, $B = \sum \mathbb{Z} D_i$, and $\det(D_i \cdot D_j)$ is the discriminant of the intersection pairing on $B$.

Tate [20] had shown that if (T) holds then $\mathrm{Br}(X)(\text{non-}p)$ (the prime-to-$p$ part) is finite with the order predicted by (A-T), using etale cohomology. **The paper's contribution is the $p$-part.** Three difficulties are flagged (p. 517): $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ and $\mathrm{NS}(X)$ live in flat cohomology while $P_2(t)$ lives in crystalline cohomology; neither is as well understood as etale cohomology; and "the $p$-part of (A-T) seems genuinely to have more content than the prime-to-$p$ part" (p. 518). **Main theorem of the paper: (T) implies the whole of (A-T) when $p \neq 2$** (p. 518).

**Section 1 (pp. 519-521): four cohomology theories in characteristic $p$.** The proof needs all four, glued together:
- **(1.1) $l$-adic etale ($l \neq p$):** $P_r(X,t) = \det(1 - \Phi t \mid H^r(\bar X, \mathbb{Q}_l))$, independent of $l$. For a surface, Poincare duality rewrites the $H^2$ factor as $\det(1 - \sigma t \mid H^2(\bar X, T_l\mu)\otimes\mathbb{Q}) = \prod (1 - \tfrac{a_{2,i}}{q} t)$.
- **(1.2) Crystalline ($H^r(X/W)$):** the slopes of $H^r$ recover the $p$-adic valuations $v(a_{r,i})$ of the Frobenius eigenvalues.
- **(1.3) Witt-vector ($H^r(\bar X, W)$):** captures the slope-$<1$ part; the $p$-torsion $H^r(\bar X, W)_t$ has finite length, giving the $p$-power contributions.
- **(1.4) Flat ($H^r(\bar X, V_{p^n}\mu)$, $p > d$):** provides the $\mu_{p^n}$-coefficient cohomology dual to the above; for $r = 2 = d$ it yields the $p$-analog $\det(1 - \sigma t \mid H^2(\bar X, V_{p^n}\mu)) = \prod_{v(a_{2,i})=1}(1 - \tfrac{a_{2,i}}{q} t)$.

**Section 2 (pp. 521-523): auto-duality of the Brauer group.** This is the geometric heart.
- **Theorem 2.1 (Poincare duality for a surface, $p \neq 2$):** the groups $H^r(X, \mu_n)$ are finite; there is a canonical non-degenerate pairing $\langle\,,\,\rangle: H^r(X,\mu_n) \times H^{5-r}(X,\mu_n) \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, compatible across $n$ and with intersection products of divisors, satisfying $\langle y, \delta_n x\rangle + (-1)^r\langle x, \delta_n y\rangle = 0$. (The hypothesis $p \neq 2$ enters only here, only when $p^3 \mid n$, Remark 2.2.)
- **Theorem 2.4:** there is a skew-symmetric pairing $\mathrm{Br}(X) \times \mathrm{Br}(X) \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ whose kernel is exactly the divisible part of $\mathrm{Br}(X)$. Consequence (Remark 2.5): if $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ is finite, its order is a square or twice a square. This auto-duality is the surface-level shadow of the Cassels-Tate pairing on $\mathrm{Sha}$ and is what makes the leading coefficient come out as $[\mathrm{Br}(X)]$ times a discriminant.

**Sections 3-5 (pp. 523-528): the diagrams and the master quasi-isomorphism.** From the Kummer sequence $0 \to \mu_n \to \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{G}_m \to 0$ and the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence for $\bar X / X$, Milne builds commutative diagrams (3.1)-(3.3) relating $\mathrm{NS}(X)$, $\mathrm{Pic}$, and $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ to $H^2(\bar X, T_l\mu)^\Gamma$ and its torsion. **Theorem 4.1** lists the equivalent forms of Tate's conjecture: (T) for $X/k$ $\iff$ $\mathrm{Br}(X)(l)$ finite for some (equivalently all) $l$ (including $l = p$) $\iff$ $\rho(X) = \mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_l} H^2(\bar X, T_l\mu)^\Gamma$ $\iff$ the cycle map $\bar h: \mathrm{NS}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_l \to H^2(\bar X, T_l\mu)^\Gamma$ is bijective $\iff$ $\rho(X) = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(H^2(X/W)_K(1)^F)$. Section 5 sets up the key diagram of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$-module maps $e, f, j, \bar h, g^*$ and computes each one's **quasi-isomorphism constant** $z(\cdot)$ (the surnatural-number ratio $[\ker]/[\mathrm{coker}]$):
$$z(e) = \frac{(-1)^{\rho-1}(\mathrm{NS}(X):B)^2}{\det(D_i \cdot D_j)\,[\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathrm{tors}}]}, \quad z(\bar h) = 1, \quad z(j) = \frac{q^{-s}}{[\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathrm{tors}}]}, \quad z(g^*) = [\mathrm{Br}(X)].$$
The map $g$ (and $\bar h$) is a quasi-isomorphism for a prime $l$ **if and only if $\mathrm{Br}(X)(l)$ is finite**. So assuming (T) makes every map a quasi-isomorphism, and the relation $z(e) = z(\bar h)\,z(f)\,z(j)\,z(g^*)$ holds.

**Section 6 (pp. 528-529): the Artin-Tate theorem.**
- **Theorem 6.1:** $X$ a smooth projective surface over $\mathbb{F}_q$, $p \neq 2$. **If (T) holds for $X/k$, then (A-T) holds.** The proof: under (T) all maps in the Section-5 diagram are quasi-isomorphisms, so multiplying the $z(\cdot)$ forces $[\mathrm{Br}(X)]$ to be a rational number, i.e., $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ finite; combining the constants yields the leading-coefficient formula, reduced to the purely cohomological identity
$$q^{\alpha(X)} = \Big|\prod_{v(a_{2,i})\neq 1}\big(1 - \tfrac{a_{2,i}}{q}\big)\Big|_p^{-1}\,q^s, \quad\text{equivalently}\quad \alpha(X) = \sum_{v(a_{2,i}) < 1}\big(1 - v(a_{2,i})\big) + s.$$

**Section 7 (pp. 529-531): the $\alpha(X)$ / Euler-characteristic identity.** Proposition 7.1 expresses $\chi(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ via the slopes of crystalline cohomology and lengths of Witt-vector modules; Lemma 7.2 computes Euler characteristics of Dieudonne ($W[F,V]$) modules in terms of slopes. Corollary 7.3 and Remark 7.4 deliver the slope identity above, completing the proof of the $p$-part. **This is the step that genuinely needs crystalline + Witt-vector cohomology**, and is the entire reason the $p$-part is harder than the prime-to-$p$ part.

**Section 8 (pp. 531-533): re-interpretation as BSD over a function field.** Via Tate's dictionary [20], a minimal elliptic surface $\mathcal{E} \to C$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ corresponds to an elliptic curve $E/K$, $K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$, with $\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{E}) \cong \mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$, $\mathrm{NS}$ encoding $E(K)$ and the fibral divisors, and $P_2(X,t)$ encoding $L(E,s)$.
- **Theorem 8.1 (stated "only for the simplest case," characteristic $p \neq 2$):** for $E$ an elliptic curve over a function field $K$ in one variable with finite constant field of characteristic $p \neq 2$, the following are equivalent:
  - **(a)** $L(E,s)$ has a zero of order $r$ at $s = 1$, where $r = \mathrm{rank}\,E(K)$ (this is **weak BSD over $K$**);
  - **(b)** for some prime $l$, the $l$-primary component of $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite;
  - **(c)** $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite, and the leading coefficient is the strong-BSD identity
  $$L^*(E,s) \;\sim\; \frac{[\mathrm{Sha}]\,\det\langle a_i', a_j\rangle}{[E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}]^2}\,(s-1)^r \quad \text{as } s \to 1,$$
  with $\det\langle a_i', a_j\rangle$ the regulator of the height pairing. The proof "follows from (4.1) and (6.1)" plus Tate's equivalence of conjectures (B) and (C) for elliptic surfaces.

Remark 8.2 gives concrete examples where (8.1) holds unconditionally (e.g. $Y^2 = X(X-1)(X-T)$ over $k(T)$ with $k$ of characteristic $p \neq 2, 3$, whose minimal model is a rational surface, so (T) is trivial), notes Artin-Swinnerton-Dyer [3] proved (8.1b) for elliptic K3 pencils, and observes (8.2c) that any elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field of char $p$ and transcendence degree $\geq 1$ descends to *some* function field $K$ for which (8.1b), hence the full BSD package over that particular $K$, holds. **The decisive structural point:** over $K$ the finiteness condition (b) is the *only* hypothesis, and it is a theorem for the surface classes where Tate's conjecture (T) is itself known (rational surfaces, abelian surfaces, products of two curves, supersingular elliptic K3 surfaces, anything birational or finite over such, p. 519). It is **not** known for all surfaces: (T) over a finite field is itself an open conjecture in general, so even over $K$ the finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$ is unconditional only in those families, not universally.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN vs the GAP.** PROVEN: over a function field $K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$, **full BSD (rank equality and the strong leading-term formula, including the $p$-part) is equivalent to finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$** (= finiteness of $\mathrm{Br}$ of the elliptic surface), and that finiteness is in turn equivalent to Tate's conjecture (T) for the surface (Theorems 6.1 and 8.1). Crucially the equivalence holds at **every rank $r$**, with no rank-$1$ ceiling: there is no Heegner-point bottleneck here because the proof is not point-construction, it is cohomological bookkeeping closed by Poincare duality. The GAP relative to $\mathbb{Q}$: there is no base curve $C$, no surface, no geometric Frobenius $\Phi$, no crystalline/flat-cohomology slope identity. The entire mechanism is unavailable over $\mathbb{Q}$. The theorem is also still *conditional* on finite $\mathrm{Sha}$/$\mathrm{Br}$; (T) is known for many surface classes but not all.

2. **EXACT regime.** Function field $K = \mathbb{F}_q(C)$, geometric (not number-field). Both the **archimedean-analog** leading term (the $L$-function is a rational function of $q^{-s}$, so "$L^*(E,s)$ as $s \to 1$" is an honest polynomial leading coefficient) and the **$p$-part** are delivered, the $p$-part being the paper's novel content via crystalline + Witt-vector + flat cohomology. The result is **rank-uniform**: it certifies the rank equality and the full formula for *all* $r \geq 0$, including $r \geq 2$, but *only over $K$*. There is no analytic-rank restriction because the obstruction that creates one over $\mathbb{Q}$ (constructing $\geq 2$ independent points) is dissolved by the surface geometry.

3. **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): TRIGGERS, canonically and in its sharpest form.** This is the reference instance of the mirage. The proof "works" precisely because it uses the geometric Frobenius endomorphism $\Phi: \bar X \to \bar X$ and Poincare duality on a *surface*, objects with no number-field counterpart. Any proposed proof over $\mathbb{Q}$ that, when transcribed to $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$, reproduces this argument has silently imported the surface and its Frobenius. The note's value is to make that import explicit and nameable: it is the etale/crystalline/flat-cohomology Tate-conjecture input of Sections 1-7. A number-field method must do *new* work exactly where Milne uses geometry it does not have.

4. **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): TRIGGERS, but transparently and as a clearly labeled hypothesis.** The entire theorem is an *equivalence*, with finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K) = \mathrm{Br}(X)$ as the explicit pivot (statement (b) of Theorem 8.1, statement (b)/(b') of Theorem 4.1). Milne never claims finiteness in general; he proves it *follows from* (T) and *implies* the full formula. So this source models the honest use of the Sha hypothesis: it is named, it is the load-bearing assumption, and it is discharged only for surface classes where (T) is a theorem. It does **not** supply unconditional finiteness over $\mathbb{Q}$ or even unconditionally over all $K$.

5. **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES.** This is the opposite of a parity method. It delivers the exact order of vanishing $r$ (equal to the rank) and the exact leading coefficient with $[\mathrm{Sha}]$, regulator, and torsion. Parity ($r \bmod 2$, root number) plays no role; the result is the full strong-form identity, conditional only on finite $\mathrm{Sha}$.

6. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This source is the cleanest illustration of *why* the rank-boundary wall is a number-field phenomenon, not an intrinsic feature of BSD. Over $K$ there is **no wall**: rank $\geq 2$ is handled by the same cohomological mechanism as rank $0$ and $1$, because the proof never constructs a single rational point and so is never capped at "one point." The thing that produces the rank-$\leq 1$ ceiling over $\mathbb{Q}$ (the Heegner / Euler-system engine being a one-degree-of-freedom object) is simply absent here; the surface's $\mathrm{NS}$ and the geometric Frobenius see all of $E(K)$ at once. This is the precise content of "the wall is a coordinate, not a verdict": move to the function field and the wall vanishes, which localizes the difficulty to the missing geometric Frobenius over $\mathbb{Q}$.

7. **Which research direction / experiment it feeds.** Primarily Research Direction 02, [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md): Milne 1975 is the existence proof that *something* closes BSD at all ranks in a geometric setting, so a higher-rank number-field method should be measured against what the geometric Frobenius does here (it bounds $\mathrm{Sha}$ at every rank via auto-duality, Theorem 2.4). For Direction 01, [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md), the lesson is sharper: the "rank-two object" sought over $\mathbb{Q}$ is, over $K$, simply $\mathrm{NS}(X) / (\text{fibral} + \text{torsion})$ together with the cycle map $\bar h$; the open problem is to manufacture a number-field analog of $\bar h$ being bijective without a surface. For Direction 03, [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md), the paper supplies a clean $p$-adic-vs-archimedean dichotomy template: the prime-to-$p$ part is etale and "easy" (Tate already had it), the $p$-part needs crystalline/flat cohomology and is the genuinely harder half, a structural hint about where $p$-adic methods over $\mathbb{Q}$ must concentrate. On the experimental side it anchors the function-field control in [`experiments/_shared/controls.py`](../../../experiments/_shared/controls.py): the smoke-test's Detector 3 should encode "does this method survive deletion of the geometric Frobenius / surface?" and Milne 1975 is the precise specification of that Frobenius.
