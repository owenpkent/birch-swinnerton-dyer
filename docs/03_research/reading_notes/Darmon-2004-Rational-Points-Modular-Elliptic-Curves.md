## One-line takeaway

The Heegner-point method is fully proven exactly where it produces one point: Gross-Zagier (Theorem 3.20) ties the Neron-Tate height of a single Heegner point $P_K \in E(K)$ to $L'(E/K,1)$, and Kolyvagin's Euler-system descent (Chapter 10) turns that one point into the statement "if $P_K$ is non-torsion then $\mathrm{rank}\,E(K)=1$ and $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite," giving BSD for analytic rank $\leq 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Theorem 1.14). Everything past rank 1 in the book is either a conjecture (Stark-Heegner points over real quadratic fields, Conjecture 9.13; the existence of nontrivial Heegner systems when $\mathrm{sign}(E,K)=-1$, Conjecture 3.19) or a structurally one-point object, so the monograph is at once the clearest statement of the proven regime and the clearest map of why that regime stops at rank 1.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Chapter 1: elliptic curves, Selmer/Sha, and the BSD statements.** The descent setup is pinned precisely. Definition 1.6 gives the $n$-Selmer group $\mathrm{Sel}_n(E/F) \subset H^1(F,E_n)$ (classes with $\partial_v = 0$ at all $v$) and the Shafarevich-Tate group $\mathrm{Sha}(E/F) \subset H^1(F,E)$ (classes locally trivial everywhere), fitting into the fundamental exact sequence (1.13)
$$0 \to E(F)/nE(F) \xrightarrow{\delta} \mathrm{Sel}_n(E/F) \to \mathrm{Sha}(E/F)_n \to 0.$$
Proposition 1.7 (finiteness of $\mathrm{Sel}_n$) gives weak Mordell-Weil; its global step rests on Hermite-Minkowski (finitely many bounded-ramification extensions). Conjecture 1.9 is the open finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}(E/F)$. The $L$-function is the Euler product (1.19) $L(E,s)=\prod_{p\nmid N}(1-a_pp^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}\prod_{p\mid N}(1-a_pp^{-s})^{-1}$, with bad $a_p\in\{0,+1,-1\}$ by reduction type. Conjecture 1.12 (Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer) is split into three increasing strengths: **BSD1** ($L(E,1)\neq 0 \iff \#E(\mathbb{Q})<\infty$), **BSD2** ($\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\mathrm{rank}\,E(\mathbb{Q})$, the Clay form), and **BSD3** the leading-coefficient formula $\lim_{s\to 1} L(E,s)/(s-1)^r = \#\mathrm{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})\,R_E\,(\prod_{p\mid N}c_p)\,c_\infty$ with $R_E=\det(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle)\,\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}^{-2}$. Theorem 1.13 records modularity (Wiles; Taylor-Wiles; CDT; BCDT): analytic continuation and the functional equation $\Lambda(E,s)=\pm\Lambda(E,2-s)$. **Theorem 1.14 (Gross-Zagier, Kolyvagin):** if $L(E,1)\neq 0$ then $r=0$; if $L(E,1)=0$ and $L'(E,1)\neq 0$ then $r=1$ (with an efficient method for $E(\mathbb{Q})$); in both cases $\mathrm{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite. The book states this as the exact frontier: "BSD2 and the Shafarevich-Tate conjecture are proved when $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\leq 1$."

**Chapter 1, function-field remark.** Darmon flags the analogy explicitly: over a function field $k(T)$, Mordell-Weil holds, ranks can be unbounded (Tate-Shafarevich; Ulmer), and Tate proved $\mathrm{rank}\,E(F)\leq \mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/F,s)$ with full BSD (BSD3) for $E/F$ **if $\mathrm{Sha}(E/F)$ is finite**. He calls the function-field evidence "probably the best evidence we dispose of at present." This is exactly the function-field-mirage warning: the function-field theorem is conditional on finite Sha and imports geometry the number-field case lacks.

**Chapter 3: Heegner points on $X_0(N)$ (the proven construction).** Heegner points $\Phi_N(\tau)$ are images under the modular parametrisation $\Phi_N:\mathcal{H}/\Gamma_0(N)\to E(\mathbb{C})$ of CM points $\tau\in CM(\mathcal{O})$. Proposition 3.8: $CM(\mathcal{O})\neq\varnothing$ iff every prime dividing $N$ splits in $K$, which forces **Hypothesis 3.9 (the Heegner hypothesis): all primes $\ell\mid N$ split in $K/\mathbb{Q}$**. The points $HP(n)\subset E(H_n)$ (ring class field of conductor $n$) satisfy the norm-compatibility of Proposition 3.10 (the Trace$_{H_{n\ell}/H_n}$ relations involving $a_\ell$, the analog of an Euler-system norm relation) and the reflection behaviour of Proposition 3.11. **Definition 3.12 (Heegner system):** a collection $P_n\in E(H_n)$, indexed by $n$ prime to $N$, satisfying those relations; it is non-trivial if some $P_n$ is non-torsion. **Theorem 3.13:** if $(E,K)$ satisfies the Heegner hypothesis, a non-trivial Heegner system exists (proof: CM points are dense in $\mathcal{H}$, so their images are infinite, and the torsion of $E(H_\infty)$ is finite by Lemma 3.14, an injection into $E(\mathbb{F}_{q^2})\oplus E(\mathbb{F}_{q'^2})$).

Section 3.6 recasts this against BSD: with $\mathrm{sign}(E,K)=\pm 1$ the global sign (Theorem 3.15, Theorem 3.17: $\mathrm{sign}(E,K)=(-1)^{\#S_{E,K}}$ counting archimedean and split-multiplicative places), $\mathrm{sign}(E,K)=-1$ forces $L(E/K,\chi,1)=0$ for all ring-class characters $\chi$, hence $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/H,s)\geq[H:K]$, and BSD predicts $\mathrm{rank}\,E(H)\geq[H:K]$. **The Gross-Zagier formula, Theorem 3.20 (Gross-Zagier, Zhang):** with $P_K=\mathrm{Trace}_{H_1/K}(P_1)\in E(K)$ and the twisted Heegner vectors $P_n^\chi=\sum_{\sigma}\bar\chi(\sigma)P_n^\sigma$,
$$\langle P_K,P_K\rangle \doteq L'(E/K,1), \qquad \langle P_n^\chi,P_n^\chi\rangle \doteq L'(E/K,\chi,1),$$
where $\doteq$ means up to an explicit nonzero factor. Consequence: $P_n^\chi\neq 0 \iff L'(E/K,\chi,1)\neq 0$. **Theorem 3.21 / 3.22 (Kolyvagin):** if $P_K$ is non-torsion then (1) $E(K)$ has rank one and $P_K$ generates a finite-index subgroup, and (2) $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite. Section 3.9 assembles 3.20 and Kolyvagin to recover Theorem 1.14 over $\mathbb{Q}$ (choosing an auxiliary imaginary quadratic $K$ via the nonvanishing twist results of Waldspurger / Bump-Friedberg-Hoffstein / Murty-Murty, all rank $\leq 1$).

**The structural ceiling (read carefully).** Everything in Chapter 3 produces, after descent to $\mathbb{Q}$, a single point $P_K$. A single point spans a subgroup of rank at most 1. Kolyvagin's conclusion is sharp: $P_K$ non-torsion gives rank exactly 1, $P_K$ torsion gives rank 0. The machine cannot exhibit two independent generators. The book makes the dual obstruction explicit at the start of Chapter 4: when $N=pq$ with $p,q$ inert in $K$, so $\mathrm{sign}(E,K)=-1$, the classical $\Phi_N$ produces only points whose traces to the relevant ring class fields are torsion ("the modular parametrisation $\Phi_N$ is inadequate to produce the non-trivial Heegner system whose existence is predicted by Conjecture 3.16"). That motivates Shimura-curve parametrisations (Chapters 4 to 6), but those still yield one-point objects.

**Conjecture 3.16 / Conjecture 3.19 (the first conjectural extension).** If $\mathrm{sign}(E,K)=-1$ then there is a non-trivial Heegner system attached to $(E,K)$. Proven for $K$ imaginary quadratic (Chapter 4 machinery). For $K$ real quadratic it is open: Darmon writes plainly that "one knows of no method for analytically constructing the class fields of real quadratic fields ... one must content oneself with a conjectural construction that works in certain special cases." This is the honest seam where the proven method ends.

**Chapter 9: Stark-Heegner points (the $p$-adic / real-quadratic conjectural extension).** Working on $\mathcal{H}_p\times\mathcal{H}$ with $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])$, Darmon builds a $p$-adic double multiplicative integral (Definition 9.7, eq. 9.25) and a 2-cocycle $\kappa_\tau\in Z^2(\Gamma,\mathbb{C}_p^\times)$. **Conjecture 9.10** (a strengthening tied to Mazur-Tate-Teitelbaum, later established for the relevant statement by Greenberg-Stevens [GS93]) makes $\kappa_\tau$ a coboundary mod $q^{\mathbb{Z}}$, yielding a homomorphism $\theta_\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma_\tau,\mathbb{C}_p^\times/q^{\mathbb{Z}})$. For $\tau$ in a **real quadratic** field $K$ in which $p$ is inert or ramified (the "modified Heegner hypotheses," roles of $p$ and $\infty$ interchanged, giving $\mathrm{sign}(E,K)=-1$), the stabiliser $\Gamma_\tau$ is infinite cyclic with generator $\gamma_\tau$, and
$$J_\tau=\theta_\tau(\gamma_\tau)\in\mathbb{C}_p^\times/q^{\mathbb{Z}}, \qquad P_\tau=\Phi_{\mathrm{Tate}}(J_\tau)\in E(\mathbb{C}_p)$$
defines the **Stark-Heegner point**, via Tate's $p$-adic uniformisation $\Phi_{\mathrm{Tate}}$. **Conjecture 9.13 (the rationality conjecture):** $P_\tau$ belongs to $E(H^+)$, where $H^+$ is the narrow ring class field of the real quadratic order $\mathcal{O}_\tau$, and $cP_\tau=w_\infty P_\tau$. This is the structural analog of a Heegner point over a real quadratic field. **It is a conjecture.** The book states it as such, supports it numerically ([DG01]), and points to [Da01] for the Shimura reciprocity law that would refine it.

**Chapter 10: Kolyvagin's theorem (the Euler-system descent in full).** Theorem 10.1: if $P_K$ has infinite order then $\mathrm{rank}\,E(K)=1$ and $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$ is finite. The proof works mod a "descent prime" $p$ (Theorem 10.2: if the image of $P_K$ in $E(K)/pE(K)$ is non-zero then $\mathrm{Sel}_p(E/K)$ is generated by $\delta(P_K)$, so rank one and $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)_p$ trivial). The engine is the Kolyvagin derivative classes: for a Kolyvagin prime $\ell$, with $D_\ell=\sum_{i=0}^{n_\ell-1} i\sigma_\ell^i$ the derivative operator, one forms $Q_\ell=\tilde D_\ell P_\ell$ from the Heegner point $P_\ell$ of conductor $\ell$, and (since $p$ divides $n_\ell$ and $a_\ell$ for Kolyvagin primes) defines $\bar\kappa(\ell)(\sigma)=(\sigma-1)Q_\ell/p$, a class $\kappa(\ell)\in H^1(K,E_p)$. **Proposition 10.21:** $\kappa(\ell)$ lies in the relaxed Selmer group $H^1_{(\ell)}(K,E_p)$, its residue $\partial_\ell(\kappa(\ell))\neq 0$ iff the image of $P_K$ in $E(K_\ell)/pE(K_\ell)$ is non-zero, and $\bar\kappa(\ell)$ lies in the $\varepsilon$-eigenspace with $\varepsilon=\mathrm{sign}(E,\mathbb{Q})$. Two-prime classes $\kappa(\ell_1\ell_2)$ (Proposition 10.22) feed the bound on the opposite eigenspace. The end of proof is two steps: Step 1, the $\partial_S^\varepsilon$ map is surjective, forcing $\mathrm{Sel}_p(E/K)^\varepsilon$ trivial; Step 2, $\mathrm{Sel}_p(E/K)^{-\varepsilon}$ has dimension exactly 1, generated by $\delta(P_K)$. Hence $\mathrm{Sel}_p(E/K)$ is one-dimensional, generated by the image of the single point. **The entire Euler system is built from, and bounded by, that one Heegner point.** Its bound "sees" only the first-order analytic datum $L'$; there is no second-order analog in the construction.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN vs the GAP.** Proven (Theorems 1.14, 3.20, 3.21/3.22, Chapter 10): full BSD2 and finiteness of Sha for $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\leq 1$, via one Heegner point plus its Euler-system descent. Gap: analytic rank $\geq 2$. The book contributes no theorem there. Its rank-$\geq 2$ content is conjectural (Stark-Heegner, Conjecture 9.13; Conjecture 3.19) or structurally one-point.

2. **EXACT regime.** Theorems 3.20 and Chapter 10 are **archimedean, analytic rank $\leq 1$** (Gross-Zagier height = $L'$; Kolyvagin descent gives rank $\leq 1$ and finite Sha). Chapter 9 is **$p$-adic and conjectural**: $P_\tau\in E(\mathbb{C}_p)$, rationality (Conjecture 9.13) and the underlying $\kappa_\tau$-splitting are not theorems. No statement in the book reaches archimedean rank $\geq 2$ unconditionally.

3. **Three detectors.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES in the proven regime.** Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin delivers the exact rank ($=1$) and an explicit generator, not merely $w=-1$. This is the gold standard the detector is calibrated against: a genuine rank certificate, not a parity statement. (Modularity, Theorem 1.13, gives only the sign; the book is careful to separate the two.)
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES in the proven regime, but note the conditional function-field remark.** Kolyvagin PROVES $\mathrm{Sha}(E/K)$ finite in rank $\leq 1$ (it does not assume it). The book's function-field BSD3 (Chapter 1 remark) is stated **conditionally on finite Sha** and is correctly flagged as such. Any attempt to reuse the function-field BSD3 over $\mathbb{Q}$ would trip Detector 2.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): the book itself raises the flag, and the conjectural extension survives the detector.** Darmon names the function-field analogy as the best available evidence (the verbatim quote: the function-field/number-field analogy "is probably the best evidence we dispose of at present" for BSD) while stating it imports finite Sha and unbounded ranks (Tate-Shafarevich [TS67], Ulmer [Ul]) that the number-field case lacks. The Stark-Heegner construction (Chapter 9) is the live attempt to manufacture, $p$-adically, a real-quadratic substitute for the geometric structure. It does not transfer verbatim to $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$: it is built from $\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])$, Tate $p$-adic uniformisation, and a real quadratic field, all number-field-specific data with no geometric Frobenius doing the work, so it does not trip the mirage detector. The reason it still does not close anything in rank $\geq 2$ is not a detector firing but the plain status of its payload: the rationality of $P_\tau$ is exactly Conjecture 9.13 and is open. A surveyor must record that the conjectural extension is not a function-field import, but is also not proven.

4. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This source is the cleanest statement of the control pair. Rank $\leq 1$: fully handled (one point suffices). Rank $\geq 2$: structurally out of reach because a single Heegner point spans rank $\leq 1$, and the Euler system inherits that ceiling (its bound degenerates to the rank-1 bound, exactly the Direction-02 falsifiability trigger). The one-point obstruction is intrinsic, not a soft gap.

5. **Which direction / experiment it feeds.**
   - **Direction 01 (specify the rank-2 object).** This note fixes precisely what a replacement for $P_K$ must beat: not one point but two with nonzero $2\times 2$ height-pairing determinant, and the independence must be forced by an order-$\geq 2$ analytic datum (a second-order Gross-Zagier formula), which Theorem 3.20 conspicuously lacks. It also supplies the cautionary input named in Direction 01: Stark-Heegner points are a candidate input but their rationality (Conjecture 9.13) is open, so they enter as "input, not solved."
   - **Direction 02 (rank-$\geq 2$ Euler system).** Chapter 10 is the canonical rank-1 Euler system to compare against. The norm relations (Proposition 3.10) and the derivative-class construction (eq. 10.10) are exactly the structures a rank-$\geq 2$ system would have to generalise with second-order norm relations. The note documents why the known system "sees" only $L'$.
   - **Direction 03 ($p$-adic to archimedean).** Chapter 9 ties Stark-Heegner / $\kappa_\tau$ to Mazur-Tate-Teitelbaum and Greenberg-Stevens, the same $p$-adic-to-archimedean seam Direction 03 targets. The Stark-Heegner point is a $p$-adic object ($E(\mathbb{C}_p)$) whose conjectural rationality would be a $p$-adic-to-global bridge.
