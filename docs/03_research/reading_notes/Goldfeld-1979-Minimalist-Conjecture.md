# Reading note: D. Goldfeld, "Conjectures on elliptic curves over quadratic fields," in Number Theory, Carbondale 1979 (M. B. Nathanson, ed.), Lecture Notes in Mathematics 751, Springer-Verlag, Berlin (1979), pp. 108-118.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the origin paper of the statistics-of-ranks architecture (architecture 5 in the atlas). It sets the *statistical target* that every later averaging result is measured against. Goldfeld conjectures that in the quadratic-twist family $\{E_d\}$ of a fixed $E/\mathbb{Q}$, the analytic rank is $0$ exactly half the time and $1$ exactly half the time, so the *average analytic rank is $1/2$* and the curves of analytic rank $\geq 2$ have *density $0$*. This is the conjecture Bhargava-Shankar later approach from above (average rank $\le 1.5$ unconditionally, via $2$-Selmer), and it is the precise sense in which the rank-$\le 1$ regime is "almost all" curves. It is a conjecture about *densities*, not a per-curve theorem, and it asserts nothing about the existence of points or the finiteness of Sha for any single curve.

## One-line takeaway

Goldfeld conjectured (1979) that for a fixed $E/\mathbb{Q}$, as $d$ ranges over fundamental discriminants ordered by $|d|$, the quadratic twists $E_d$ have average analytic rank exactly $1/2$, achieved by a clean $50/50$ split between analytic rank $0$ and analytic rank $1$ with the rank-$\geq 2$ twists forming a set of density $0$. This is a *conjecture about densities only*. Its lower half (average at least $1/2$) is already forced by the root number being equidistributed $\pm 1$, so the genuine conjectural content is the matching upper bound. Crucially, density $0$ does **not** make the rank-$\geq 2$ twists empty, so BSD remains open for exactly the infinitely many hard curves this conjecture pushes into a measure-zero corner.

## Technical content (section by section)

**The quadratic-twist family and the object being averaged.** Fix an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$, say $E: y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$. For a squarefree integer $d$ (equivalently a fundamental discriminant) the quadratic twist is
$$E_d: \; d\,y^2 = x^3 + ax + b, \qquad \text{equivalently} \qquad E_d: \; y^2 = x^3 + a d^2 x + b d^3,$$
the curve isomorphic to $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ but generally not over $\mathbb{Q}$. The associated $L$-function $L(E_d, s)$ is the twist of $L(E,s)$ by the quadratic character $\chi_d$, and its sign in the functional equation (the root number) is
$$w(E_d) = w(E)\,\chi_d(-N_E)\cdot(\text{ramified local factors}),$$
so as $d$ varies the root number $w(E_d)\in\{+1,-1\}$ flips according to a quadratic character and is, away from a thin set, equidistributed between $+1$ and $-1$. The *analytic rank* is $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E_d,s)$; by modularity (proven, BCDT 2001) the $L$-function has analytic continuation and a functional equation, so this order is well defined, and the functional-equation sign gives the theorem $w(E_d) = (-1)^{\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E_d,s)}$ relating the root number to the parity of the *analytic* rank.

**The conjecture (Goldfeld's minimalist / density conjecture).** Let $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E_d) = \mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E_d,s)$. Goldfeld conjectures the average over the twist family is the minimum compatible with the equidistributed sign:
$$\boxed{\;\lim_{X\to\infty} \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{0<|d|\le X,\ d\ \mathrm{sqfree}} r_{\mathrm{an}}(E_d)}{\displaystyle\#\{0<|d|\le X : d\ \mathrm{sqfree}\}} \;=\; \frac{1}{2}.\;}$$
The conjectured mechanism is the strongest possible refinement consistent with parity: among the $\sim 50\%$ of twists with $w(E_d) = +1$ the analytic rank is $0$ (not $2, 4, \dots$), and among the $\sim 50\%$ with $w(E_d) = -1$ the analytic rank is $1$ (not $3, 5, \dots$). Equivalently, writing $N_r(X)$ for the number of squarefree $|d|\le X$ with $r_{\mathrm{an}}(E_d) = r$,
$$\frac{N_0(X)}{\#\{d\}} \to \tfrac12, \qquad \frac{N_1(X)}{\#\{d\}} \to \tfrac12, \qquad \frac{N_r(X)}{\#\{d\}} \to 0 \ \ (r\ge 2).$$
This is why it is called the **minimalist conjecture**: ranks are as small as the sign permits, and "extra" vanishing (rank $\geq 2$ when the sign already forces rank $\ge 0$, or rank $\geq 3$ when the sign forces rank $\ge 1$) happens with density $0$.

**Density zero is not emptiness (the load-bearing subtlety).** The conjecture says the rank-$\geq 2$ twists are a *thin* set (density $0$ in the family), but it is fully consistent with there being *infinitely many* of them. Indeed, for many fixed $E$ it is a theorem that the twist family contains infinitely many curves of rank $\ge 2$ (constructions of Stewart-Top, Rubin-Silverberg, and others). Density $0$ governs the *proportion*, not the *count*. Goldfeld's conjecture relocates the hard curves to a measure-zero corner; it does not eliminate them, and it certifies nothing about any one of them.

**Algebraic vs analytic form.** Goldfeld phrased the conjecture for the analytic rank (order of vanishing of $L$). The *algebraic* (Mordell-Weil) version, $\lim \frac1X\sum_{|d|\le X}\mathrm{rank}\,E_d(\mathbb{Q}) = 1/2$, is equivalent to the analytic version *only granting BSD's rank equality on the family*. Since BSD is proven only in analytic rank $\le 1$ (Gross-Zagier + Kolyvagin), the two forms agree on the density-$1$ part precisely where they predict ranks $0$ and $1$, which is exactly the regime where rank equality is a theorem. So the conjecture is internally consistent with the proven regime: its $50/50$ prediction lands entirely inside the rank-$\le 1$ window where analytic and algebraic ranks are known to coincide. The discrepancy (if any) lives only in the conjectured density-$0$ tail, where BSD is open.

**Relation to root-number equidistribution and parity.** Half of the average, the "$\ge 0$ from $w=+1$ and $\ge 1$ from $w=-1$" floor, is essentially forced by the equidistribution of the root number. For the *analytic* rank this floor is a theorem, not a conjecture: by the functional equation (which exists by modularity, BCDT 2001) the analytic rank $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E_d,s)$ has the parity of $\tfrac{1-w(E_d)}{2}$, so $w(E_d)=-1$ forces analytic rank $\ge 1$. Equidistribution of $w(E_d)$ over the family then gives an average analytic rank of *at least* $1/2$. (The *parity conjecture* proper, that the Mordell-Weil rank shares this parity, is the separate statement needed only to transfer the floor to the algebraic rank; it is not invoked here.) The full content of Goldfeld's conjecture is the *upper* bound matching the floor: that the rank does not systematically exceed it, i.e. that higher vanishing is density $0$. The sign gives the lower bound for free; the minimalist conjecture is the assertion that the lower bound is also the truth on average.

**Later refinements (context, not in the 1979 paper).** Random-matrix heuristics (Katz-Sarnak symmetry-type philosophy; Conrey-Keating-Rubinstein-Snaith) refine the bare $50/50$ to predictions about the *secondary* term, e.g. the count of rank-$\ge 2$ twists growing like $X^{3/4}(\log X)^{b}$ for an explicit exponent, and the CKRS bias in the sign distribution. These sharpen Goldfeld's density-$0$ claim into a conjectural rate, but they do not change its logical status: still a conjecture, still about the family, still silent per curve.

## Project mapping

1. **Conjectured, not proven (do not upgrade).** The statement "average analytic rank $= 1/2$, with density-$0$ rank $\geq 2$" is a *conjecture*. It is open. What is *proven* near it is strictly weaker and from a different paper: Bhargava-Shankar give the unconditional upper bound (average rank $\le 1.5$ via average $\#S_2 = 3$), which is consistent with $1/2$ but does not reach it; the exact value $1/2$ and the $50/50$ split remain conjectural. Conditional improvements still fall short of the conjecture: GRH for the family's $L$-functions sharpens the average-rank *upper* bound (Heath-Brown $\le 2$, Young $\le 25/14 \approx 1.79$) but does not deliver the exact value $1/2$, nor BSD for almost all twists. No known hypothesis, conditional or otherwise, reaches the predicted average; every route to it remains open.

2. **The gap.** This is a *population/density* statement; it certifies nothing for any individual twist: not the rank, not the rank equality $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E_d,s) = \mathrm{rank}\,E_d(\mathbb{Q})$, not finiteness of Sha, not the leading-coefficient formula. Its *only* relation to per-curve BSD is sociological: it predicts that the curves where BSD is genuinely open (analytic rank $\ge 2$) are statistically rare. Rare is not handled. Direction [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md) must still produce the rank-$\geq 2$ object one curve at a time.

3. **Exact regime.** Archimedean, analytic-rank statement (order of vanishing of $L(E_d,s)$ at $s=1$), averaged over the quadratic-twist family ordered by $|d|$. Not $p$-adic; no Iwasawa or Euler-system content. The conjecture's predicted mass sits at analytic rank $0$ and $1$ (the *proven* BSD regime), with the *open* regime (analytic rank $\geq 2$) pushed to density $0$. So on the per-curve rank axis it straddles the wall: density $1$ lands in the proven side, the conjecture's whole subtlety is that the open side is nonempty but thin.

4. **Detector scorecard.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PARTIALLY TRIGGERS, by design, and this is the key honesty flag.** The *lower* half of the conjecture (average $\geq 1/2$) is essentially parity output: equidistribution of the root number $w(E_d) = (-1)^{r_{\mathrm{an}}(E_d)}$ forces the average rank to be at least $1/2$, and that part is modularity-level, not BSD-closing. The genuine conjectural content is precisely the part *parity cannot give*: that vanishing does not exceed the parity floor (no systematic rank $\ge 2$). A method that "confirms Goldfeld" by only reproducing the equidistributed sign has delivered the parity floor and nothing more. Flag: any claimed progress must add the *upper* bound (suppression of higher vanishing), or it is parity in disguise.
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES as stated (conjecture is about analytic rank), but its consumers do not.** Goldfeld's conjecture is stated for the analytic rank, so it does not itself assume $\#\mathrm{Sha}$ finite. The moment one passes to the *algebraic* (Mordell-Weil) form of the conjecture on the density-$0$ tail, one is in the open regime where Sha-finiteness is not known; that import lives in the consumer, not in the 1979 statement. The density-$1$ part (ranks $0$ and $1$) is exactly where Sha-finiteness *is* a theorem (Kolyvagin), so the conjecture's bulk prediction is Sha-clean.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): FLAGGED.** Goldfeld's conjecture has a function-field analog over $\mathbb{F}_q(t)$ that is *much* more tractable (geometric methods, monodromy, and in the large-$q$ / cohomological setting one has genuine theorems and explicit data; cf. the Experimental-Data-for-Goldfeld over function fields literature). The mirage is to mistake the function-field success for progress on $\mathbb{Q}$: the function-field case has a geometric Frobenius / monodromy group (Katz-Sarnak symmetry type) that the number-field case lacks. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ the conjecture is open. Any "proof of Goldfeld" that works verbatim over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ has imported that geometry and proves nothing over $\mathbb{Q}$.

5. **Control pair (rank $\le 1$ proven vs rank $\ge 2$ open).** This conjecture is the *quantitative shape* of the control pair. It predicts the proven arm (rank $\le 1$) has density $1$ in the twist family and the open arm (rank $\ge 2$) has density $0$. That is the strongest possible statement that the rank-$1$ ceiling is "almost everywhere" the true ceiling, while leaving the open arm strictly nonempty. The atlas's "one missing object" (a rank-$\ge 2$ construction or an unconditional Sha bound) is untouched: a density-$0$ set is still infinite and still requires the missing object. The conjecture is therefore a *compass*, not a closure: it says effort spent on rank $\ge 2$ is spent on a thin but unavoidable set.

6. **What it feeds.** Primarily the statistics architecture (atlas 5): it is the target of Bhargava-Shankar (approached from above) and the structural prediction behind Bhargava-Skinner-Zhang (positive proportion satisfy BSD, consistent with most twists being rank $0$ or $1$). For the proof program it is a *boundary marker*: it tells Direction [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md) that the rank-$\geq 2$ curves are statistically negligible yet still must be constructed individually, and it warns (via Detector 1) that reproducing the $1/2$ floor is parity, not BSD. Natural experiment hook (none currently in [`experiments/PLAN.md`](../../../experiments/PLAN.md)): compute root numbers and analytic ranks across a quadratic-twist family of a fixed small curve (e.g. 37a1, 389a1) and check the empirical rank histogram against the $50/50$ / density-$0$ prediction, parallel to the Sato-Tate thread; this would also exercise Detector 1 by separating the parity floor from the higher-vanishing suppression.
