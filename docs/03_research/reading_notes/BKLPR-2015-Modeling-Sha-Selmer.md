# Reading note: M. Bhargava, D. Kane, H. Lenstra, B. Poonen, E. Rains, "Modeling the distribution of ranks, Selmer groups, and Shafarevich-Tate groups of elliptic curves," Cambridge Journal of Mathematics 3 (2015), 275-321. arXiv:1304.3971.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the heuristic backbone of the statistics-of-Selmer architecture (architecture 5 in the atlas). Where Bhargava-Shankar PROVES an average ($\#\mathrm{Sel}_2 E = 3$ on average), this paper builds the random-algebraic MODEL that the proven averages are consistent with, and then derives from a single clean conjecture the entire wish list of the minimalist picture: $50\%$ rank $0$, $50\%$ rank $1$, $0\%$ rank $\geq 2$, finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ for $100\%$ of curves, the full conjectural distribution of $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e} E$, and Delaunay's Cohen-Lenstra-type law for $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$. The model is a random maximal-isotropic-intersection (equivalently, a random alternating matrix cokernel). It is the source from which the population predictions flow, but it PROVES nothing about any individual curve and, crucially, MODELS finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ rather than proving it.

## One-line takeaway

Equip $V = \mathbb{Z}_p^{2n}$ with the split hyperbolic quadratic form, choose two maximal isotropic direct summands $Z, W$ at random, and form the short exact sequence $0 \to R \to S \to T \to 0$ from $R = (Z\cap W)\otimes \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $S = (Z\otimes \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)\cap(W\otimes \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)$; as $n\to\infty$ this converges to a universal distribution $\mathcal{Q}$, and the single Conjecture 1.3 asserts that the arithmetic sequence $0\to E(k)\otimes \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}E \to \mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]\to 0$ has exactly this distribution as $E$ varies over a fixed global field $k$ ordered by height, from which $50/50$ rank $0$/$1$, density-zero rank $\geq 2$, $100\%$ finite $\mathrm{Sha}$, and Delaunay's $\mathrm{Sha}$ law all follow; everything past the matrix theorems is conjectural, so $\mathrm{Sha}$-finiteness here is a prediction of the model and not a theorem, and a method that consumes these distributions inherits exactly that unproven status.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Section 1.1: the arithmetic object being modeled (p. 1).** Fix a global field $k$ with set of nontrivial places $\Omega$. For $E/k$ and a prime $p$, the $p^\infty$-Selmer group $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}E := \varinjlim \mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}E$ and the $p$-primary part of $\mathrm{Sha} = \mathrm{Sha}(E) := \ker(\mathrm{H}^1(k,E)\to \prod_{v} \mathrm{H}^1(k_v,E))$ sit in the exact sequence of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules
$$0 \longrightarrow E(k)\otimes \frac{\mathbb{Q}_p}{\mathbb{Z}_p} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}E \longrightarrow \mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]\longrightarrow 0. \tag{$\mathrm{Seq}_E$}$$
**Question 1.1** asks: ordering $E$ by height, what is the probability that $\mathrm{Seq}_E$ is isomorphic to a given short exact sequence $\mathcal{S}$ of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules? Note $E(k)\otimes \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p \cong (\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)^r$ records the rank $r$, $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is CONJECTURALLY finite, and (since the first term is divisible) the sequence splits.

**Section 1.2: the random model (p. 2).** Equip $V := \mathbb{Z}_p^{2n}$ with $Q(x_1,\dots,x_n,y_1,\dots,y_n) = \sum_i x_i y_i$. A $\mathbb{Z}_p$-submodule $Z$ is isotropic if $Q|_Z = 0$; let $\mathrm{OGr}_V(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ be the set of maximal isotropic direct summands (each free of rank $n$). There is a natural probability measure under which, for every $e\geq 0$, the reduction $Z/p^e Z$ in $V/p^e V$ is uniform among all possibilities. Choose $Z, W \in \mathrm{OGr}_V(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ independently and define
$$R := (Z\cap W)\otimes \tfrac{\mathbb{Q}_p}{\mathbb{Z}_p}, \qquad S := \left(Z\otimes \tfrac{\mathbb{Q}_p}{\mathbb{Z}_p}\right)\cap \left(W\otimes \tfrac{\mathbb{Q}_p}{\mathbb{Z}_p}\right), \qquad 0\to R\to S\to T\to 0.$$
Here $R$ is a finite power of $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ (a corank, modeling the rank), $T$ is finite (modeling $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$), and $S$ is of co-finite type (modeling $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}$).

> **Theorem 1.2 (p. 2).** (a) As $Z, W$ vary, $0\to R\to S\to T\to 0$ defines a discrete probability distribution $\mathcal{Q}_{2n}$ on isomorphism classes of short exact sequences of co-finite type $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules. (b) The $\mathcal{Q}_{2n}$ converge to a distribution $\mathcal{Q}$ as $n\to\infty$.

> **Conjecture 1.3 (the model; p. 2).** Fix a global field $k$. For each short exact sequence $\mathcal{S}$ of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules, the density of $\{E : \mathrm{Seq}_E \cong \mathcal{S}\}$ equals the $\mathcal{Q}$-probability of $\mathcal{S}$.

Stated plainly: $R$ models the rank object $E(k)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$, $S$ models $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}E$, and $T$ models $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$. This is one conjecture, over an arbitrary global field, ordering by height (Remark 1.4 explains why height and not conductor).

**Consequences of Conjecture 1.3 (p. 3).** The authors prove that Conjecture 1.3 IMPLIES each of:
- Asymptotically $50\%$ of $E/k$ have rank $0$ and $50\%$ have rank $1$ (Goldfeld's minimalist conjecture; Conjecture B of [Gol79]).
- $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite for $100\%$ of $E/k$.
- The Poonen-Rains conjecture on the distribution of $\mathrm{Sel}_p E$ holds, in fact generalized to $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}E$ for every $e\geq 0$ (Section 5.5).
- Delaunay's Cohen-Lenstra-type conjecture for the distribution of $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$ for rank-$r$ curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ holds for $r=0$ and $r=1$ (Section 5.6).

The honest reading: these are conditional consequences of an unproven conjecture. Crucially, **for $r\geq 2$ the model says nothing about the distribution of $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$**, because the locus $\{(Z,W) : \mathrm{rk}(Z\cap W) = r\}$ has measure $0$ for $r\geq 2$ (Proposition 5.6). To reach $r\geq 2$ the authors must restrict to that measure-zero locus and put a separate measure on it (Theorem 1.6, Conjecture 1.7), which is exactly where the rank-$\geq 2$ regime drops out of the main model.

**Section 1.4 and Section 3: the alternating-matrix avatar (pp. 4, 6-9).** The same predicted $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$ distribution arises as the cokernel of a random alternating matrix, in the Friedman-Washington spirit. For even $n$, choose $A\in M_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)_{\mathrm{alt}}$ (zero diagonal, $A^t = -A$) at random with Haar measure; let $\mathcal{A}_{n,0}$ be the distribution of $\mathrm{coker}\,A$, and $\mathcal{A}_{n,r}$ the distribution of $(\mathrm{coker}\,A)_{\mathrm{tors}}$ over alternating $A$ of corank $r$.

> **Theorem 1.10 (p. 4).** For each $r\geq 0$: (a) $\mathcal{A}_{n,r}$ converge to a limit $\mathcal{A}_r$ as $n\to\infty$ through $n$ with $n-r$ even; (b) $\mathcal{A}_r$ coincides with $\mathcal{T}_r$ (the isotropic-intersection $\mathrm{Sha}$-model of Theorem 1.6).

> **Theorem 3.9 (p. 9).** For each symplectic $p$-group $G$, for random alternating $A\in M_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)_{\mathrm{alt}}$ ($n$ even),
> $$\lim_{n\to\infty,\, n\text{ even}} \mathrm{Prob}(\mathrm{coker}\,A \cong G) = \frac{\#G}{\#\mathrm{Sp}(G)}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1 - p^{1-2i}),$$
> and the sum of the right side over all symplectic $p$-groups $G$ equals $1$.

The combinatorial engine is the Haar-measure computation of $\mathrm{Prob}([\,,\,]_A = [\,,\,])$ for a fixed nondegenerate alternating pairing (Corollary 3.5, $= \frac{\#\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{F}_p)_{\mathrm{alt}}}{\#M_m(\mathbb{F}_p)_{\mathrm{alt}}}(\#G)^{1-n}$), with $\frac{\#\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb{F}_p)_{\mathrm{alt}}}{\#M_m(\mathbb{F}_p)_{\mathrm{alt}}} = \prod_{i=1}^{m/2}(1-p^{1-2i})$ (Lemma 3.6), plus the genericity input that random alternating matrices have small kernel: $\mathrm{Prob}(\dim\ker A \geq n/2)\to 0$ (Lemma 3.7). The weight $\#G/\#\mathrm{Sp}(G)$ is exactly Delaunay's $w_G$, so the matrix model and Delaunay's heuristic produce the same law by construction.

**Section 1.5: the Cassels-Tate pairing as built-in evidence (p. 4, and Proposition 5.5, p. 16).** The Cassels-Tate pairing $\mathrm{Sha}\times\mathrm{Sha}\to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ is alternating, with kernel the maximal divisible subgroup; on $p$-parts it gives a nondegenerate alternating $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]\times\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]\to \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ when $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite. The model's $T = (\mathrm{coker}\,A)_{\mathrm{tors}}$ carries a canonical nondegenerate alternating pairing (Proposition 5.5, defined via $[x,y] = Q(z_x - w_y)\bmod \mathbb{Z}_p$), matching this arithmetic structure. This is why the random object is alternating, not merely symmetric: it is reverse-engineered to carry a Cassels-Tate pairing.

**Section 5.1 through 5.4: why the limit exists and splits (pp. 15-18).** Proposition 5.1: the maximal divisible subgroup of $S$ is $R$. Corollary 5.2: $T$ is finite. Corollary 5.3: the sequence $0\to R\to S\to T\to 0$ splits. Proposition 5.4: $S[q]\cong (Z/qZ)\cap(W/qW)$ in $V/qV$ for $q = p^e$, tying $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}$ directly to the mod-$p^e$ intersection. Proposition 5.6 is the rank prediction:

> **Proposition 5.6 (p. 17).** Fix $n$. If $Z$ is chosen randomly from $\mathrm{OGr}_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ (with $W$ fixed), then $Z\cap W$ is free of rank $0$ or $1$, each with probability $1/2$.

Combined with Conjecture 1.3 this is the source of the $50/50$ rank-$0$/$1$ split and the density-zero rank-$\geq 2$ claim. The parity mechanism is Proposition 4.5 / Corollary 4.7: $\mathrm{OGr}_n$ splits into even and odd components, $\dim(Z\cap W)\bmod 2$ records the component, and $\dim(Z_1\cap Z_2)+\dim(Z_2\cap Z_3)+\dim(Z_3\cap Z_1)\equiv n\pmod 2$.

**Section 5.5: predictions for $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}$ (pp. 19-20).** Lemma 5.7: asymptotically $100\%$ of $E/k$ have $E(k)_{\mathrm{tors}} = 0$. Proposition 5.9(c): under $E(k)_{\mathrm{tors}}=0$, $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}E\cong (\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}E)[p^e]$, so the $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}$ model determines the $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}$ model. Theorem 5.10 computes the model's moments: the average of $I_m(Z\cap W)$ (injective homomorphism count) tends to $q^{m(m+1)/2}$, leading to

> **Conjecture 5.11 (p. 20).** For each $m\geq 0$, the average of $I_m(\mathrm{Sel}_q E)$ over $E\in\mathcal{E}$ equals $q^{m(m+1)/2}$.

Remark 5.13 records that the prime case recovers the Poonen-Rains $m$-th moment $\prod_{i=1}^m(p^i+1)$, and Remark 5.14 notes the $q=2, m=1$ case relates to the Tamagawa number $\tau(\mathrm{PGL}_2)=2$, the source of Bhargava-Shankar's average $3$.

**Section 5.6: the $\mathrm{Sha}$ law and finiteness, as a model output (pp. 21-22).** Define $T := \bigoplus_p T_p$ with each $T_p$ chosen independently from $\mathcal{T}_r$ (equiv. $\mathcal{A}_r$).

> **Theorem 5.15 (p. 21).** If $r\geq 1$, then $T$ is finite with probability $1$, and has the distribution of Delaunay's heuristic (with the correction $r/2 \rightsquigarrow r$).

The proof uses $\mathrm{Prob}(T_p\neq 0) = 1 - \prod_{i=r+1}^\infty(1-p^{1-2i}) = O(p^{-1-2r})$ (from Theorem 3.9 with $G=0$); for $r\geq 1$, $\sum_p \mathrm{Prob}(T_p\neq 0)$ converges and Borel-Cantelli gives finiteness almost surely. **This is the precise sense in which $\mathrm{Sha}$-finiteness is modeled: it is a probability-$1$ statement about the random object, conditional on Conjecture 1.3 to transfer it to actual curves.** For $r=0$ the sum diverges, so no individual group has positive density, and one must instead integrate $L^1$ functions against Delaunay's weight $w_G = \#G/\#\mathrm{Sp}(G)$ (Theorem 5.16, matching Delaunay's prediction). Theorem 5.16 proves the model's averaged $\mathrm{Sha}$ predictions agree with Delaunay's.

**Section 6: the only genuinely PROVEN arithmetic (pp. 23-26).** This is where unconditional theorems live. The aim is to justify why $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}E$ should behave like an intersection of maximal isotropic direct summands.

> **Proposition 6.1 (p. 24).** Let $E/k$ be an elliptic curve, $p$ prime, $e\geq 0$. If $\mathrm{char}\,k\neq p$ and the image $G$ of $G_k$ in $\mathrm{Aut}\,E[p^e]\cong \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})$ contains $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})$, or if $\mathrm{char}\,k = p$ and the image is cyclic, then $\mathrm{Sha}^1(k, E[p^e]) = 0$, where $\mathrm{Sha}^1(k,M) := \ker(\mathrm{H}^1(k,M)\to \prod_v \mathrm{H}^1(k_v,M))$.

Remark 6.2 notes the hypothesis holds for $100\%$ of $E/k$ (Hilbert irreducibility). The proof rests on Lemma 6.3 (a Chebotarev / inflation-restriction toolkit reducing $\mathrm{Sha}^1$ to $\mathrm{H}^1_{\mathrm{cyc}}$) and, in characteristic $p$, on Lemmas 6.4 and 6.5 (finite-group-scheme cohomology, $\mathrm{Sha}^1(k,\mu_p) = \mathrm{Sha}^1(k,\alpha_p) = 0$). These vanishing results are real theorems. They are the arithmetic backbone underneath the Poonen-Rains identification of $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}E$ with an intersection of two maximal isotropic subgroups of $\mathrm{H}^1(\mathbf{A}, E[p^e])$, and they say nothing about $\mathrm{Sha}(E)$ itself, only about the local-global kernel for the finite module $E[p^e]$.

## Project mapping

1. **Proven, exactly.** The MATRIX side: Theorem 1.2 ($\mathcal{Q}_{2n}\to\mathcal{Q}$), Theorem 1.6, Theorem 1.10 (existence/coincidence of the limiting distributions), Theorem 3.9 (the explicit $\mathrm{coker}$ law), Theorem 5.10/5.16 (model moments and the Delaunay-compatibility), and the ARITHMETIC side: Proposition 6.1 with Lemmas 6.3 through 6.5 (vanishing of $\mathrm{Sha}^1(k,E[p^e])$ for $100\%$ of curves, unconditional, over any global field). These are theorems about random algebra and about finite-module cohomology.

2. **The gap (everything that touches an actual elliptic curve's $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}$, $\mathrm{Sha}$, or rank is conjectural).** Conjecture 1.3 is the load-bearing assumption; from it follow $50/50$ rank $0$/$1$, density-zero rank $\geq 2$, $100\%$ finite $\mathrm{Sha}$, the $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^e}$ distribution (Conjecture 5.11), and Delaunay's $\mathrm{Sha}$ law. None of these is proven. In particular, finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ is a probability-$1$ output of the model (Theorem 5.15) CONDITIONAL on Conjecture 1.3, not a theorem about curves. The model also structurally abandons rank $\geq 2$: that locus is measure zero (Proposition 5.6), so the main distribution carries no information there, and the rank-$\geq 2$ refinement (Conjecture 1.7) is a separate, also-unproven, conjecture on a measure-zero set.

3. **Exact regime.** Population / averages over all $E$ ordered by height, simultaneously across all ranks. The model is intrinsically $p$-adic: it lives in $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules and predicts $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}$ and $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$ one prime at a time, then reassembles over $p$. It is orthogonal to the per-curve analytic-rank-$0$/$1$ vs $\geq 2$ axis, but it does make a per-rank prediction: it predicts the answer for ranks $0$ and $1$ cleanly and degenerates at rank $\geq 2$. It is NOT archimedean and carries no $L$-function, no root number, no Heegner point.

4. **Detector scorecard.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES, with an honest nuance.** The model predicts the full distribution of $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}$ and $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$, not merely a parity. BUT the rank prediction itself (Proposition 5.6, $\mathrm{rk}(Z\cap W)\in\{0,1\}$ each with probability $1/2$) is driven by a parity invariant: the even/odd component of $\mathrm{OGr}_n$ via $\dim(Z\cap W)\bmod 2$ (Proposition 4.5, Corollary 4.7). The model never produces a rank $\geq 2$ from the bulk measure precisely because the parity mechanism caps the generic intersection at $1$. So the detector is satisfied (this is not a method that only recovers root-number parity), but the structural reason the model cannot reach rank $\geq 2$ is itself parity-flavored, which is the same wall the Heegner-point machine hits from the constructive side.
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): TRIGGERS by design, and must be flagged loudly.** Finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ is MODELED here (Theorem 5.15: probability $1$ for $r\geq 1$ conditional on Conjecture 1.3), not proven. The Cassels-Tate nondegeneracy that motivates the alternating structure (Section 1.5) only holds when $\mathrm{Sha}[p^\infty]$ is finite, which is itself the open question for analytic rank $\geq 2$. Any downstream method that consumes BKLPR's predicted distributions of $\mathrm{Sel}$ or $\mathrm{Sha}$ inherits this unproven finiteness as an assumption. This is the central caution for this source: the predictions look like facts and are not.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): TRIGGERS in a subtle, instructive way.** Conjecture 1.3 is stated over an arbitrary global field $k$, function fields included, with the SAME distribution $\mathcal{Q}$. The model is field-agnostic: it sees only $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module data and a quadratic form, with no geometric Frobenius and no distinction between $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$. Over a function field, BSD is a theorem CONDITIONAL on finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ (Tate, Artin-Tate, Milne); finiteness is the hypothesis there, not a consequence, and it is supplied by the geometric Frobenius and the associated cohomological machinery a number field lacks. So over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ the model's predictions can be cross-checked against genuine geometry; over $\mathbb{Q}$ the same predictions float free. A method that "validates" the BKLPR predictions over a function field and then claims the number-field case is settled has imported the Frobenius the model deliberately does not contain. The detector flag: the universality of $\mathcal{Q}$ across fields is a feature of the heuristic, NOT evidence that the number-field consequences are proven.

5. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** The model lands cleanly on the proven side and degenerates exactly at the open boundary. Proposition 5.6 gives rank $\in\{0,1\}$ as the generic outcome, matching the only regime where BSD is a theorem (Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin), and the model's rank-$\geq 2$ predictions live on a measure-zero locus requiring a separate conjecture (1.7). This is a precise mirror of the constructive wall: the Heegner machine produces one point and cannot reach rank $2$; the BKLPR random intersection produces corank $\leq 1$ generically and cannot reach corank $2$ from the bulk measure. The "one missing object" of the atlas (a rank-$\geq 2$ point construction or an unconditional $\mathrm{Sha}$ bound) is untouched: a heuristic cannot construct it, by design.

6. **What it feeds.** Primarily the statistics architecture (atlas 5) as its heuristic spine, and it supplies the predicted distributions that Bhargava-Shankar's average $3$ is a single moment of (Remark 5.13/5.14). For the proof program it is a boundary marker and a warning label: it tells Direction 01 ([`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md)) that rank $\geq 2$ is statistically negligible AND structurally invisible to the model, so a proof there gets no help from these distributions. It tells Direction 03 ([`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md)) that the $p$-adic $\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}$ object has a clean conjectural shape per prime, which is the right target for an honest Selmer-bound that does NOT assume $\mathrm{Sha}$ finite. The reusable export is Section 6's unconditional $\mathrm{Sha}^1(k,E[p^e]) = 0$ vanishing (Proposition 6.1), the one piece a Lean formalization or a rigorous Selmer argument could actually cite. No current experiment in [`experiments/PLAN.md`](../../../experiments/PLAN.md) tests these distributions; the natural validation hook is a numerical check that the $\mathrm{Sel}_2$ moments on a height-ordered sample track $q^{m(m+1)/2} = 2^{m(m+1)/2}$ (Conjecture 5.11 at $q=2$), parallel to the average-$3$ check and the Sato-Tate thread.
