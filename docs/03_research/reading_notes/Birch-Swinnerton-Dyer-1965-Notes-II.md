# Reading note: B. J. Birch, H. P. F. Swinnerton-Dyer, "Notes on elliptic curves. II," Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik (Crelle) 218 (1965), 79-108.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the founding document. It is the paper where the Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture is born, not as a theorem and not even first as a statement about $L$-values, but as an empirical regularity squeezed out of EDSAC machine runs on the one-parameter family $\Gamma_D: y^2 z = x^3 - D x z^2$. The defining object of the entire program, the proposed equality $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q}) = \operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E, s)$, appears here as the "weak" conjecture (A), born from the observed growth law $f(P) = \prod_{p \le P} N_p/p \sim C(\log P)^g$. The paper also states the "strong" conjecture (B) relating the leading coefficient to $\#\mathrm{Sha}$, periods, and the Tamagawa number $\tau(D)$. Everything later in this repository (Gross-Zagier, Kolyvagin, Iwasawa theory, the statistics) is measured against the two conjectures stated on pages 79-81 of this paper. It proves none of them.

## One-line takeaway

For the CM family $\Gamma_D: y^2 z = x^3 - D x z^2$, Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer observed by machine that the partial Euler product $f(P) = \prod_{p \le P} N_p/p$ grows like $C(\log P)^g$ where $g$ is the rank, and that the same data is governed by the Hecke $L$-series value $L_D(1)$ (which they evaluate in finite closed form via Weierstrass division values); from this they conjecture (A) the rank equals $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}$ of the $L$-series and (B) the leading term encodes $\#\mathrm{Sha}$, periods and the Tamagawa number, but they prove neither, they prove only that their finite invariant $\sigma(D)$ is a rational integer (class field theory), and they explicitly flag that finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ is unproven for every curve and that the strong conjecture (B) presupposes it.

## Technical content (section by section)

### $\S 1$: Introduction, the growth law, and the two conjectures (pp. 79-81)

**The empirical object (p. 79).** Working with the cubic $\Gamma: y^2 z = x^3 - A x z^2 - B z^3$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, and motivated by Siegel's mass formula on quadratic forms, they study the product
$$ f(P) = \prod_{p \le P} N_p / p, $$
where $N_p$ is the number of points of $\Gamma$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. In autumn 1958 they computed $f(P)$ as $P$ grew for many curves and found its rate of increase "fairly closely correlated with the number of generators of infinite order" of the Mordell-Weil group (their $\mathscr{A}$), and that $f(P)$ tended to be large when the generators were small.

**Conjecture (A), the original BSD conjecture (p. 79).** Stated verbatim as a conjecture:

> **(A)** *If $g$ is the number of generators of $\mathscr{A}$ of infinite order, then $f(P) \sim C(\log P)^g$ as $P \to \infty$, and $\zeta_\Gamma(s) \sim C'(s-1)^g$ as $s \to 1$, for some constants $C, C'$ depending on $\Gamma$.*

This is the **defining equality of the BSD program**: the order of vanishing of the $L$-series at the center equals the Mordell-Weil rank $g$. (Here it is phrased through $\zeta_\Gamma$; with $\zeta_\Gamma(s) = \zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)/L_\Gamma(s)$ the vanishing of $\zeta_\Gamma$ to order $g$ at $s=1$ is the vanishing of $L_\Gamma(s)$ to order $g$ at $s = 1$.) It is a **conjecture in every rank**. Nothing in the paper proves it.

**The numerical difficulty and the retreat to a special curve (pp. 80-81).** They could not pin down $C$ by this method: $f(P)$ oscillates badly (error $\sim 10\%$), the bad-prime factors at $p \mid 6(4A^3 - 27B^2)$ were "almost certainly the wrong ones" (Tamagawa's work, $\S 7$, later fixed this), and $O(p)$ operations per $N_p$ made large $P$ infeasible. So for the rest of the paper they confine to the CM family
$$ \Gamma_D: y^2 z = x^3 - D x z^2 \qquad (1.3) $$
where complex multiplication by $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ lets $\zeta_\Gamma$ be written through the Riemann zeta and a Hecke $L$-series $L_D(s)$:
$$ \zeta_{\Gamma_D}(s) = \frac{\zeta(s)\,\zeta(s-1)}{L_D(s)}, \qquad \text{formally } L_D(1) = \prod (N_p/p)^{-1}. \qquad (1.4) $$
In summer 1960 they approximated $L_D(1)$ and got "fair confidence" for the **weaker form of (A)**:
$$ L_D(1) = 0 \quad\text{if and only if}\quad g > 0. \qquad (1.5) $$
This is the rank-0 case of the conjecture: $L(E,1) \ne 0 \iff$ rank $0$. They could decide *whether* $L_D(1)$ vanished but, when $g = 0$, not compute it accurately enough to interpret. **One direction of (1.5), namely rank $> 0 \Rightarrow L_D(1) = 0$ for CM curves of this type, is the theorem Coates-Wiles proved in 1977; the full rank-0 biconditional and its quantitative refinement (and the analytic-rank-1 case) are Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin. In 1965 the whole of (1.5) is a conjecture supported only by EDSAC data, and it is open to this day for analytic rank $\ge 2$.**

**Davenport's idea and the finite form (p. 80).** Davenport suggested $L_D(1)$ should be expressible in finite form via division values of the Weierstrass $\wp$-function (with $\wp'^2 = 4\wp^3 - 4\wp$). Exact evaluation in simple cases, plus Kneser's remark that "the answer should be an integer," gave the shape
$$ L_D(1) = \begin{cases} D^{-1/4}\,\omega\,\sigma(D) & D > 0, \\ (-4D)^{-1/4}\,\omega\,\sigma(D) & D < 0, \end{cases} \qquad (1.6) $$
where $\omega$ is the real period of $\wp$ and $\sigma(D)$ is a (conjecturally small, positive) **integer**. They prove in $\S 4$ that $\sigma(D)$ is a rational integer (sign unknown), and compute it on EDSAC II for many $D$ (Table 1).

**Conjecture (B), the strong conjecture (p. 81).** After identifying $\sigma(D)$ heuristically with $\#$Tate-Shafarevich (which Cassels showed is a power of $2$ times a square when finite), they replace the awkward product $\prod N_p/p$ by the **Tamagawa number** $\tau(D)$ and state:

> **(B)** *If $g = 0$ then the order of the Tate-Shafarevich group of $\Gamma_D$ is $\eta^2(D)/\tau(D)$, where $\eta(D)$ is the number of rational points on $\Gamma_D$.*

This is the **rank-0 strong BSD formula**: $\#\mathrm{Sha} = \#\Gamma_D(\mathbb{Q})^2 / \tau(D)$, with the torsion-squared replacing the regulator (there is no regulator in rank $0$). They are explicit about the load-bearing assumption (p. 81): "unfortunately little is known about the Tate-Shafarevich group; indeed there is not even any case in which it has been proved to be finite." **Conjecture (B) presupposes finiteness of Sha and the paper says so.**

**Function-field analogue, named on p. 81.** They note that "certain cases of the function field analogue of (A) are known to be true," citing Mumford (two elliptic curves over a finite field are isogenous iff they have the same $N_p$ in the classical sense), deducing the analogue of (1.5) over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$, and citing Tate's conjectures on zeta functions of varieties over finite fields "which he has confirmed in many cases." This is the function-field mirage, present at the creation.

### $\S 2$: The zeta-function of $\Gamma$ (pp. 81-83)

For genus-1 $\Gamma$, the local zeta-function is
$$ \zeta_{\Gamma, p}(s) = \frac{1 + (N_p - p - 1)p^{-s} + p^{1-2s}}{(1 - p^{-s})(1 - p^{1-2s})}, \qquad (2.1) $$
so globally $\zeta_\Gamma(s) = \zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)/L_\Gamma(s)$ with $L_\Gamma(s) = \prod_p \{1 + (N_p - p - 1)p^{-s} + p^{1-2s}\}^{-1}$. The paper states plainly (p. 82) that for general $\Gamma$ this product converges only in $\Re(s) > 3/2$ and "it is not known whether it can be continued analytically outside this region." **Analytic continuation of $L(E,s)$ past $\Re(s) > 3/2$ was not available in 1965 for general $E$; it is exactly what modularity (Wiles et al.) later supplied.** For the CM curve $\Gamma_D$, Deuring's work gives continuation and a functional equation, and they write $L_D(s)$ explicitly as a Hecke $L$-series with quartic-residue Größencharakter (formulae (2.5)-(2.7)):
$$ L_D(s) = \sum_{\sigma \equiv 1 (2+2i)} \left(\frac{D}{\sigma}\right)_4 \frac{\bar\sigma}{(N\sigma)^s}. \qquad (2.7) $$
They also note $\Gamma_D$ is $2$-isogenous to $\Gamma_{-4D}: y^2 z = x^3 + 4Dxz^2$, so the two share $g$, $N_p$, zeta and $L$-series but may differ in the $2$-part of Sha.

### $\S 3$: Finite formulae for $L_D(1)$ (pp. 84-90)

Starting from (2.7) and assuming $D$ a fourth-power-free Gaussian integer, they analytically continue $L_D(s)$ to $s = 1$ and rewrite $L_D(1)$ as a finite sum over residue classes, using the Weierstrass $\zeta$- and $\wp$-functions (periods $\omega, i\omega$ with $\omega = 2^{1/2}\pi e^{-\pi/6}\prod(1 - e^{-2\pi n})^2 = 2.6220575\ldots$). The master formula (3.5) reduces, in the four cases by residue of $D \bmod 4$, to closed sums such as
$$ L_D(1) = \frac{\omega}{\Delta}\sum_\beta \left(\frac{\beta}{D}\right)_4 \frac{\wp}{\wp^2 - 2\wp - 1} \quad (D \equiv 1 \bmod 4, \, D \ne 1), \qquad (3.13) $$
with $L_1(1) = \tfrac14\omega$ for the excluded value. These are the formulae actually fed to the machine. The point is that $L_D(1)$, an analytic object, becomes a **finite algebraic expression in division values of $\wp$**, hence exactly computable.

### $\S 4$: $\sigma(D)$ is a rational integer (pp. 91-92)

Using the Kronecker Jugendtraum / class field theory over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ (Lemmas 6-7, the strahlklass field mod $2\Delta$ generated by $\wp(\alpha\omega/\Delta)$, Artin symbol action), they prove the Corollary that $E^{1/4}\sum_\beta (\beta/E)_4 R(\wp(\beta\omega/\Delta)) \in \mathbb{Q}(i)$, and deduce **Theorem 1**: for $D$ a rational integer, fourth-power-free, $|D| > 2$, the quantity in (1.6) is a **rational integer** $\sigma(D)$ (the rationality argument bounds its denominator by a fixed power of $2\Delta$ whose only rational-integer divisors are $\pm 1$, so $\sigma(D) \in \mathbb{Z}$). This is the **one genuine theorem of the paper about the conjecture's invariants**: it makes $\sigma(D)$ a well-defined integer, but proves nothing about its sign and nothing about rank or Sha.

### $\S 5$: Estimation of $g$ by descent (pp. 92-96)

To compare data against (1.5) they need $g$. Multiplication by $2$ is factored through the isogeny $\Gamma_{-4D} \to \Gamma_D$, and $\nu$-coverings (homogeneous spaces) are organized into groups $G_\nu$ (everywhere locally soluble) and $G'_\nu$ (with a rational point), with $G'_\nu \cong \mathscr{A}/\nu\mathscr{A}$. Writing $2^t$ for the order of the $2$-part of Sha they get (Lemma 9)
$$ g = \lambda' + \lambda_1' - 2, \qquad g + t \le \lambda + \lambda_1 - 2, \qquad (5.6) $$
the "number of first descents," and using $\lambda^*$ (coverings lifting to everywhere-locally-soluble $2$-coverings) a second-descent refinement $g + t = \lambda^* + \lambda_1^* - 2$. They report that third descent was worked out but, "owing to machine development," not run, so some $g$ entries in Table 1 are left as inequalities ($1{-}$ meaning $g \le 1$ with nontrivial Sha; $1{+}$ meaning a generator was found). **This is the honest origin of the rank-computation gap: even the data backing the conjecture has unresolved ranks where descent stalls.**

### $\S 6$: The Tamagawa number (pp. 96-99)

For $V = \Gamma_D$ with invariant differential $\omega = dx/y$, they define $\tau(D) = \prod_p \int_{\Gamma_p} \omega_p$ over all primes including infinity (formal product, summed à la Abel since it differs from $[L_D(1)]^{-1}$ only at finitely many primes). Local integrals are computed in Lemmas 11-14: $\int\omega_p = N_p/p$ at good odd $p$ (Lemma 12), special values at $p \mid D$ and $p = 2$ and $\infty$ ($\int\omega_\infty = 4\omega D^{-1/4}$ for $D > 0$). Combining with (1.6) gives the clean identity
$$ \tau(D)\,\sigma(D) = 4\prod \int\omega_p \qquad (6.4) $$
over the finitely many bad primes. This is the technical device that lets conjecture (B) be stated with $\tau(D)$ in place of the divergent $\prod N_p/p$. It is bookkeeping that makes the strong conjecture computable; it proves nothing.

### $\S 7$: The numerical evidence on EDSAC 2 (pp. 99-108)

They computed $\sigma(D)$ on EDSAC 2 for all fourth-power-free, non-$4$-divisible $D$ with $|\Delta| < 108$ or $|\Delta| \in \{113, 165, 195, 231\}$, batching by $\Delta$ (a run took about $\Delta^2/20$ seconds), with a built-in machine-error check (the result must be an integer). For as many curves as feasible they also computed $g$ by the $\S 5$ descents (first, and where possible second). **Table 1** records, per $D$, the values of $\sigma, g, \lambda, \lambda_1$ (and the same for $-D, 2D, -2D$); **Table 2** flags (asterisk on $\sigma$) the cases where $\sigma \ne 0$ but $\tau(D) \ne \eta^2(D)$, i.e. the cases of nontrivial Sha predicted by (B). Their summary verdict (p. 100): with $\gamma = \eta^2/\tau$, the conjectures are that $\gamma \ne 0 \iff g = 0$ and then $\gamma = \#\mathrm{Sha}$; "the first part of this agrees with our calculations whenever we know $g$," and the known $2$-component of Sha "yields always the correct power of $2$ in $\gamma$." This is the entire evidential basis: **agreement of machine data with both conjectures, in the cases where $g$ and the $2$-part of Sha could be computed, for one CM family.**

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN here vs the GAP.** Proven: $\sigma(D) \in \mathbb{Z}$ (Theorem 1, $\S 4$, via class field theory over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$); the finite closed form for $L_D(1)$ ($\S 3$); the Tamagawa identity $\tau(D)\sigma(D) = 4\prod\int\omega_p$ ($\S 6$); the descent inequalities for $g$ ($\S 5$). GAP: **everything that is BSD**. Conjecture (A) (rank $=$ $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L$), its weak form (1.5) ($L(E,1) = 0 \iff g > 0$), and conjecture (B) (the leading-term / Sha formula) are all stated as **conjectures**, supported only by EDSAC data on the family $\Gamma_D$. The paper proves no rank equality and no finiteness of Sha.

2. **Exact regime.** **Archimedean** (complex $L_D(1)$ and its value at the center $s = 1$; no $p$-adic $L$-function appears, that is the 1974 Mazur-Swinnerton-Dyer sequel). The data covers **all ranks** $g$ but the conjectures are *verified*, not *proven*, in any rank. The deepest data point is the rank-$0$ vs positive-rank dichotomy (1.5); higher-rank entries in Table 1 are exactly where the descent stalls and ranks are left as inequalities. Restricted to the **CM family** $\Gamma_D$ with complex multiplication by $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ (this is what makes $L_D(s)$ a Hecke $L$-series with continuation and functional equation, a luxury general $E/\mathbb{Q}$ did not have until modularity).

3. **Detectors.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES, and is in fact the antidote.** This paper is the *source of the full equality* (A), not a parity surrogate. It does not stop at root numbers or rank mod $2$; it conjectures the exact order of vanishing equals the exact rank, and its evidence is the integer growth exponent $g$ in $f(P) \sim C(\log P)^g$, which is rank, not rank mod $2$. Any method elsewhere in the repo that recovers only parity is failing to reach what BSD-II already demanded in 1965.
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): TRIGGERED, and honestly disclosed.** Conjecture (B) presupposes $\#\mathrm{Sha}$ finite, and the authors state on p. 81 that finiteness is proven for no curve, and that they have only ever computed the $2$-component. The strong-BSD formula here is conditional on a hypothesis open to this day in rank $\ge 2$. The note flags this; the paper itself flags it.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): NAMED at the creation, not fallen into.** Page 81 cites the function-field side as already partly settled: Mumford's isogeny criterion gives the function-field analogue of the *weak* form (1.5) (curves over a finite field with the same $N_p$ are isogenous), while the analogue of the *full* (A) rests on Tate's conjectures on zeta functions of varieties over finite fields, which in 1965 were themselves conjectures "confirmed in many cases," not theorems. The number-field conjecture is kept strictly open throughout. The paper is scrupulous about the distinction: the geometric/Frobenius case is a separate, easier world (and Tate, Artin-Tate, and Milne later made the function-field BSD a theorem *under finite Sha*). This is the template for the mirage detector: a result that "works over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$" was already understood in 1965 to be importing something the number field lacks.

4. **Control pair (rank $\le 1$ proven vs rank $\ge 2$ open).** This paper *predates* the control pair and *defines its measuring stick*. The weak form (1.5) is precisely the rank-$0$ boundary later settled in low analytic rank (Coates-Wiles proved one direction for CM curves; Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin established the rank equality and finiteness of Sha in analytic rank $\le 1$). The rank-$\ge 2$ entries in Table 1 are exactly where BSD-II's own machinery (descent) runs out and ranks become inequalities: the open regime is visible as a gap in the original data tables. The conjecture asks for an equality in all $g$; the proof exists only for $g \le 1$; the difficulty BSD-II could not resolve is the same $g \ge 2$ object the program still targets.

5. **What it feeds.** This note is the **root** that all research directions ultimately reference. Primary feed to **Direction 01** ([`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md)): conjecture (A) in rank $\ge 2$ is the literal object Direction 01 must construct, and the stalled third descent in $\S 5$ is the earliest sign that the rank-$2$ structure resists the rank-$\le 1$ toolkit. Feeds **Direction 03** ([`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md)): the archimedean finite formula for $L_D(1)$ and the Tamagawa identity (6.4) are the archimedean side of the leading-term comparison. Experiments: the growth law $f(P) = \prod_{p\le P} N_p/p \sim C(\log P)^g$ is directly machine-reproducible and is the conceptual ancestor of the weak-BSD-table and strong-BSD-quantities experiments; the $a_p$-from-point-counting convention in this repo is exactly BSD-II's $N_p = p + 1 - a_p$.
