## One-line takeaway

Wiles' Clay description states the conjecture in two strengths: the WEAK form $L(C,s)=c(s-1)^r+\cdots$ with $c\neq 0$ and $r=\mathrm{rank}\,C(\mathbb{Q})$ (so $L(C,1)=0\iff C(\mathbb{Q})$ infinite), and the STRONG (refined) form which, for the completed $L$-series $L^*(C,s)\sim c^*(s-1)^r$, predicts the exact leading coefficient $c^*=|\Sha_C|\,R_\infty\,w_\infty\prod_{p\mid 2\Delta}w_p\,/\,|C(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2$; and the document then records the only theorem in hand, namely that "if $L(C,s)\sim c(s-1)^m$ with $c\neq 0$ and $m=0$ or $1$, then the conjecture holds," assembled from Coates-Wiles (rank $0$, CM), Gross-Zagier (the Heegner point), Kolyvagin (rank $\leq 1$ and finiteness of $\Sha$ there), and the now-complete modularity of all $E/\mathbb{Q}$. The survey is explicit that $\Sha$-finiteness is open in general (it would follow from a proof of BSD), that the rank-$\geq2$ case has "almost no methods," and that the function-field analog is proven by Artin-Tate only under finiteness of $\Sha$, so the document itself encodes all three of this repo's detectors.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Front matter, genus stratification (PDF p. 1).** A polynomial relation $f(x,y)=0$ defines a curve $C_0$; a non-singular projective model $C$ has a genus, and $C_0(\mathbb{Q})$ and $C(\mathbb{Q})$ are simultaneously finite or infinite. The document stratifies by genus: **genus $\geq 2$** is settled by Faltings (Mordell's conjecture), stated here as a Theorem: "If the genus of $C_0$ is greater than or equal to $2$, then $C_0(\mathbb{Q})$ is finite," with the explicit caveat that the proof is NOT effective (an effective bound on the NUMBER of solutions exists but not on their height). **Genus $0$** is classical (Hilbert-Hurwitz reduce to lines and conics; $C(\mathbb{Q})\neq\varnothing$ iff $C$ has $p$-adic points for all $p$, the Legendre / Hasse-Minkowski criterion, a finite set of congruences; then $C(\mathbb{Q})$ is infinite and rationally parametrized). **Genus $1$** is "the most elusive case": no method is known to decide whether $C(\mathbb{Q})$ is empty, and when non-empty whether it is finite or infinite. A genus-$1$ curve with a rational point is an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Mordell-Weil and the rank (PDF p. 1).** Stated as a Theorem (Mordell 1922): for $C$ an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$,
$$C(\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^r\oplus C(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}},$$
with $C(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ finite. The integer $r\geq 0$ is the RANK; $r=0\iff C(\mathbb{Q})$ finite. A Weierstrass model $C:y^2=x^3+ax+b$ with $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$ is fixed, $\Delta$ is the discriminant of the cubic, and
$$N_p:=\#\{y^2\equiv x^3+ax+b\bmod p\},\qquad a_p:=p-N_p.$$

**The $L$-series (PDF p. 2).** The INCOMPLETE $L$-series omits the Euler factors at $p\mid 2\Delta$:
$$L(C,s):=\prod_{p\nmid 2\Delta}\bigl(1-a_p\,p^{-s}+p^{1-2s}\bigr)^{-1},$$
convergent for $\mathrm{Re}(s)>3/2$. The document records that the conjectured (Hasse) holomorphic continuation to all of $\mathbb{C}$ "has now been proved," citing Wiles, Taylor-Wiles, and Breuil-Conrad-Diamond-Taylor (references [25], [24], [1]). This is the modularity input: it is what makes the central point $s=1$ and the Taylor expansion there even meaningful.

**The Conjecture, weak form (PDF p. 2).**
> **Conjecture (Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer).** The Taylor expansion of $L(C,s)$ at $s=1$ has the form $L(C,s)=c(s-1)^r+\text{higher order terms}$ with $c\neq 0$ and $r=\mathrm{rank}(C(\mathbb{Q}))$.

In particular $L(C,1)=0\iff C(\mathbb{Q})$ is infinite. This is the analytic-rank-equals-algebraic-rank statement; it is a CONJECTURE, stated as such.

**The strong (refined) form, Remark 1 (PDF p. 2).** Defining the COMPLETED $L$-series $L^*(C,s)$ by including Euler factors at $p\mid 2\Delta$, the conjecture predicts $L^*(C,s)\sim c^*(s-1)^r$ with the exact leading coefficient
$$c^*=\frac{|\Sha_C|\,R_\infty\,w_\infty\prod_{p\mid 2\Delta}w_p}{|C(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2}.$$
The document specifies each factor: $|\Sha_C|$ is the order of the Tate-Shafarevich group, "a group which is not known in general to be finite although it is conjectured to be so," counting equivalence classes of homogeneous spaces (torsors) of $C$ that have points in all local fields; $R_\infty$ is the $r\times r$ REGULATOR, the determinant of the height pairing applied to a system of generators of $C(\mathbb{Q})/C(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$; the $w_p$ are the elementary local (Tamagawa) factors; and $w_\infty$ is a simple multiple of the real period of $C$. (The document refers to Tate, "see [20] or [22]," for the precise definitions of these factors.) The document flags directly that "a proof of the conjecture would also yield a proof of the finiteness of $\Sha_C$." (This is the official source for the claim that finiteness of $\Sha$ is a COROLLARY of, not a hypothesis for, BSD.)

**Remark 2, generalizations (PDF p. 2).** The conjecture extends to abelian varieties over any number field, and sits inside the broader special-value philosophy of Tate, Lichtenbaum, Deligne, Bloch, Beilinson, relating ranks of groups of algebraic cycles to orders of vanishing (or poles) of $L$-functions.

**Remark 3, the function-field analog (PDF p. 2).** The document states the function-field mirror with precision: there is an analogous conjecture for elliptic curves over function fields; Artin-Tate (reference [20]) proved "that the $L$-series has a zero of order AT LEAST $r$"; but "the conjecture itself remains unproved" over function fields too, AND "in the function field case it is now known to be equivalent to the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group" (Tate; Milne, [17, Corollary 9.7]). This is the canonical articulation of the function-field-mirage detector: even over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ the full statement is conditional on finite $\Sha$; the function-field "theorem" is only an inequality plus a $\Sha$-finiteness equivalence, not an unconditional rank equality.

**Remark 4, the role of modularity (PDF p. 2).** For the integrality of $|\Sha_C|$ in the $c^*$ formula, one needs no interpretation as a $\Sha$ order; Manin (reference [16]) proved this conditional on modularity, "now known for all elliptic curves" by Wiles / Taylor-Wiles / BCDT. So modularity is what makes even the FORMAL statement of the strong form well-grounded.

**Section 1, Early History (PDF p. 3).** Diophantus' tangent-and-secant construction; Fermat's descent (showing sets of solutions are sometimes finite or empty); and the CONGRUENT NUMBER PROBLEM: $n$ is congruent iff the curve $C_n:y^2=x^3-n^2x$ has infinitely many rational points, i.e. $C_n(\mathbb{Q})$ infinite $\iff n$ congruent. The document is careful with logical strength here: "Assuming the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture (or even the weaker statement that $C_n(\mathbb{Q})$ infinite $\iff L(C_n,1)=0$) one can show that any $n\equiv 5,6,7\bmod 8$ is a congruent number," and TUNNELL'S criterion (reference [23]) gives, again ASSUMING the conjecture, an explicit quadratic-form test for congruence: for $n$ odd and square-free, $n$ is congruent $\iff \#\{x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}:2x^2+y^2+8z^2=n\}=2\cdot\#\{x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}:2x^2+y^2+32z^2=n\}$, with an analogous criterion for $n$ even. The document is precise about logical direction: Tunnell proved the implication "$n$ congruent $\Rightarrow$ this counting identity holds" UNCONDITIONALLY, with the help of the main theorem of Coates-Wiles ([5]); the converse (criterion $\Rightarrow$ congruent) is what needs BSD. (Exact regime flag: the congruent-number application sits in the rank-$0$ vs rank-$\geq1$ distinction and uses only the $L(C_n,1)=0$ half.)

**Section 2, Recent History (PDF pp. 3-4).** Poincare's 1901 paper raised the question of the minimal number of generators of $C(\mathbb{Q})$. The conjecture itself was "first stated in the form we have given in the early 1960s" (Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer, reference [4]), "found experimentally using one of the early EDSAC computers at Cambridge." The status ladder is then given exactly:
- **Coates-Wiles 1976/77 ([5]):** if $C$ has complex multiplication and $L(C,1)\neq 0$, then $C(\mathbb{Q})$ is finite. (Rank-$0$ direction, CM hypothesis.)
- **Gross-Zagier (document says 1983; paper published Invent. Math. 84, 1986, reference [11]):** if $C$ is a modular elliptic curve with $L(C,1)=0$ but $L'(C,1)\neq 0$, then an earlier construction of Heegner gives a rational point of INFINITE order. (Rank-$1$ direction, produces ONE point.)
- **Kolyvagin 1990:** using new ideas plus Gross-Zagier, for modular elliptic curves, $L(C,1)\neq 0\Rightarrow r=0$, and $L(C,1)=0,\ L'(C,1)\neq 0\Rightarrow r=1$. The document states that "in the former case" (the $L(C,1)\neq 0$, $r=0$ direction) Kolyvagin "needed an analytic hypothesis which was confirmed soon afterwards," and points to Darmon [6] for the history. (External context, not in this source: that confirmation is the nonvanishing of a twisted $L'$ supplied by Bump-Friedberg-Hoffstein and Murty-Murty; the note flags it as imported, since the Clay description names no author for it.)

Then, because all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ are now modular, the document states the THEOREM in hand:
> **Theorem.** If $L(C,s)\sim c(s-1)^m$ with $c\neq 0$ and $m=0$ or $1$, then the conjecture holds.

With the closing remark that "in the cases where $m=0$ or $1$ some more precise results on $c$ (which of course depends on the curve) are known by work of Rubin and Kolyvagin," i.e. partial strong-form ($\Sha$-bound) results, again ONLY in $m\leq 1$. (This is the single most important sentence in the document for this repo: the proven theorem is exactly $m\in\{0,1\}$ and nothing for $m\geq 2$.)

**Section 3, Higher-dimensional varieties (PDF p. 4).** Lang's conjecture (reference [14]): removing from a variety $V$ the closure of all subvarieties that are images of $\mathbb{P}^1$ or of abelian varieties, the remaining open $W$ should have $W(\mathbb{Q})$ finite (proved by Faltings, [10], when $V$ is itself a subvariety of an abelian variety). The Elkies counterexample to Euler's 1769 conjecture is given as a worked instance where BSD-style methods on an abelian variety produce points: $2682440^4+15365639^4+18796760^4=20615673^4$, found via a genus-$1$ curve of infinite order on the relevant surface. The document closes honestly: "there are still almost no methods for finding such points," and expresses the hope that a proof of BSD will illuminate the general problem.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN, in the document's own words.** Exactly the theorem "$L(C,s)\sim c(s-1)^m$, $m=0$ or $1\Rightarrow$ BSD holds," resting on Coates-Wiles (CM rank $0$), Gross-Zagier (Heegner point), Kolyvagin (rank $\leq1$ and $\Sha$-finiteness there), and the modularity of all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ (Wiles / Taylor-Wiles / BCDT) which supplies the analytic continuation. Partial strong-form ($\Sha$) results in $m\leq1$ by Rubin and Kolyvagin. Nothing else is asserted as theorem.

2. **The GAP, stated by the source.** For $m\geq 2$ ("$r\geq2$") the document gives NO theorem and says higher-dimensional point-finding has "almost no methods." Finiteness of $\Sha_C$ is "not known in general to be finite although it is conjectured to be so," and would FOLLOW from BSD, not the reverse. The strong-form constant $c^*$ is conjectural in full; only its integral structure (via Manin) is anchored. The entire rank-$\geq2$ structure is open.

3. **EXACT regime.** Weak form: analytic rank $=$ algebraic rank, proven only for analytic rank $m\in\{0,1\}$, OPEN for $m\geq2$. Strong form: the leading coefficient $c^*$ with $|\Sha_C|$, $R_\infty$ (regulator), $w_\infty$ (real period multiple), $w_p$ (Tamagawa), torsion-squared denominator. ARCHIMEDEAN throughout as stated (the height pairing in $R_\infty$ is the Neron-Tate height; $w_\infty$ is a real period); the document does not state the $p$-adic Mazur-Tate-Teitelbaum analog, so the $p$-adic regime is OUT OF SCOPE of this source and lives in [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md).

4. **Three detectors.**
   - **Parity-only: the document distinguishes the FULL rank equality from parity.** It states $r=\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L$ as an equality of integers, not a congruence mod $2$. It never claims that the root number alone (which gives only $m\bmod 2$) suffices. So the official statement is exactly the thing parity-only methods fail to reach; this note is the canonical reference for what "more than parity" means.
   - **$\Sha$-finiteness-assumed: the document REFUSES to assume it.** It says $\Sha_C$ is only conjecturally finite and that a proof of BSD would PROVE its finiteness. Any method in this repo that uses finite $\Sha$ in the $m\geq2$ regime is therefore assuming an open consequence of the very theorem it wants. This source is the authority for that flag.
   - **Function-field-mirage: the document is the canonical citation.** Remark 3 states that over function fields Artin-Tate prove only $\mathrm{ord}\geq r$ (an inequality), the conjecture "remains unproved," and is "equivalent to the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group" ([17, Cor. 9.7]). So a number-field method that "works verbatim over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$" has either imported geometric Frobenius or silently assumed finite $\Sha$. This note pins the detector to its source.

5. **The rank $\leq1$ (proven) vs rank $\geq2$ (open) control pair.** This document IS the official drawing of that line: the proven Theorem is $m\in\{0,1\}$, full stop, and the open region is $m\geq2$. It credits the proven side to a Heegner-point / Euler-system machine that (per the Gross-Zagier and Kolyvagin notes) produces ONE point and reads $L$ to first order, hence cannot certify rank $2$. The control pair `37a1` (rank $1$, proven side) vs `389a1` (rank $2$, open side) is the experimental instantiation of exactly this sentence.

6. **Research direction / experiment fed.** This note is the upstream anchor for ALL directions: [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md) (the rank-$\geq2$ object that must beat the $m\leq1$ ceiling stated here), [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md) (generalize the Kolyvagin system the document credits), and [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md) (the $p$-adic analog of the archimedean $R_\infty,w_\infty$ the document writes down). Experimentally it fixes the precise quantities the strong-BSD thread must compute: $\#\Sha$, $R_\infty$, $w_\infty$, $\prod w_p$, $|C(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|$. The atlas top line should quote this source verbatim for the official weak and strong statements and for the "$m=0$ or $1$" theorem.
