# Reading note: K. Rubin, "Euler Systems," Annals of Mathematics Studies 147, Princeton University Press (2000)

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the canonical reference for the *axiomatic* Euler-system machine: it abstracts the method shared by Thaine, Kolyvagin, Kato, and Rubin into a single theorem that takes a norm-compatible family of Galois cohomology classes (an Euler system) and outputs an *upper bound* on a Selmer group, and dually on the relevant ideal class group or Tate-Shafarevich group. For BSD specifically, the book's elliptic-curve and CM applications give the cleanest statement of *why* the method certifies the $p$-part $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite in analytic rank $0$ for CM curves (via the Euler system of *elliptic units*) and *where the machine stops*: it needs a nonzero starting class at the bottom of the system, and a system "of the right length," and at analytic rank $\geq 2$ neither the Heegner-point system nor the elliptic-unit system supplies it.
>
> **Reading depth.** Written from authoritative knowledge of the monograph's structure (the axiomatic setup, the derived-class construction, the Selmer-bounding theorem, the Iwasawa-theoretic refinement, and the two worked examples: cyclotomic units in the $\mathbb{G}_m$ case and elliptic units in the CM case, plus the Galois-cohomology and module-theory appendices), cross-checked against Rubin's own open lecture notes "Euler systems and Kolyvagin systems" (UC Irvine, math.uci.edu/~krubin), which cover the same axioms and the elliptic-curve application. ## One-line takeaway

Rubin's monograph proves a single abstract theorem ("an Euler system for $T$ bounds the Selmer group $\operatorname{Sel}(T^*)$ from above, with the bound measured by the index of the bottom class") and then instantiates it twice that matter for BSD: cyclotomic units give the Mazur-Wiles main conjecture / Gras-style class-group bounds, and *elliptic units* give, for a CM elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of analytic rank $0$, finiteness of the $p$-part $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]$ for the primes covered and one divisibility in the two-variable main conjecture; the method is exact and unconditional *only where a nonzero, norm-compatible starting class exists*, which is the precise reason it cannot reach rank $\geq 2$.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Chapter I (Introduction) and Chapter II (The Euler system of a number field / general setup).**
Fix a $p$-adic representation: $T$ a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of finite rank with a continuous $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$-action, unramified outside a finite set, and let $T^* = \operatorname{Hom}(T, \mu_{p^\infty})$ be the Cartier dual. An **Euler system** $\mathbf{c}$ for $T$ (Definition, Chapter II) is a collection of classes
$$ c_F \in H^1(F, T) $$
indexed by finite abelian extensions $F$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ inside a large abelian field (typically $F$ ranging over subfields of $\mathbb{Q}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^\infty}) \cdot \mathbb{Q}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathfrak{m}})$ for squarefree $\mathfrak{m}$ prime to $p$), subject to the **norm-compatibility (Euler-factor) relation**: for $F \subset F'$ with $F'/F$ ramified exactly at a new prime $\ell$,
$$ \operatorname{Cores}_{F'/F}\, c_{F'} \;=\; P_\ell(\operatorname{Fr}_\ell^{-1})\, c_F, \qquad P_\ell(x) = \det\!\big(1 - \operatorname{Fr}_\ell^{-1} x \,\big|\, T^*\big), $$
i.e. the corestriction (norm) of a class one level up equals the local Euler factor $P_\ell$ of the $L$-function, evaluated at Frobenius, times the class below. This single relation is the axiomatic content; it encodes the $L$-function into the cohomology.

**Derived classes (the Kolyvagin derivative).**
From the top-level classes Rubin builds **Kolyvagin derivative classes**. For a squarefree product $\mathfrak{r} = \ell_1 \cdots \ell_k$ of "Kolyvagin primes" (primes $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod{p^M}$ with controlled Frobenius), one forms a derivative operator $D_{\mathfrak{r}} = \prod_i D_{\ell_i}$ (a telescoping sum over the cyclic Galois group of $\mathbb{Q}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_\ell)/\mathbb{Q}$) and shows $D_{\mathfrak{r}} c_{\mathbb{Q}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathfrak{r}})}$ descends, modulo $p^M$, to a class
$$ \kappa_{\mathfrak{r}} \in H^1(\mathbb{Q}, T/p^M T) $$
whose local behavior is *finite (unramified)* away from $\mathfrak{r}$ and *prescribed* at each $\ell_i$. These $\kappa_{\mathfrak{r}}$ are the actual annihilators: their local conditions trade ramification at the primes $\ell_i$ for global information.

**The Bounding Theorem (bounding the Selmer group).**
The central result (stated for general $T$ under a set of hypotheses on $T$: roughly, $T/pT$ irreducible, existence of $\tau \in G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ acting close to a scalar with $T/(\tau-1)T$ free of rank one, and a nontrivial bottom class) is:

> **Theorem (Rubin, Euler-system bound).** If $\mathbf{c}$ is an Euler system for $T$ with $c_{\mathbb{Q}} \neq 0$ (more precisely, $c_{\mathbb{Q}}$ not divisible by arbitrarily high powers of $p$), then the **Selmer group** $\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}, T^*)$ (Bloch-Kato / relaxed-strict Selmer condition) is finite, and its order is bounded by the index of the bottom class:
> $$ \#\, \operatorname{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}, T^*) \;\Big|\; [\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, T) : \mathbb{Z}_p\, c_{\mathbb{Q}} \,] \cdot (\text{local fudge factors}). $$

The Iwasawa-theoretic refinement (the $\Lambda$-adic chapters, applied over the cyclotomic tower) replaces $\mathbb{Q}$ by that tower and yields a **divisibility of characteristic ideals**: the characteristic ideal of the Selmer / class-group module divides the ideal generated by the image of the Euler system (a $p$-adic $L$-function), i.e. *one* inclusion of the main conjecture. The opposite inclusion is the harder, separately argued direction, not delivered by the Euler-system bound alone.

The load-bearing hypothesis for BSD purposes: the bound is *only as strong as the bottom class is nonzero*. If $c_{\mathbb{Q}} = 0$ the theorem gives nothing. The **length** (number of independent Euler systems / the "core rank") controls how many independent Selmer classes can be killed. A *single* Euler system of "rank one" bounds a corank-one Selmer group; bounding corank $\geq 2$ requires a system of higher core rank, which the classical constructions do not provide.

**Iwasawa theory (the two-variable picture).**
Rubin develops the $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[\operatorname{Gal}]]$-module theory and proves the Euler system gives a divisibility between the characteristic ideal of an Iwasawa module and the $p$-adic $L$-function attached to $\mathbf{c}$. This is the engine behind both the cyclotomic-unit and elliptic-unit applications.

**Cyclotomic units (the multiplicative group $\mathbb{G}_m$).**
Taking $T = \mathbb{Z}_p(1)$ and the Euler system of **cyclotomic units** $\{\,1 - \zeta_F\,\}$, the machine recovers (and in places reproves more cleanly) the **Mazur-Wiles main conjecture** for $\mathbb{Q}$ and Gras-conjecture-type bounds: the $p$-part of ideal class groups of abelian fields is bounded by the index of cyclotomic units (Stickelberger / analytic class number side). Regime: this is the $\mathbb{G}_m$ / Dirichlet-$L$-function case, fully proven.

**Elliptic units; CM elliptic curves (the BSD-relevant application).**
Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ have complex multiplication by the ring of integers of an imaginary quadratic field $K$, and let $p$ be a prime of good ordinary (or, with care, supersingular) reduction. The **Euler system of elliptic units** in the ray class fields of $K$ provides classes for $T = T_p(E)$ (more precisely for the relevant Hecke-character twist). The machine yields:

> **Theorem (Rubin, CM case; Chapter IX).** For such a CM curve, the elliptic-unit Euler system bounds the Selmer group, giving:
> (i) **one divisibility** in the two-variable main conjecture of Iwasawa theory for $K$ (the algebraic characteristic ideal divides the $p$-adic $L$-function), and
> (ii) when $L(E,1) \neq 0$ (analytic rank $0$): $E(\mathbb{Q})$ and $E(K)$ are finite and the $p$-part of $\operatorname{Sha}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite, with order bounded by the algebraic part of $L(E,1)/\Omega$ (the $p$-adic valuation matching the BSD prediction for the primes covered).

This is historically Rubin's 1991 *Inventiones* result ("The 'main conjectures' of Iwasawa theory for imaginary quadratic fields") repackaged inside the abstract framework. Combined with Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin and the deformation of these methods, it is part of why finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}$ is genuinely *known* (not assumed) for CM curves of analytic rank $0$.

**Appendices A and B.**
Appendix A collects the Galois-cohomology / local-duality input (Poitou-Tate, local Tate duality, the global Euler-characteristic formula) used to convert local conditions at the Kolyvagin primes into the global bound. Appendix B (with contributions on the structure of the relevant modules) handles the technical module theory ($\Lambda$-adic, the "$\tau$-element" hypothesis). These are the plumbing that makes the Bounding Theorem's index inequality exact.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN.** The abstract bounding theorem (Euler system with nonzero bottom class $\Rightarrow$ finite Selmer group, with an explicit index bound) is a theorem. The cyclotomic-unit instance (Mazur-Wiles main conjecture) is a theorem. The elliptic-unit instance for **CM** elliptic curves is a theorem: it gives *one divisibility* in the main conjecture and, in **analytic rank $0$**, the finiteness of $\operatorname{Sha}(E)[p^\infty]$ for the covered primes. None of this is conjectural in its stated regime.

2. **The GAP.** The theorem is conditional on having an Euler system with $c_{\mathbb{Q}} \neq 0$ *and* of the right core rank. For a general (non-CM) $E/\mathbb{Q}$ of analytic rank $\geq 2$, no such system is known: the Heegner system has bottom class $= 0$ once $L'(E/K,1) = 0$, and the elliptic-unit system does not even exist without CM. The machine is *fully built* but *starved of input* in the open regime. The gap is not in the abstraction; it is in the supply of starting classes.

3. **Exact regime.** Analytic rank $0$ for the unconditional Sha-finiteness statement (CM case via elliptic units); rank $\leq 1$ once paired with the Heegner-point system (Kolyvagin). **$p$-adic / algebraic**, not archimedean: the output is a bound on $\operatorname{Sel}/\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ and a divisibility of $p$-adic characteristic ideals. It does *not* deliver the archimedean leading term ($\Omega_E$, $\operatorname{Reg}_E$); bridging to that is Beilinson-Bloch-Kato, open. Rank $\geq 2$: the machine is silent.

4. **Three detectors.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES.** The Euler-system bound is a genuine cardinality bound on the Selmer group, not a mod-$2$ statement. It does not collapse to root-number parity. (It is *more* than parity, which is its strength; its limitation is elsewhere.)
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES, and is the canonical reference for it.** The method *proves* $\operatorname{Sha}[p^\infty]$ finite (it does not assume it), but *only* where a nonzero bottom class exists, i.e. analytic rank $0$ in the CM case (and rank $\leq 1$ once paired with the Heegner system). This note is the primary anchor showing the theorem's finiteness output is exactly co-extensive with the existence of the input class, so any rank-$\geq 2$ argument invoking "Euler systems bound Sha" must exhibit the missing starting class or it has silently assumed Detector 2.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): SHARP TEST CASE.** Over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ the analogous bound is cheap: finiteness of the Brauer group of the surface supplies the cohomology directly. The number-field machine has *no* geometric Frobenius to lean on; its Euler factors $P_\ell(\operatorname{Fr}_\ell^{-1})$ are an *arithmetic* substitute. A proposed rank-$\geq 2$ Euler system must produce its classes by an input existing over $\mathbb{Q}$, not by importing the geometry that trivializes the function-field case.

5. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This source sits exactly on the wall. Rank $0$ CM (elliptic units) and rank $\leq 1$ (Heegner, Kolyvagin) are the *proven* side; the abstract theorem makes precise that crossing to rank $\geq 2$ requires either a single Euler system of core rank $\geq 2$ or several independent systems, neither known. The control pair is therefore not just empirical: the book *characterizes* the obstruction as a missing higher-rank starting class.

6. **Which direction / experiment it feeds.** It is the **defining reference for Research Direction 02** ([`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md)): the precise norm relation $\operatorname{Cores}_{F'/F} c_{F'} = P_\ell(\operatorname{Fr}_\ell^{-1}) c_F$ that a candidate rank-$\geq 2$ system must satisfy is stated here, as is the core-rank notion that any such system must raise above $1$. It also feeds **Direction 01** ([`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md)) by fixing what "a Sha bound" must mean (a nonzero bottom class of higher core rank), and **Direction 03** ([`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md)) by marking that the output is $p$-adic and the archimedean bridge is the separate open problem.
