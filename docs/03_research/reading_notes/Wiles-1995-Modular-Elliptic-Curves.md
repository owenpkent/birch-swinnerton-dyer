# Reading note: A. Wiles, "Modular elliptic curves and Fermat's Last Theorem," Annals of Mathematics 142 (1995), 443-551.

## One-line takeaway

Wiles proves that every semistable elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ is modular (Theorem 0.4), by establishing a modularity *lifting* theorem (Theorems 0.2, 0.3) that identifies a universal deformation ring of a residual Galois representation with a Hecke algebra ($R = \mathbb{T}$); the corollary for BSD is that $L(E,s) = L(f,s)$ for a weight-2 newform $f$, so $L(E,s)$ has analytic continuation to all of $\mathbb{C}$ and a functional equation $s \leftrightarrow 2-s$ relating $\Lambda(E,s)$ to $\pm \Lambda(E,2-s)$, which makes $s=1$ a genuine center and the sign $w \in \{\pm 1\}$ the parity of the analytic rank. This is necessary infrastructure for BSD and gives exactly one bit (the rank mod 2), never the rank.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Introduction: the definition of modular and the immediate $L$-function consequence (p. 443).** An elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ is *modular* if it has a finite covering by a modular curve of the form $X_0(N)$. Wiles records at once the consequence that powers the entire BSD-infrastructure use of this paper: "Any such elliptic curve has the property that its Hasse-Weil zeta function has an analytic continuation and satisfies a functional equation of the standard type." The Shimura-Taniyama conjecture (attributed to Shimura and Taniyama in the 1950s-60s, popularized by Weil 1967) asserts every $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is modular; prior to this paper only finitely many $j$-invariants were known modular. Ribet's theorem (1986, via Serre's $\varepsilon$-conjecture) reduces FLT to modularity of *semistable* curves.

**The method: Galois representations and the $\rho_3$ choice (p. 444).** The approach studies $E$ via $\rho_p = $ the Galois action of $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ on the $p$-division points. The choice $p=3$ is critical: by Langlands-Tunnell, if $\rho_3$ is irreducible then it is modular (because $\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3) \cong S_4$). The strategy is then to show every suitable lifting of a modular $\rho_3$ is modular, reducing the problem "via novel arguments from commutative algebra, to a class number problem of a well-known type," solved with [TW] (Taylor-Wiles). This proves $E$ modular iff its associated 3-adic representation is modular. The two traditions linked are: (a) Galois representations $\leftrightarrow$ modular forms (Eichler-Shimura, Deligne, Langlands, Carayol), and (b) special values of $L$-functions in the style of the Dirichlet class number formula and Iwasawa theory (which Wiles "to a large extent" replaces). The restriction that $\rho_3$ be irreducible is bypassed by the "3-5 switch" using a family of curves sharing a common $\rho_5$.

**Theorem 0.1 (Eichler-Shimura for $k=2$, Deligne for $k>2$; p. 445).** For an eigenform $f$ on $\Gamma_1(N)$ of weight $k \geq 2$ and character $\chi$, with Hecke eigenvalues $c(n,f)$ (so $T_n f = c(n,f) f$), coefficient field $K_f$ and $\lambda \mid p$ a prime of $\mathcal{O}_f$, there is a continuous representation
$$\rho_{f,\lambda} : \operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \longrightarrow \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathcal{O}_{f,\lambda}),$$
unramified outside primes dividing $Np$, with
$$\operatorname{trace} \rho_{f,\lambda}(\operatorname{Frob} q) = c(q,f), \qquad \det \rho_{f,\lambda}(\operatorname{Frob} q) = \chi(q) q^{k-1} \quad \text{for } q \nmid Np.$$
This is the *forward* direction (modular form $\Rightarrow$ Galois representation). The paper's task is the converse: criteria under which a $\lambda$-adic representation arises from a modular form.

**The residual problem and the Fontaine-Mazur-Serre conjecture (pp. 445-446).** Let $\rho_0 : \operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{GL}_2(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ be continuous with $\det \rho_0$ odd. Call $\rho_0$ *modular* if $\rho_0 \cong \rho_{f,\lambda} \bmod \lambda$ for some $f, \lambda$. Serre conjectured every irreducible odd $\rho_0$ is modular (then known only for dihedral, $A_4$, $S_4$ image, the last by Langlands-Tunnell). For a lifting $\rho$ of $\rho_0$ to the integers $\mathcal{O}$ of a local field, two local conditions at $p$ are isolated: (I) $\rho_0$ *ordinary* (a stable line on which inertia acts by the right character) and (II) $\rho_0$ *flat* (arising from a finite flat group scheme over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, with $\det$ on inertia the cyclotomic character $\varepsilon$). The **Conjecture** (a form of Fontaine-Mazur extending Serre, p. 446): an irreducible lifting $\rho$ of a modular $\rho_0$, unramified outside a finite set, with $\det \rho = \varepsilon^{k-1}\chi$ ($k \geq 2$) in the ordinary case (or flat in the flat case, $p$ odd), comes from a modular form. This is the target Wiles proves in restricted cases.

**Theorem 0.2 (the modularity lifting theorem; p. 447).** Suppose $\rho_0$ is irreducible, satisfies (I) or (II), is *modular*, and satisfies two technical conditions: (i) $\rho_0$ is absolutely irreducible when restricted to $\mathbb{Q}\big(\sqrt{(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\,p}\big)$, and (ii) a ramification condition at primes $q \equiv -1 \bmod p$. Then any $\rho$ as in the conjecture *does* come from a modular form. Wiles flags the essential hypothesis explicitly: "The only condition which really seems essential to our method is the requirement that $\rho_0$ be modular." Everything else is technical.

**Theorem 0.3 (modularity criterion for $E$ via 3-division points; p. 447).** Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and let $\rho_0$ be the Galois action on the 3-division points. If (i) $E$ has good or multiplicative reduction at 3, (ii) $\rho_0$ is absolutely irreducible restricted to $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, and (iii) a condition at $q \equiv -1 \bmod 3$, then $E$ is modular. (Here $\rho_0$ is automatically modular by Langlands-Tunnell since $\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3) \cong S_4$.) Wiles notes (p. 447-448) that the *stronger* statement "$E$ is covered by $X_0(N)$" also needs Faltings' isogeny theorem.

**Theorem 0.4 (the main modularity theorem; p. 448).** *Suppose that $E$ is a semistable elliptic curve defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then $E$ is modular.* Semistable means square-free conductor. Such curves satisfy (i) and (iii) of Theorem 0.3 but not always (ii); when (ii) fails, $\rho_0$ is reducible, and Theorem 0.2 is applied instead with $p=5$ via the 5-division points of an auxiliary curve (the "3-5 switch," Chapter 5, the only place deformations of $E$ rather than of $\rho$ are used).

**Theorem 0.5 (Fermat's Last Theorem; p. 448).** *Suppose $u^p + v^p + w^p = 0$ with $u,v,w \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $p \geq 3$. Then $uvw = 0$.* This is deduced from Theorem 0.4 via Frey-Serre-Ribet: a nontrivial solution would yield a semistable Frey curve $y^2 = x(x+u^p)(x-v^p)$ whose mod-$p$ representation, if modular, would attach to a (nonexistent) weight-2 conductor-2 form.

**Theorem 0.6 (a second lifting theorem, CM/dihedral case; p. 449).** For $\rho_0 = \operatorname{Ind}_L^{\mathbb{Q}} \kappa_0$ induced from a character of an imaginary quadratic $L$ (unramified at $p$), with $\det \rho_0|_{I_p} = \omega$, a representation $\rho$ as in the conjecture comes from a modular form. This case does *not* assume $\rho_0$ modular (it is already known there). It powers Chapter 4.

**The proof mechanism: $R = \mathbb{T}$ and the numerical criterion (pp. 449-454).** The translation into commutative algebra is due to Mazur's deformation theory: the universal deformation ring $R$ of $\rho_0$ (with prescribed local conditions $\Sigma$) should equal a Hecke ring $\mathbb{T}$. Wiles' breakthrough (spring 1991) was a numerical criterion using two invariants introduced in the Appendix: the $\eta$-invariant (testing isomorphism of Gorenstein rings, after Kunz/Tate-Grothendieck duality) and the $\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^2$-invariant (testing complete intersection). Equality of these forces a Gorenstein ring to be a complete intersection, and lets one prove $R = \mathbb{T}$ by counting generators and relations. A calculation of Hida converts the main problem into a class-number bound of Iwasawa type. The Selmer-group estimate (Chapter 3) replaces the standard cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension by an auxiliary-prime method using primes $q_i \equiv 1 \bmod p^{n_i}$ with $n_i \to \infty$, glued by Poitou-Tate duality. The Euler-system approach (extending Flach's construction, in the Kolyvagin tradition) was set aside in the fall of 1993 when Wiles became convinced it was incomplete and possibly flawed; by his own account in the acknowledgments, the critical questions of Katz (who, together with Illusie, read the Euler-system argument) led him to discover the problem. The repair (the Taylor-Wiles patching argument, reached in August/September 1994) is in the companion paper [TW]. Table of Contents (p. 454): Ch.1 Deformations of Galois representations; Ch.2 The Gorenstein property / Congruences between Hecke rings / The main conjectures; Ch.3 Estimates for the Selmer group; Ch.4 The ordinary CM case / Calculation of $\eta$; Ch.5 Application to elliptic curves; Appendix; References.

**Chapter 4 sampling (the ordinary CM case; printed pp. 535-538).** This chapter does the concrete $\eta$-computation in the dihedral case. It uses Hida's formula $\pi(\eta^2) = \langle f,f\rangle^2 / u_f \bar{u}_f \times (\text{unit})$ (Proposition 4.4, p. 535), relates the period invariant $u_f$ to $\Omega^2$ (Proposition 4.5, p. 538: $u_f = \tfrac{1}{4}\Omega^2 \cdot (1/p\text{-adic integer})$, so $u_f$ is the complex period squared up to a $p$-adic unit), and gives a Petersson-inner-product formula in terms of $L$-values (Proposition 4.6, p. 538): for $f = f_\varphi$ attached to a grossencharacter $\varphi$ on the imaginary quadratic $L$, $\langle f_\varphi, f_\varphi \rangle$ is a finite-Euler-product times a product of two Dirichlet/Hecke $L$-values, schematically
$$\langle f_\varphi, f_\varphi \rangle = (\text{prefactor})\cdot N^2 \Big\{\prod_{q \mid N,\; q \notin S_\varphi}\big(1 - \tfrac{1}{q}\big)\Big\}\, L_N(2, \varphi^2 \bar{\chi})\, L_N(1, \psi),$$
where $L_N$ denotes the $L$-function with Euler factors at $q \mid N$ removed. (The exact numerical prefactor, a small power of $2$ and $\pi$, is not legible in the scan and is omitted rather than guessed.)
The point for us: even in the place where $L$-values appear most explicitly, they appear as *periods and congruence numbers* feeding the $R = \mathbb{T}$ identity, not as a statement about $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(E,s)$ or about rational points. The paper never touches the rank.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN here vs the GAP.** PROVEN: every semistable $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is modular (Theorem 0.4), hence $L(E,s) = L(f,s)$ for a weight-2 newform $f$ on $\Gamma_0(N)$, hence $L(E,s)$ continues to an entire function with a functional equation $\Lambda(E,s) = w\,\Lambda(E,2-s)$, $w \in \{\pm 1\}$. The full conjecture (all $E/\mathbb{Q}$) is completed by Breuil-Conrad-Diamond-Taylor (2001), a separate source. The GAP relative to BSD: this gives only that $s=1$ is a defined center and that $w = (-1)^{\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)}$. It says nothing about the value of $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$ beyond its parity, and nothing about $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})$.

2. **EXACT regime.** Archimedean (it is about the complex-analytic $L(E,s)$ and its functional equation), and regime-agnostic in rank: modularity holds for *all* semistable $E/\mathbb{Q}$ regardless of rank. But its BSD payload is confined to the parity bit. It does not distinguish analytic rank 0 from 2, nor 1 from 3; it only fixes them mod 2. So with respect to the rank-$\leq 1$ (proven) vs rank-$\geq 2$ (open) control pair, modularity is *upstream of the wall*: it is equally available on both sides and equally silent about the rank on both sides.

3. **Three detectors.**
   - **Detector 1, PARITY-ONLY: TRIGGERED, and this note is its origin.** Modularity's entire BSD-relevant output is $w$, the sign of the functional equation, which gives the analytic rank only mod 2. This is precisely the failure mode `root_number_parity` in `controls.py` flags: a conclusion that uses only $w$ cannot prove the full rank equality. Any candidate proof whose new content reduces to "compute $w$" is modularity-level and is caught here.
   - **Detector 2, SHA-FINITENESS ASSUMED: PASSES (not engaged).** The paper makes no claim about $\operatorname{Sha}(E)$ and does not assume its finiteness. It is clean on this axis because it never reaches the strong-BSD formula.
   - **Detector 3, FUNCTION-FIELD MIRAGE: PASSES (not engaged).** The work is intrinsically about $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$-representations; it imports no geometric Frobenius. Indeed Wiles notes (p. 446) that Drinfeld's function-field observation *inspired* the idea of a geometric local condition at $p$, but the number-field machinery (Langlands-Tunnell, Poitou-Tate duality, deformation rings) is doing genuine arithmetic work, not borrowing a function-field theorem.

4. **Control pair.** Modularity does *not* do anything new in the rank-$\geq 2$ regime: it is the shared foundation that both the proven (rank $\leq 1$) and open (rank $\geq 2$) regimes already enjoy. The rank-boundary thesis of the atlas is unaffected by it. This is the cleanest illustration of the project's central discipline: a true, deep, hard-won theorem can be entirely orthogonal to the BSD difficulty, because the difficulty lives in constructing rank-$\geq 2$ rational points or bounding Sha unconditionally, neither of which modularity touches.

5. **What it feeds.** It feeds *everything downstream as infrastructure*: Gross-Zagier + Kolyvagin (`gross_zagier_kolyvagin.md`) needs $E$ modular to even speak of $L(E/K,s)$ and Heegner points; the Iwasawa thread (`iwasawa_skinner_urban.md`) needs the modular form $f$ to form $p$-adic $L$-functions. For the research directions specifically: it sets the *floor* that Direction 01 ([`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md)) must exceed (the rank-2 object must produce two independent points, which the parity bit can never witness), and it provides the analytically-continued $L(E,s)$ whose archimedean leading term Direction 03 ([`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md)) seeks to connect to the $p$-adic side. Experimentally it underwrites the root-number / parity controls in `experiments/_shared/controls.py` (Detector 1) and the analytic-rank computation in `experiments/l_function_rank/`.
