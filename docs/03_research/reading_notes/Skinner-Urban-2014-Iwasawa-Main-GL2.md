# Reading note: C. Skinner, E. Urban, "The Iwasawa main conjectures for $\mathrm{GL}_2$," Inventiones Mathematicae 195 (2014), 1-277.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the *reverse divisibility* half of the cyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture for an ordinary elliptic curve / modular form. Kato (2004) proved one inclusion: the $p$-adic $L$-function lies in the characteristic ideal of the dual Selmer group ($\mathcal{L}_f \in Ch(f)$, the analytic side bounds the algebraic side). Skinner-Urban prove the opposite inclusion ($Ch(f) \subseteq (\mathcal{L}_f)$) by constructing Eisenstein congruences on the unitary group $GU(2,2)$. Combining the two gives the *equality* $Ch_{\mathbf{Q}_\infty}(f) = (\mathcal{L}_f)$ in many cases, and Greenberg's control theorem then converts the equality into the **$p$-part of the BSD leading-term formula** in analytic rank $0$, plus a Selmer-corank statement that touches analytic rank $1$. It is the cleanest unconditional statement we have about $v_p(\#\mathrm{Sha})$.

## One-line takeaway

For an ordinary $p$ and a modular form / elliptic curve satisfying explicit irreducibility and ramification hypotheses, Skinner-Urban supply the Eisenstein-congruence divisibility that, together with Kato's Euler-system divisibility, proves the cyclotomic Iwasawa-Greenberg main conjecture as an *equality* of ideals in $\Lambda \otimes \mathbf{Q}_p$; Greenberg's machinery then yields the exact $p$-adic valuation of the BSD leading term in analytic rank $0$ (the $p$-part of $\#\mathrm{Sha} \cdot \prod c_\ell$) and a Selmer-corank lower bound when $L(E,1)=0$. This is a theorem about $v_p(\#\mathrm{Sha})$ under hypotheses, not about the archimedean leading term and not about rank $\geq 2$.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Section 1.1 (Introduction): the Iwasawa-Greenberg main conjecture for $f$.**
Fix an odd prime $p$ and embeddings $\overline{\mathbf{Q}} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{C}$, $\overline{\mathbf{Q}} \hookrightarrow \overline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$. Let $\mathbf{Q}_\infty$ be the cyclotomic $\mathbf{Z}_p$-extension, $\Gamma_{\mathbf{Q}} = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf{Q}_\infty/\mathbf{Q})$, $\gamma$ the topological generator identified with $1+p$. Let $f \in S_k(N,\chi)$ be a weight-$k$ newform ($k \geq 2$) that is *ordinary* at $p$ ($a(p,f)$ a $p$-adic unit), with coefficients in a finite extension $\mathbf{Q}(f)$, and let $L$ be a finite extension of $\mathbf{Q}_p$ with ring of integers $O_L$, $\Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L} := O_L[[\Gamma_{\mathbf{Q}}]]$.

*The $p$-adic $L$-function.* By Amice-Velu / Vishik / Mazur-Tate-Teitelbaum there is a power series $\mathcal{L}_f \in \Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L}$ interpolating critical $L$-values. For a continuous $O_L$-homomorphism $\phi:\Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L} \to \overline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$ with $\phi(\gamma) = \zeta(1+p)^m$ ($\zeta$ a primitive $p^{t_\phi - 1}$th root of unity, $0 \le m \le k-2$),
$$
\mathcal{L}_f(\phi) \;=\; e(\phi)\,\frac{p^{t'_\phi(m+1)}\,m!\,L(f,\chi_\phi^{-1}\omega^{-m},m+1)}{(-2\pi i)^m\,G(\chi_\phi^{-1}\omega^{-m})\,\Omega_f^{\mathrm{sgn}((-1)^m)}},
$$
with $\chi_\phi$ a finite-order character, $G(\cdot)$ a Gauss sum, $\Omega_f^{\pm}$ the canonical periods, and $e(\phi)$ an explicit interpolation (Euler-factor) factor. This is the *analytic* object.

*The Selmer group.* Since $f$ is ordinary there is a unique $G_p$-stable unramified line $V_f^+ \subset V_f$ in the $2$-dimensional Galois representation $\rho_f$. Choosing a stable $O_L$-lattice $T_f$ and twisting by $\det \rho_f^{-1}$ to get $T = T_f(\det\rho_f^{-1})$, $T^+ = T_f^+(\det\rho_f^{-1})$, they define
$$
Sel_{\mathbf{Q}_\infty,L}(f) := \ker\Big\{ H^1(\mathbf{Q}_\infty, T\otimes \mathbf{Q}_p/\mathbf{Z}_p) \to H^1(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty,p}, T/T^+ \otimes \mathbf{Q}_p/\mathbf{Z}_p)\Big\},
$$
unramified away from $p$. Its Pontryagin dual $X_{\mathbf{Q}_\infty,L}(f)$ is a finitely generated $\Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L}$-module, with characteristic ideal $Ch_{\mathbf{Q}_\infty,L}(f)$. The dependence on the lattice is only through the valuation at the prime over $p$, so $Ch$ is well-defined in $\Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L} \otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_p} \mathbf{Q}_p$; if $\bar\rho_f$ is residually irreducible it is well-defined already in $\Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L}$ (lattice-independent).

*The conjecture (statement, not the theorem).* The Iwasawa-Greenberg main conjecture for $f$ asserts the equality of ideals
$$
Ch_{\mathbf{Q}_\infty}(f) = (\mathcal{L}_f) \quad \text{in } \Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_p}\mathbf{Q}_p,
$$
and in $\Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L}$ if $\bar\rho_f$ is residually irreducible. Kato [Ka04, Thm 17.4] proved $\mathcal{L}_f \in Ch_{\mathbf{Q}_\infty,L}(f)$ (one divisibility) under hypotheses. The paper proves the reverse.

**Theorem 1 (= Theorem 3.6.4): the main conjecture for $f$, completed.** Suppose:
- $\chi = 1$ and $k \equiv 2 \bmod p-1$;
- the reduction $\bar\rho_f$ modulo the maximal ideal of $O_L$ is irreducible;
- there exists a prime $q \neq p$ with $q \,\|\, N$ and $\bar\rho_f$ ramified at $q$;
- $p \nmid N$.

Then $Ch_{\mathbf{Q}_\infty,L}(f) = (\mathcal{L}_f)$ in $\Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_p}\mathbf{Q}_p$. If furthermore there is an $O_L$-basis of $T_f$ for which $\mathrm{im}(\rho_f) \supseteq \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf{Z}_p)$, then the equality holds in $\Lambda_{\mathbf{Q},O_L}$ itself (the integral statement). This is *deduced* by combining Kato's divisibility with the paper's own main divisibility (Theorem 3, below).

**Section 1.2 / Theorem 2 (= Theorem 3.6.11): application to elliptic curves and the $p$-part of BSD.** Let $E/\mathbf{Q}$ have conductor $N_E$. Suppose:
- $E$ has good *ordinary* reduction at $p$;
- $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ (the mod-$p$ representation $G_{\mathbf{Q}} \to \mathrm{Aut}(E[p])$) is irreducible;
- there exists a prime $q \neq p$ with $q \,\|\, N_E$ and $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ ramified at $q$.

Then:

(a) If $L(E,1) \neq 0$ (analytic rank $0$) and $\bar\rho_{E,p}$ is *surjective*, then
$$
\left| \frac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_E} \right|_p^{-1} \;=\; \#\mathrm{III}(E/\mathbf{Q})_p \cdot \prod_{\ell \mid N_E} c_\ell(E),
$$
where $|\cdot|_p$ is the $p$-adic absolute value, $\mathrm{III}(E/\mathbf{Q})_p$ is the $p$-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group, and $c_\ell(E) := |\#E(\mathbf{Q}_\ell)/E_0(\mathbf{Q}_\ell)|_p^{-1}$ is the $p$-part of the Tamagawa number at $\ell$. This is exactly the **$p$-part of the BSD leading-term formula in rank $0$**: the predicted $p$-adic valuation of $L(E,1)/\Omega_E$ matches $v_p(\#\mathrm{III}) + \sum v_p(c_\ell)$.

(b) If $L(E,1) = 0$, then the corank of the $p^\infty$-Selmer group $Sel_{p^\infty}(E/\mathbf{Q})$ is *at least one*.

The hypotheses of Theorem 2 are satisfied, e.g., whenever $E$ is *semistable* and $p \geq 11$ is a prime of good ordinary reduction.

*What part (a) is and is not.* Part (a) is the rank-$0$ strong-BSD identity localized at one prime $p$. It pins the exact $p$-adic valuation of $\#\mathrm{III}$ (its $p$-part) under the stated hypotheses. It says nothing about the archimedean period $\Omega_E$ as a transcendental quantity, nothing about a regulator (there is no regulator in rank $0$), and nothing for $\ell \neq p$ beyond the explicit Tamagawa factors. It is the strongest known *unconditional* control of $v_p(\#\mathrm{III})$, and it is the rank-$0$ statement.

*What part (b) is and is not.* Part (b) is a one-sided Selmer-corank inequality at analytic rank $1$. It says: analytic vanishing forces algebraic corank $\geq 1$. It does **not** prove $\mathrm{rank}\,E(\mathbf{Q}) = 1$ on its own; the full rank-$1$ result is Gross-Zagier + Kolyvagin (a different machine). Read carefully, (b) is a "half" of rank $1$ obtained from the $p$-adic main conjecture, complementary to the Heegner-point input.

**Section 1.3 (the nature of the proof) and Theorem 3 (= Theorem 3.6.1): the reverse divisibility.** Following Wiles' strategy for the main conjecture over totally real fields (congruences between Eisenstein and cusp families, rather than an Euler system), the paper proves the inclusion *opposite* to Kato's. Let $\mathbf{f}$ be an $\mathbb{I}$-adic ordinary eigenform (Hida family) of tame level $N$ and trivial character, $L \supseteq \mathbf{Q}[\boldsymbol\mu_{Np}, i, D_{\mathcal{K}}^{1/2}]$. Let $\mathcal{K}$ be an imaginary quadratic field in which $p$ splits, and factor $N = N^+ N^-$ with $N^+$ divisible only by primes split in $\mathcal{K}$ and $N^-$ by primes inert in $\mathcal{K}$. Assume:
- **(irred)$_{\mathbf{f}}$**: $\bar\rho_{\mathbf{f}}$ is irreducible;
- **(dist)$_{\mathbf{f}}$**: $\bar\rho_{\mathbf{f}}$ is $G_p$-distinguished (its restriction to a decomposition group at $p$ is a sum of two *distinct* characters; this yields the unramified rank-one summand $T_{\mathbf{f}}^+$);
- $N^-$ is square-free with an *odd* number of prime factors;
- $\bar\rho_{\mathbf{f}}$ is ramified at every $\ell \mid N^-$.

Let $\Sigma$ be a finite set of primes containing those dividing $N D_{\mathcal{K}}$ and some prime $\ell \neq p$ split in $\mathcal{K}$. Then
$$
Ch^{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{K}_\infty}(\mathbf{f}) \;\subseteq\; \big(\mathcal{L}^{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{f},\mathcal{K}}\big) \quad \text{in } \mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{K}} = \mathbb{I}[[\Gamma_{\mathcal{K}}]],
$$
where $\mathcal{K}_\infty$ is the composite of the two $\mathbf{Z}_p$-extensions of $\mathcal{K}$ (so $\Gamma_{\mathcal{K}} \cong \mathbf{Z}_p^2$), $Ch^{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{K}_\infty}(\mathbf{f})$ is the characteristic ideal of the dual of a $\Sigma$-imprimitive Selmer group $Sel^{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{K}_\infty}(\mathbf{f})$, and $\mathcal{L}^{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{f},\mathcal{K}}$ is a **three-variable $p$-adic $L$-function** (one Hida-family variable, two cyclotomic/anticyclotomic variables of $\mathcal{K}$). Theorem 1 / Theorem 2 are *deduced* from Theorem 3 by descending the three-variable inclusion over $\mathcal{K}$ to the one-variable cyclotomic statement over $\mathbf{Q}$ and combining with Kato. The inclusion of Theorem 3 is upgraded to equality in many cases in Theorem 3.6.6.

**Sections 1.4, 5-13 (the proof architecture: Eisenstein congruences on $GU(2,2)$).** The mechanism: work with automorphic forms on $G := GU(2,2)_{/\mathbf{Q}}$, the unitary similitude group of a signature-$(2,2)$ Hermitian pairing over $\mathcal{K}$. The maximal $\mathbf{Q}$-parabolic $P = MN$ has Levi $M \cong GU(1,1) \times \mathrm{Res}_{\mathcal{K}/\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{G}_m$. Eisenstein series induced from cusp forms on $M(\mathbf{A})$ associated to the Hida family $\mathbf{f}$ have *constant terms* along $P$ that involve $L$-values $L^{\Sigma}(BC(\pi)\otimes\xi, s)$ of the base change of $\pi$ to $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{K}})$, and *singular Fourier coefficients* that are simple multiples of products of normalized $L$-values
$$
\mathfrak{L} := L^{\Sigma}(f,\xi,k-1)\,L^{\Sigma}(\chi^{-1}\xi',k-2)/\Omega.
$$
The strategy (generalizing Ribet's converse to Herbrand and Wiles' totally-real proof): if $\mathfrak{L}$ is *not* a $p$-adic unit while some non-singular Fourier coefficient *is* a unit, then the Eisenstein series is congruent mod $\mathfrak{L}$ to a *cusp form*; the Galois representation attached to that cusp form then produces a non-trivial class in the Selmer group, forcing $\mathfrak{L} \mid Ch$. Carrying this out in a $p$-adic *family* (replacing $\mathfrak{L}$ by the three-variable $p$-adic $L$-function) gives Theorem 3.

The proof has two halves:
1. *Abstract / Hida-theoretic (Sections 2-7).* Section 3 develops the Selmer-group sorites and the precise relations $Sel^{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{K}_\infty}(\mathbf{f}) \leftrightarrow Sel$ over $\mathbf{Q}$ used to deduce Theorems 1-2. **Section 4** is the abstract "cocycle construction": given a complete local DVR $A$ with fraction field $F$, a reduced finite flat $A$-algebra $R$, representations $\rho$ ($n$-dimensional, residually irreducible) and $\sigma$ ($m = n+2$-dimensional, "Eisenstein-type", with $\mathrm{tr}\,\sigma \equiv \chi' + \mathrm{tr}\,\rho + \chi \bmod I$ for a congruence ideal $I$), it manufactures extensions of Galois representations and hence subgroups of cocycle classes whose order is bounded below by the order of the congruence ideal $I$ (the lattice lemmas 4.3.5-4.3.11, the pairing $\Phi$ and its reduction mod $I$, the exact sequences 4.4.1.a-c). This is the engine that turns "Eisenstein congruence of order $n$" into "characteristic-ideal divisibility of order $\geq n$." Sections 5-6 build Hida theory for $GU(2,2)$ (Igusa tower, ordinary $\Lambda$-adic forms, the universal ordinary Hecke algebra, and the *Eisenstein ideal* $I_{\mathbf{D}} \subseteq h_{\mathbf{D}}$). Section 7 supplies the Galois representations attached to cuspidal automorphic representations of $G$ and to the Hida families, and assembles Sections 4-6 into the proof.
2. *Automorphic / analytic (Sections 8-13).* Construct a $p$-adic family $\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{D}}$ of Eisenstein series on $GU(2,2)$ whose singular Fourier coefficients are divisible by the three-variable $p$-adic $L$-function $\mathcal{L}^{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{f},\mathcal{K}}$. Sections 9-11 compute Fourier-Jacobi expansions via the Garrett-Shimura pull-back formula from a Siegel-Eisenstein series on $GU(3,3)$, realizing the relevant $L$-values as inner products $\langle \mathbf{f}_\phi, \text{(theta lift)}\rangle$. Sections 12-13 perform the $p$-adic interpolation and prove the crucial *co-primality*: the non-singular Fourier coefficients of $\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{D}}$ are *prime to* $\mathcal{L}^{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{f},\mathcal{K}}$ (so the congruence is genuinely "Eisenstein $\equiv$ cusp" and not vacuous). The co-primality rests on mod-$p$ non-vanishing results of Vatsal and Finis and on the vanishing of an anticyclotomic $\mu$-invariant.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN vs the GAP.** PROVEN (under explicit hypotheses): the cyclotomic main conjecture *equality* $Ch(f) = (\mathcal{L}_f)$ for ordinary $f$ (Theorem 1), and from it the exact $p$-adic valuation $v_p(\#\mathrm{III}(E/\mathbf{Q}))$ via the rank-$0$ $p$-part formula (Theorem 2(a)), plus a Selmer-corank $\geq 1$ statement when $L(E,1) = 0$ (Theorem 2(b)). The GAP: this controls one prime $p$ at a time, in the *ordinary* case, under irreducibility/ramification hypotheses. It does **not** give global finiteness of $\mathrm{III}$ (that would need all $p$ simultaneously plus a bound on the contributing primes), it does **not** give the archimedean leading term, and it does **not** reach analytic rank $\geq 2$ in any form.

2. **EXACT regime.** $p$-adic, *ordinary* $p$. Theorem 2(a) is **analytic rank $0$** ($L(E,1)\neq 0$). Theorem 2(b) is a one-sided statement *at* **analytic rank $1$** ($L(E,1) = 0 \Rightarrow$ Selmer corank $\geq 1$), and it is only a lower bound on corank, not the rank equality. There is **no** statement at analytic rank $\geq 2$. The leading term controlled is the $p$-adic valuation of $L(E,1)/\Omega_E$, i.e., the $p$-part of $\#\mathrm{III}\cdot\prod c_\ell$. The transcendental archimedean term ($\Omega_E$, regulators) is untouched.

3. **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES.** This is not a parity method. It produces the exact $p$-adic valuation of the leading term (an honest order, mod $p$-units), far finer than rank mod $2$. It is the kind of result a parity method structurally cannot give. No trigger.

4. **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): TRIGGERS, but honestly and in the proven regime.** The result *proves* (does not assume) finiteness of $\mathrm{III}(E/\mathbf{Q})_p$ and computes its order, but only where the main conjecture is unconditional: analytic rank $0$ (Theorem 2(a)), ordinary $p$, under the hypotheses. Any *use* of this to claim global $\#\mathrm{III} < \infty$ or to feed a rank-$\geq 2$ argument would be illegitimate: the theorem says nothing there. Flag: the $p$-part finiteness is a theorem here precisely and only in the rank-$0$ unconditional window. The archimedean / rank-$\geq 2$ finiteness of $\mathrm{III}$ stays **open**.

5. **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): PASSES.** This is genuinely a number-field argument. The "geometry" it imports is the $GU(2,2)$ Shimura variety and its Hida theory over $\mathbf{Q}$, not a geometric Frobenius over $\mathbf{F}_q(C)$. There is no smuggled function-field input; the difficulty (constructing the Eisenstein congruence and proving co-primality of Fourier coefficients) is exactly the number-field difficulty. No trigger.

6. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This source sits entirely on the *proven* side of the wall and reinforces where the wall is. Theorem 2(a) is rank $0$; Theorem 2(b) is a $p$-adic "half" of rank $1$ complementary to Heegner points. Nothing in the $GU(2,2)$ machine reaches across to rank $\geq 2$, because the Eisenstein series on $GU(2,2)$ is built from the *one* Hida family $\mathbf{f}$ and its base change, and the resulting Selmer class is one-dimensional in the relevant sense. The analytic-rank-$\geq 2$ archimedean statement is entirely outside its reach.

7. **Which research direction / experiment it feeds.** Primarily Research Direction 03, [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md): this note fixes exactly which factor of the BSD formula the $p$-adic theory delivers ($v_p(\#\mathrm{III})$ and the $p$-adic valuation of the leading term in rank $0$) and which it does not ($\Omega_E$, $\mathrm{Reg}_E$, the archimedean transcendence). For Direction 01, [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md), it is a constraint: any rank-$2$ object must produce a *two-dimensional* Selmer/cohomology phenomenon that the $GU(2,2)$ Eisenstein congruence, tied to a single family $\mathbf{f}$, structurally does not. For Direction 02, [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md), the relevant lesson is that the "reverse divisibility" here replaces the Euler system with Eisenstein congruences; a higher-rank Euler system would be the alternative route, and this paper shows the congruence route also caps at the rank where the $p$-adic $L$-function's order is controlled. On the experimental side it informs the strong-BSD-quantities thread ([`experiments/strong_bsd_quantities/`](../../../experiments/strong_bsd_quantities/)): the $p$-part identity in Theorem 2(a) is a concrete numerical check one can run on rank-$0$ curves (compare $v_p(L(E,1)/\Omega_E)$ against $v_p(\#\mathrm{III}\cdot\prod c_\ell)$ for ordinary $p$).
