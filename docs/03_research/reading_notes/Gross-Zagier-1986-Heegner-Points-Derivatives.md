# Reading note: B. Gross, D. Zagier, "Heegner points and derivatives of L-series," Inventiones Mathematicae 84 (1986), 225-320.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the analytic-rank-1, archimedean half of the only fully proven regime of BSD. It proves the height formula $L'(f,\chi,1) = \tfrac{8\pi^2\,(f,f)}{u^2\sqrt{|D|}}\,\hat{h}(c_{\chi,f})$ (Theorem (6.3); $(f,f)$ the Petersson norm), equivalently $L'(E/K,1) = c\cdot\hat{h}(y_K)$ with $c>0$, so a Heegner point is non-torsion exactly when the first derivative of the $L$-series over $K$ is nonzero. It supplies ONE point of infinite order in $E(\mathbb{Q})$ when $L(E,1)=0$ and $L'(E,1)\neq 0$. Kolyvagin (a separate note) later converts that one point into the rank equality and finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ in rank $\leq 1$. Gross-Zagier alone does not bound $\mathrm{Sha}$ and does not prove the full rank statement; it produces the point and the height identity.

## One-line takeaway

For a newform $f$ of weight 2 and level $N$, an imaginary quadratic field $K$ of discriminant $D$ coprime to $N$ satisfying the Heegner hypothesis (every prime dividing $N$ splits in $K$), and the Heegner divisor class $c=(x)-(\infty)$ on $\mathrm{Jac}(X_0(N))$, Gross and Zagier prove the exact identity $L'(f,\chi,1)=\tfrac{8\pi^2\,(f,f)}{u^2\,|D|^{1/2}}\,\hat{h}(c_{\chi,f})$ (with $(f,f)$ the Petersson norm) relating the first derivative at the center $s=1$ of the Rankin-Selberg $L$-series to the Neron-Tate height of the $\chi$-eigencomponent of the Heegner point. The proof matches two independently computed cusp forms of weight 2: the generating series of global height pairings $\sum_m \langle c, T_m c^\sigma\rangle q^m$, computed place-by-place as a sum of Neron local symbols (archimedean via a Green's function on $X_0(N)(\mathbb{C})$ summed over CM points, non-archimedean via intersection theory on an arithmetic surface), against the holomorphic projection of the derivative of the Rankin-Selberg kernel. Because the construction outputs a single divisor class and the formula reads off only the first-order term of $L$ at $s=1$, it distinguishes analytic rank 0 (Heegner point torsion) from analytic rank 1 (Heegner point non-torsion) and is structurally silent at rank $\geq 2$, where $L'(E/K,1)=0$ tells it nothing.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Chapter I, sections 1-3: the modular curve, correspondences, Heegner points (pp. 226-227).** $X=X_0(N)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ parametrizes cyclic $N$-isogenies $\phi:E\to E'$. Its Jacobian $J$ carries the Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(J)$ generated by the self-dual correspondences $T_m=\sum_{C}(x_C)$ (sum over order-$m$ subgroups meeting $\ker\phi$ trivially), of bidegree $\sigma_1(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}d$ when $(m,N)=1$. A point $x=(E\to E')$ is a **Heegner point of discriminant $D$** if both $E$ and $E'$ have CM by the maximal order $\mathcal{O}$ of $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})$. Such points exist iff $D$ is congruent to a square mod $4N$; there are then $2^s\cdot h$ of them (with $s$ the number of prime factors of $N$ and $h=h(D)$ the class number), all rational over the Hilbert class field $H=K(j(E))$, permuted simply transitively by $W\times\mathrm{Gal}(H/K)$ where $W\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^s$ is the Atkin-Lehner group. The paper assumes throughout that $D$ is odd, hence squarefree and $\equiv 1\pmod 4$. The **Heegner hypothesis** in coordinate form (Chapter II.1, p. 235): $N=\prod p_i^{r_i}$ with every $p_i$ split in $K$, equivalently $\varepsilon(p)=1$ for all $p\mid N$, so that $N$ is the norm of an integral ideal $\mathfrak{n}$ with $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{n}\cong\mathbb{Z}/N$.

**Chapter I, section 4: local and global heights (p. 228).** Neron's local symbol $\langle a,b\rangle_v\in\mathbb{R}$ for coprime degree-zero divisors is bi-additive, symmetric, continuous, and equals $\log|f(a)|_v$ when $b=\mathrm{div}(f)$. The global pairing $\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_v\langle a,b\rangle_v$ over places of $H$ descends to $J(H)\otimes\mathbb{C}$, and $\hat{h}(a)=\langle a,a\rangle$ is the canonical Neron-Tate height, a positive-definite quadratic form on $J(H)\otimes\mathbb{R}$ (extended Hermitian to $J(H)\otimes\mathbb{C}$). This positive-definiteness is what forces the sign in the corollaries below.

**Chapter I, section 5: the L-series (p. 229).** For a newform $f=\sum a_n q^n$ of weight 2 on $\Gamma_0(N)$ and an ideal class $\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{Cl}_K$ with theta-series $\theta_{\mathcal{A}}$ (weight 1 on $\Gamma_1(D)$), the Rankin-Selberg convolution is
$$L_{\mathcal{A}}(f,s)=\Big(\sum_{(n,DN)=1}\varepsilon(n)\,n^{1-2s}\Big)\cdot\sum_{n\geq 1}a_n r_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\,n^{-s},$$
and for a character $\chi$ of $\mathrm{Cl}_K$, $L(f,\chi,s)=\sum_{\mathcal{A}}\chi(\mathcal{A})L_{\mathcal{A}}(f,s)$. **Proposition (5.5):** by Rankin's method both have analytic continuation to all of $\mathbb{C}$, satisfy a functional equation $s\mapsto 2-s$, and **vanish at the central point $s=1$**. The vanishing at $s=1$ (forced by the Heegner hypothesis, which fixes the sign of the functional equation) is precisely why the FIRST DERIVATIVE is the leading term and why a height, not an $L$-value, appears.

**Chapter I, section 6: the main result (p. 230).** Let $c=(x)-(\infty)\in J(H)$.
- **Theorem (6.1):** the generating series $g_{\mathcal{A}}(z)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\langle c,T_m c^\sigma\rangle\,e^{2\pi imz}$ is a cusp form of weight 2 on $\Gamma_0(N)$ satisfying, for all newforms $f$,
$$(f,g_{\mathcal{A}})=\frac{u^2\,|D|^{1/2}}{8\pi^2}\,L'_{\mathcal{A}}(f,1),$$
where $(\,,)$ is the Petersson product and $u=\#(\mathcal{O}^\times/\{\pm1\})$ ($u=1$ when $|D|>4$).
- **Theorem (6.3):** assembling the $\chi$-eigencomponent $c_\chi=\sum_\sigma\chi^{-1}(\sigma)c^\sigma$ and projecting to the $f$-isotypic part $c_{\chi,f}$,
$$L'(f,\chi,1)=\frac{8\pi^2\,(f,f)}{u^2\,|D|^{1/2}}\,\hat{h}(c_{\chi,f}),$$
rewritten attractively (6.5) as $L'(f,\chi,1)=\dfrac{\lVert\omega_f\rVert^2}{u^2|D|^{1/2}}\,\hat{h}_K(c_{\chi,f})$ with $\lVert\omega_f\rVert^2=8\pi^2(f,f)$ the period, so the two forms agree. This is **the height-derivative formula**: the first derivative at $s=1$ equals a positive constant times a Neron-Tate height.

**Chapter I, section 7: application to elliptic curves (p. 230-231).** For $E/\mathbb{Q}$ modular with newform $f$:
- **Theorem (7.3):** assume $L(E,1)=0$. Then there is a rational point $P\in E(\mathbb{Q})$ with $L'(E,1)=\alpha\,\Omega\,\langle P,P\rangle$, $\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$. In particular (1) if $L'(E,1)\neq 0$ then $E(\mathbb{Q})$ contains a point of **infinite order**; (2) if $L'(E,1)\neq 0$ and $\mathrm{rank}\,E(\mathbb{Q})=1$, the BSD formula (7.2) holds up to a nonzero rational factor. The point $P$ is the trace of the Heegner point, $P_K=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(H/K)}\pi(x)^\sigma\in E(K)$, pushed down by the modular parametrization $\pi:X_0(N)\to E$.
- **Proposition (7.4):** for the curve $-139\,y^2=x^3+10x^2-20x+8$ (algebraic rank $3$, established by separate descent computation) the paper proves $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\geq 3$. The mechanism: the Heegner point $P_K$ is torsion, so $L'(E/K,1)=0$ by Theorem (6.3), forcing analytic rank $\geq 2$; the Galois-conjugation and sign Corollaries (1.2)/(1.3) then push the order to $\geq 3$. What Gross-Zagier prove here is the lower bound $\mathrm{ord}\geq 3$ on the analytic side, not the full BSD rank equality (which in rank $3$ is open). This is the explicit example where the rank-1 Heegner machine produces nothing (a torsion point) and the analytic rank is genuinely $\geq 2$.

**Chapter I, section 8: the class number problem of Gauss (pp. 231-232).** Combined with Goldfeld's theorem, Proposition (7.4) gives an EFFECTIVE lower bound: **Theorem (8.1):** for any $\varepsilon>0$ there is an effectively computable $\kappa(\varepsilon)>0$ with $h(D)>\kappa(\varepsilon)(\log|D|)^{1-\varepsilon}$. Oesterle's sharper form $C(t)h(D)\geq\log|D|$ (with Mestre's later replacement of $5077\cdot714877$ by conductor $5077$) yields $h(D)>\tfrac{1}{55}\log|D|$ for $D$ prime, completing the determination that the largest $|D|$ with $h(D)=3$ is $907$. This is the unexpected dividend: an explicit curve with provably $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L\geq 3$ feeds Goldfeld's theorem (which needs one curve whose $L$-function vanishes to order $\geq 3$ at the center) to settle an effective class-number bound.

**Chapter I, section 9: the plan of proof (pp. 232-233).** The whole paper is the matching of two cusp forms. **Track A (heights, Chapters II-III).** Compute $\langle c, T_m c^\sigma\rangle=\sum_v\langle c,T_m d^\sigma\rangle_v$ place by place (Proposition (9.1): $c$ and $T_m d^\sigma$ are coprime iff $N>1$ and $r_{\mathcal{A}}(m)=0$). Archimedean places via a Green's function on $X_0(N)(\mathbb{C})$ summed over CM points (Chapter II); non-archimedean via intersection theory on a regular model / arithmetic surface, with quaternionic deformation formulas (Chapter III). **Track B (L-derivatives, Chapter IV).** By Rankin's method plus the theory of **holomorphic projection**, construct for each weight $2k$ a cusp form $\phi_{\mathcal{A}}=\sum a_{m,\mathcal{A}}q^m$ with $(f,\phi_{\mathcal{A}})=\tfrac{(2k-2)!}{2^{4k-1}\pi^{2k}}|D|^{1/2}L'_{\mathcal{A}}(f,k)$ (equation (9.2)). **The match.** The explicit Fourier coefficients satisfy, in weight 2,
$$\langle c,T_m c^\sigma\rangle = u^2\,a_{m,\mathcal{A}}\qquad (m\geq 1,\ (m,N)=1),$$
equation (9.3), so $g_{\mathcal{A}}$ and $u^2\phi_{\mathcal{A}}$ differ by an old form, and Theorem (6.1) follows from (9.2). The two independent computations of the same $q$-expansion coefficients are the engine of the proof.

**Chapter II, sections 1-2: archimedean heights and the Green's function (pp. 233-238).** The local symbol $\langle a,b\rangle_v$ at an archimedean place is built from a function $G(x,y)=\langle(x)-(x_0),(y)-(y_0)\rangle$ on $X_0(N)(\mathbb{C})\times X_0(N)(\mathbb{C})$ with logarithmic singularities of residue $\pm 1$ along the diagonal and harmonic elsewhere. To produce it from an automorphic kernel, the naive averaged function $g(z,z')=\log\big|\tfrac{z-z'}{\bar z-z'}\big|^2$ summed over $\Gamma_0(N)$ **diverges**, so Gross and Zagier deform harmonicity to $\Delta g=\varepsilon g$ (eigenfunction of the hyperbolic Laplacian with eigenvalue $\varepsilon=s(s-1)$), sum the resulting kernel, analytically continue in $s$, and recover the harmonic Green's function by subtracting the singular term in the limit at the relevant special value of $s$. The kernel is the **Legendre function of the second kind** $Q_{s-1}(t)$ (equation (2.5)), with $g_s(z,z')=-2Q_{s-1}\!\big(1+\tfrac{|z-z'|^2}{2yy'}\big)$. Evaluating the resolvent kernel $G^m_{N,s}(z,z')$ at pairs of Heegner (CM) points and taking $\tfrac{d}{ds}$ at the center produces the archimedean contribution to $\langle c,T_m c^\sigma\rangle$. This is the concrete meaning of "archimedean height = sum over CM points": the local height is a finite sum of values of an explicit transcendental kernel at the $2^s h$ Heegner points and their Hecke translates.

**Chapter IV, Theorem (6.9) and the holomorphic projection (pp. 304-305).** The closing identity of Chapter IV gives the holomorphic cusp form $\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}=\sum a_{m,\mathcal{A}}q^m$ of weight 2 with $L_{\mathcal{A}}(f,1)=0$ and $L'_{\mathcal{A}}(f,1)=\tfrac{8\pi^2}{|D|^{1/2}}(f,\Phi_{\mathcal{A}})$, together with the explicit Fourier coefficient $a_{m,\mathcal{A}}$ as a sum over $\sigma'_{\mathcal{A}}(n)r_{\mathcal{A}}(m|D|-nN)$ plus class-number and Legendre-$Q$ terms. This is the analytic side that must equal the geometric side of Track A.

**Chapter V, section 1: the main identity (pp. 305-308).** The proof that $g_{\mathcal{A}}$ is a cusp form is a short formal argument: for any $\mathbb{Q}$-linear $\alpha:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}$, $\sum_m\alpha(T_m)q^m$ is a weight-2 cusp form because $\mathbb{T}$ acts faithfully on the cotangent space $S=S_2(\Gamma_0(N))$ and the pairing $\beta(T,f)=a_1(Tf)$ is perfect. Identifying $g_{\mathcal{A}}$ uses Manin-Drinfeld ($c$ and $d$ give the same class in $J(H)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$) and the place-by-place sum from Chapters II-IV, yielding $\langle c,T_m c^\sigma\rangle=u^2 a_{m,\mathcal{A}}$, i.e. Theorem (6.1). Theorem (6.3) then follows by Galois-averaging over $G=\mathrm{Gal}(H/K)$ and the orthogonality of distinct Hecke eigenvectors.

**Chapter V, section 1: the sign corollaries (pp. 308-309).**
- **Corollary (1.1):** for any newform $f$ and any character $\chi$, $L'(f,\chi,1)\geq 0$. (Immediate from (6.3): both the Petersson norm $(f,f)$ and the global height $\hat{h}$ are positive, so the right-hand side is non-negative. This is the sign BSD predicts; it is a positivity of the central derivative, not a statement about zeros on the critical line, and so is not the Riemann hypothesis for $L(f,\chi,s)$.)
- **Corollary (1.2):** all conjugates $L(f^\alpha,\chi^\alpha,s)$ either have a simple zero at $s=1$ or all have order $\geq 3$.
- **Corollary (1.3):** $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(f,s)\in\{0,1\}\iff\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(f^\alpha,s)$ matches, and likewise $\geq 2,\geq 3$. (The order of vanishing at the central point is invariant under Galois conjugation of $f$, in line with a general motivic conjecture.)

**Chapter V, section 2: comparison with BSD over $K$ (pp. 309-312).** Setting $\chi=\mathbf{1}$ and working over $K$, with $P_K=\sum_\sigma\pi(x^\sigma)\in E(K)$ the trace of the Heegner point:
- **Theorem (2.1):** $L'(E/K,1)=\lVert\omega\rVert^2\,m^2\,\hat{h}(P_K)\,/\,c^2 u_K^2|D|^{1/2}$, the precise descended height formula over $K$.
- **Conjecture (2.2)** (NOT a theorem): if $P_K$ has infinite order, it generates a finite-index subgroup of $E(K)$ and that index equals $c\cdot m\cdot u_K\cdot\lVert\mathrm{Sha}_K\rVert^{1/2}$, **stated with the explicit caveat that $\mathrm{Sha}_K$ is only conjecturally finite (and, if finite, a square).** This is the exact point where the paper itself does not cross into the open territory. The proven Theorem (2.1) determines $L'$ from the height up to a rational square; the identification of the index with $\mathrm{Sha}$ is left conjectural here. (Kolyvagin later proves $P_K$ of infinite order $\Rightarrow$ rank $E(K)=1$ and $\mathrm{Sha}_K$ finite; that is a different paper.)
- **Conjecture (2.3):** if $E(K)$ has rank 1, then $c\cdot m\cdot u_K\cdot\lVert\mathrm{Sha}_K\rVert^{1/2}$ is divisible by $t=\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tor}}$.

Descending from $K$ to $\mathbb{Q}$: choose $K$ by Waldspurger's theorem so the twisted value $L_\varepsilon(f,1)\neq 0$, use $L(f,s)L_\varepsilon(f,s)=L(E/K,s)$, and conclude that $\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=1$ implies $\mathrm{rank}\,E(K)\geq 1$, with equality giving the BSD formula over $K$ up to a nonzero rational SQUARE. The descent therefore relies on the auxiliary nonvanishing theorems (Waldspurger; Bump-Friedberg-Hoffstein, Murty-Murty for $L'$), all themselves in the rank $\leq 1$ regime.

**Chapter V, section 3: generalized Heegner points (pp. 313+).** When $\varepsilon(N)=-1$ (the Heegner hypothesis fails), the formula for $L_{\mathcal{A}}(f,1)$ has the same shape but the relevant points live on a Shimura curve attached to a quaternion algebra $B/\mathbb{Q}$ (indefinite if $|S|$ even, definite if $|S|$ odd, $S=\{p:\mathrm{ord}_p(N)\text{ odd},\varepsilon(p)=-1\}$). The detailed proofs are deferred to a later paper. Section 4 ($k>1$) states only an ALGEBRAICITY CONJECTURE about higher-weight Heegner cycles: combinations of special values of the resolvent kernel at Heegner points are conjecturally logarithms of algebraic numbers in $H$. **This is explicitly a conjecture, not proven here.**

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN.** The height-derivative identity (Theorems (6.1), (6.3), (6.5)) is a theorem: $L'(f,\chi,1)=\tfrac{8\pi^2(f,f)}{u^2|D|^{1/2}}\hat{h}(c_{\chi,f})$ (with $(f,f)$ the Petersson norm), hence $L'(E/K,1)=c\cdot\hat{h}(P_K)$, $c>0$ (Theorem (2.1) over $K$; (7.3) over $\mathbb{Q}$ under $L(E,1)=0$). Consequence: $L'(E,1)\neq 0\Rightarrow E(\mathbb{Q})$ has a point of infinite order. Corollary (1.1): $L'(f,\chi,1)\geq 0$. The effective class-number bound (8.1) is a theorem.

2. **The GAP, stated by the paper itself.** Gross-Zagier does NOT prove rank $E(\mathbb{Q})=1$ from $L'(E,1)\neq 0$, and does NOT bound or prove finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$. Conjectures (2.2) and (2.3) of Chapter V carry the explicit "$\mathrm{Sha}_K$ conjecturally finite" caveat. The full rank equality and $\mathrm{Sha}$-finiteness in rank $\leq 1$ are Kolyvagin's contribution (separate note), via the Euler system built FROM this one Heegner point.

3. **EXACT regime.** Analytic rank 1 (and the boundary case rank 0, where $c_{\chi,f}$ is torsion and $L'$ vanishes to higher order), ARCHIMEDEAN. The central derivative $L'(\cdot,1)$ is the archimedean special value; the height $\hat{h}$ is the archimedean (Neron-Tate) height assembled from local symbols at all places of $H$. This is NOT a $p$-adic statement: contrast with [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md) and with the $p$-adic Gross-Zagier / Mazur-Tate-Teitelbaum line.

4. **Three detectors.**
   - **Parity-only: PASSES (does more than parity).** The root number gives only rank mod 2; here we get an exact real identity $L'=c\,\hat{h}$ and an actual non-torsion point. This is strictly stronger than the parity that modularity already delivers. So it is NOT a parity-only mirage.
   - **Sha-finiteness-assumed: PASSES (does not assume it).** The proven Theorems (6.1)/(6.3)/(2.1) make no finiteness assumption. The paper isolates the $\mathrm{Sha}$ dependence cleanly inside Conjectures (2.2)/(2.3), which it labels conjectural. So Gross-Zagier itself does not smuggle in $\mathrm{Sha}$-finiteness.
   - **Function-field-mirage: PASSES (genuinely a number-field theorem).** The archimedean height uses a Green's function on the Riemann surface $X_0(N)(\mathbb{C})$ and the analytic continuation of a real-analytic Eisenstein/Legendre kernel; the non-archimedean side uses intersection theory on an arithmetic surface over $\mathbb{Z}$. There is no geometric Frobenius doing the work; the result does not collapse to the Tate/Artin-Tate function-field template.
   - **Net: clean against all three, but STRUCTURALLY CAPPED.** The single new detector this source motivates is the rank-1 ceiling itself (see point 5).

5. **The rank $\leq 1$ (proven) vs rank $\geq 2$ (open) control pair.** This source IS the proven side of the control pair, and it shows exactly why the wall sits at rank 1. The construction outputs ONE divisor class $c=(x)-(\infty)$, equivalently one point $P_K\in E(K)$. A single point spans at most a rank-1 subgroup. The height formula reads off $L'$ to FIRST order only. The machine certifies rank exactly 1 ($P_K$ non-torsion) or rank 0 ($P_K$ torsion); it can NEVER produce two independent generators or a nonzero $2\times 2$ height-pairing determinant. Proposition (7.4) is the witness: a curve (algebraic rank 3, proven analytic order $\geq 3$) where the Heegner construction yields a torsion point and the machine says nothing. The cap is intrinsic, not a soft limitation.

6. **Research direction / experiment fed.** Direct successor: [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md), which asks for an object replacing the single Heegner point with one that outputs two independent points (equivalently makes a $2\times 2$ height determinant nonzero). This note fixes the precise target the replacement must beat. Secondary: [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md), because Kolyvagin's Euler system inherits this exact ceiling (it is built from $y_K$ and sees only first-order behavior). Experimentally it feeds the height / strong-BSD threads: the smallest rank-1 curve `37a1` is where $\hat{h}(P_K)\neq 0$ is computable and the formula is checkable; the smallest rank-2 curve `389a1` is where the construction provably falls silent. The atlas Heegner-point row should read "PROVEN, rank $\leq 1$, archimedean; produces one point; clean against all three detectors; structurally capped."
