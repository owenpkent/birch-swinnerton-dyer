# Reading note: J. H. Silverman, "Advanced Topics in the Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves," Graduate Texts in Mathematics 151, Springer (1994).

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the foundational reference for the two pieces of machinery that sit on the *regulator side* of strong BSD: (i) complex multiplication theory (Chapter II), which supplies the CM curves whose Hecke $L$-functions are handled by Coates-Wiles and Rubin and whose Heegner points seed Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin; and (ii) the theory of *local heights* and the canonical-height decomposition $\hat{h} = \sum_v \hat{\lambda}_v$ (Chapter VI), which is the analytic-geometric definition of the Neron-Tate pairing whose determinant is the regulator $\operatorname{Reg}_E$ in the strong-BSD leading-term formula. The book does not prove BSD or any case of it. It builds, rigorously and from the ground up, the *objects* that the proven regime (rank $\leq 1$) and any future rank-$\geq 2$ regime must manipulate: the positive-definite height pairing, its local pieces, the sigma-function archimedean formula, and the quasi-parallelogram law that makes the local pieces computable. The positive-definiteness of the height pairing, which is exactly what a rank-$\geq 2$ method must witness via a nonzero $r \times r$ determinant, is established here as a structural fact about the Neron-Tate form, not as anything BSD-specific.

## One-line takeaway

Silverman's second volume builds, from first principles and independent of BSD, the two foundations on the regulator side of the conjecture: the theory of complex multiplication (Chapter II), which produces the CM curves and the Hecke $L$-functions used by the proven rank-0 CM results and the imaginary-quadratic fields that carry Heegner points; and the theory of local and canonical heights (Chapter VI), which *defines* the Neron-Tate height $\hat{h}$ as a sum of local heights $\hat{\lambda}_v$, gives the archimedean local height explicitly via the Weierstrass $\sigma$-function, characterizes each $\hat{\lambda}_v$ by a quasi-parallelogram law, and establishes that $\hat{h}$ is a positive-definite quadratic form on $E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})/E_{\mathrm{tors}} \otimes \mathbb{R}$ whose pairing determinant on a basis of $E(\mathbb{Q})$ is the regulator $\operatorname{Reg}_E$. The book proves no BSD case; it is the construction manual for the height-pairing object whose nonvanishing determinant a rank-$\geq 2$ proof must exhibit and whose positive-definiteness it establishes once and for all.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Chapter II: complex multiplication.** The chapter develops CM for elliptic curves over $\mathbb{C}$ and then over number fields. Over $\mathbb{C}$, an elliptic curve $E_\Lambda = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda$ has $\operatorname{End}(E) = \mathbb{Z}$ generically; the curves with $\operatorname{End}(E) \otimes \mathbb{Q} = K$ an imaginary quadratic field are exactly the **CM curves**, with $\operatorname{End}(E)$ an order $\mathcal{O}$ in $K$. The central arithmetic results:
- **The main theorem of CM.** If $E/\mathbb{C}$ has CM by the maximal order $\mathcal{O}_K$, then $j(E)$ is an algebraic integer, $K(j(E)) = H$ is the **Hilbert class field** of $K$, and $[\mathbb{Q}(j(E)):\mathbb{Q}] = [K(j(E)):K] = h_K$ (the "first main theorem" of CM). The action of $\operatorname{Gal}(H/K) \cong \operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_K)$ on the $j$-invariants of the $h_K$ curves with CM by $\mathcal{O}_K$ is given by the ideal-class action $\mathfrak{a} \star E = \mathbb{C}/\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\Lambda$.
- **The Hecke character and its $L$-function.** To a CM curve $E/L$ (with $L \supseteq K$) one attaches a **Grossencharacter** $\psi_{E/L}$ of $L$, an idele-class character with the property that $L(E/L, s) = L(s, \psi_{E/L}) L(s, \bar{\psi}_{E/L})$: the Hasse-Weil $L$-function of a CM curve factors as a product of Hecke $L$-functions of the imaginary quadratic field. This factorization is what makes the CM case analytically tractable (Deuring; Weil; and the input to Coates-Wiles), because the analytic continuation and functional equation of $L(E,s)$ are *Hecke's*, available unconditionally for CM curves rather than via modularity. (It does not by itself prove BSD for CM curves; it makes $L(E,s)$ a well-understood object that the Coates-Wiles and Rubin arguments then act on.)
- **Good reduction and the Frobenius.** $E/L$ with CM by $\mathcal{O}_K$ has potential good reduction everywhere, and at a prime of good reduction the Frobenius eigenvalue is $\psi(\mathfrak{p})$, an *arithmetic* (Hecke-character) Frobenius. This is the precise object that the function-field mirage lacks an honest analog of over $\mathbb{Q}$: it is an arithmetic Hecke value, not a geometric Frobenius acting on the cohomology of a surface.

For the BSD program this chapter is upstream of three proven results: the CM rank-0 results (Coates-Wiles), the elliptic-unit Euler system (Rubin), and the existence of the imaginary quadratic fields and ring-class structure carrying Heegner points (Gross-Zagier). None of those proofs lives here; their raw material does.

**Chapter III: elliptic surfaces.** The chapter treats elliptic curves over function fields $k(C)$ and their associated **elliptic surfaces** $\mathcal{E} \to C$: the Kodaira classification of singular fibers ($I_n$, $II$, $III$, $IV$, $I_n^*$, etc.), the Shioda-Tate formula relating the Mordell-Weil rank of $E/k(C)$ to the Neron-Severi group of the surface,
$$ \operatorname{rank} \operatorname{NS}(\mathcal{E}) = 2 + \sum_v (m_v - 1) + \operatorname{rank} E(k(C)), $$
and the height pairing on $E(k(C))$ realized as an intersection pairing on $\operatorname{NS}(\mathcal{E})$ modulo the trivial lattice. **This is the function-field picture in its cleanest form, and it is exactly the source of the function-field mirage (Detector 3):** over $k(C)$ the Mordell-Weil rank is read off geometrically from divisor classes on a surface, and the regulator is a geometric intersection determinant. The geometric Frobenius acting on $H^2$ of the surface (in the $\ell$-adic realization) is what makes Tate's theorem and the function-field BSD provable under finite Brauer group. The chapter is the dictionary that lets one see precisely which geometric input the number-field case is missing.

**Chapter IV-V: formal groups, bad reduction, the Neron model (context).** Chapter IV develops the formal group of $E$ and Chapter V (Tate curves, bad reduction) the local structure at primes of multiplicative and additive reduction. These supply the component groups, the Tamagawa numbers $c_p = [E(\mathbb{Q}_p):E^0(\mathbb{Q}_p)]$ (the local factor in strong BSD), and the local behavior needed to make the non-archimedean local heights of Chapter VI well-defined. They are background for the height theory, not its core.

**Chapter VI: heights (the core for this note).** This chapter builds the canonical (Neron-Tate) height by *localizing* it, and is the regulator side of strong BSD in definitional form.

- **The classical theory of local heights (Neron's quasi-functions).** A **Neron local height** (local height function) on $E/K_v$ is a function $\hat{\lambda}_v : E(K_v) \setminus \{O\} \to \mathbb{R}$, attached to the divisor $(O)$, characterized up to the parallelogram law by: it is continuous, it is bounded outside any $v$-adic neighborhood of $O$, near $O$ it satisfies $\hat{\lambda}_v(P) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\log|x(P)|_v + O(1)$ (a logarithmic singularity along $(O)$), and it obeys the **quasi-parallelogram law**
$$ \hat\lambda_v(P+Q)+\hat\lambda_v(P-Q) = 2\hat\lambda_v(P)+2\hat\lambda_v(Q)+\log|x(P)-x(Q)|_v $$
up to a normalization constant depending on the model (Chapter VI's existence-and-uniqueness theorem for Neron local heights). The local height is the unique such quasi-quadratic function; it is the building block.

- **The archimedean local height via the sigma function.** Over $K_v = \mathbb{C}$, with $E(\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda$ and $z$ the elliptic logarithm of $P$, the local height has a **closed sigma-function formula**
$$ \hat{\lambda}_\infty(P) = -\tfrac{1}{12}\log| \Delta | \;-\; \log| \sigma(z; \Lambda)| \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2} \eta(z)\, z \quad (\text{schematic, up to model-dependent normalization}), $$
i.e. it is expressed through the **Weierstrass $\sigma$-function** $\sigma(z;\Lambda)$ together with the quasi-period $\eta$ and the discriminant $\Delta$, normalized so that the resulting global sum is the canonical height. In practice it is written via $|\sigma(z)| \exp(-\tfrac{1}{2} z \eta(z))$ and the real period; the displayed expression is schematic and is not the book's exact normalized identity. This is **the sigma-function archimedean formula**: it is what makes $\hat{\lambda}_\infty$ numerically computable to high precision and is the archimedean term in the local decomposition. It is the archimedean analog, on the height side, of the Green's function that Gross-Zagier use on $X_0(N)(\mathbb{C})$.

- **The non-archimedean local height.** Over a non-archimedean $K_v$, $\hat{\lambda}_v$ is given on the curve with split multiplicative or good reduction by an explicit formula in $v(x(P))$, $v(\Delta)$, and the component of the special fiber that $P$ reduces to: for good reduction $\hat\lambda_v(P) = \tfrac12 \max\{0, -v(x(P))\}\log q_v + \tfrac{1}{12} v(\Delta) \log q_v$ (up to normalization), and for multiplicative reduction a piecewise-quadratic Bernoulli-type expression $B_2(\cdot)$ in the component index. The non-archimedean local height is thus an **intersection multiplicity on the Neron model** in disguise: it measures how $P$ meets the zero section and the bad fibers. This is exactly the local-intersection reading that the function-field case (Chapter III) makes literal.

- **The canonical global height as a sum of local heights.** The **canonical (Neron-Tate) height** decomposes as
$$ \hat{h}(P) = \frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]} \sum_{v} n_v \, \hat{\lambda}_v(P), $$
the sum over all places $v$ of $K$ with local multiplicities $n_v = [K_v:\mathbb{Q}_v]$ (the decomposition theorem of Chapter VI). This is the central structural statement of the chapter: the global object that appears in BSD is literally a sum of the explicit local objects above. The associated **canonical height pairing**
$$ \langle P, Q \rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\big( \hat{h}(P+Q) - \hat{h}(P) - \hat{h}(Q) \big) $$
is a symmetric bilinear form on $E(\bar{K})/E_{\mathrm{tors}}$, and (with the quadratic-form theory carried over from the first volume's height machinery) one has:
- $\hat{h}(P) \geq 0$ with $\hat{h}(P) = 0 \iff P \in E_{\mathrm{tors}}$ (the height is nondegenerate modulo torsion);
- consequently the pairing $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ is **positive-definite** on $E(K) \otimes \mathbb{R} \cong \mathbb{R}^r$.

The **regulator** of $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is then $\operatorname{Reg}_E = \det\big( \langle P_i, P_j \rangle \big)_{1 \leq i,j \leq r}$ for a basis $P_1, \dots, P_r$ of $E(\mathbb{Q})/E_{\mathrm{tors}}$, and positive-definiteness gives $\operatorname{Reg}_E > 0$ whenever $r \geq 1$ (with the convention $\operatorname{Reg}_E = 1$ for $r = 0$). **This is the object in the strong-BSD numerator.** Silverman establishes its positivity unconditionally; what strong BSD asks, and what the proof program must deliver in rank $\geq 2$, is to *produce a basis* (or even two independent points) and tie $\operatorname{Reg}_E$ to the leading $L$-coefficient.

**The duplication / quasi-parallelogram law as the computational engine.** Throughout the local-height sections of Chapter VI the quasi-parallelogram law is what pins each $\hat\lambda_v$ down uniquely and what lets the global height be computed by the standard descent / averaging: $\hat{h}(P) = \lim_{n\to\infty} 4^{-n} h(2^n P)$ where $h$ is the naive Weil height of $x(\cdot)$, and the local laws make each step explicit. This is the algorithmic backbone of every numerical regulator computation in the experiments thread.

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN here.** Two foundational theories, both unconditional and regime-free: (a) the CM theory of Chapter II ($j(E)$ algebraic, $K(j(E)) = H$ the Hilbert class field, $L(E/L,s) = L(s,\psi)L(s,\bar\psi)$ a product of Hecke $L$-functions); (b) the height theory of Chapter VI (existence and uniqueness of local heights $\hat\lambda_v$ via the quasi-parallelogram law, the sigma-function archimedean formula, the decomposition $\hat{h} = \sum_v n_v \hat\lambda_v$, and positive-definiteness of the canonical height pairing, hence $\operatorname{Reg}_E > 0$ for $r \geq 1$). The elliptic-surface theory of Chapter III (Shioda-Tate, Kodaira fibers) is proven over function fields.

2. **The GAP.** This book proves **no** case of BSD. It does not relate $\operatorname{Reg}_E$ to any $L$-value, does not produce rational points, and does not bound $\operatorname{Sha}$. It defines and constructs the objects; the conjecture is the assertion that the leading $L$-coefficient equals $\Omega_E \operatorname{Reg}_E \prod_p c_p \#\operatorname{Sha} / (\#E_{\mathrm{tors}})^2$, which is entirely outside the book. The positive-definiteness it proves guarantees $\operatorname{Reg}_E > 0$ but gives no handle on *computing* a full-rank basis in rank $\geq 2$, which is where the difficulty sits.

3. **EXACT regime.** Foundational, hence **all ranks and no rank**: the height pairing, its positive-definiteness, and the local decomposition are stated for arbitrary $r$, so this material is *equally available* in rank 0, 1, and $\geq 2$. That is the point: the regulator object is not the obstruction (it is well-defined and positive in every rank); the obstruction is exhibiting points and tying their height determinant to $L$-data. Chapter VI's local height is **archimedean and non-archimedean** but, where archimedean (the $\sigma$-function formula), it is the *real* (not $p$-adic) local height; the $p$-adic local height feeding a $p$-adic regulator is the parallel object in the $p$-adic line ([`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md)), not this book.

4. **Three detectors.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): NOT APPLICABLE, but anchors the contrast.** The book proves no rank statement, so it cannot be a parity method. It is, however, the source of the precise meaning of "more than parity": the canonical height is a *real-valued positive-definite form*, and a rank-$\geq 2$ certificate is a *nonzero $2\times 2$ determinant of this form*, which is strictly richer than the root-number sign. The Chapter VI pairing is literally the determinant in Direction 01's spec.
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES (n/a).** The height and CM constructions make no finiteness assumption on $\operatorname{Sha}$ and say nothing about it; $\operatorname{Sha}$ does not appear in Chapters II, III, or VI. Clean.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): the SOURCE of the dictionary, must be read with care.** Chapter III is precisely the function-field / elliptic-surface picture where the Mordell-Weil rank and the regulator are *geometric* (Shioda-Tate, intersection on $\operatorname{NS}(\mathcal{E})$) and where geometric Frobenius on the surface powers function-field BSD (a theorem of Tate / Artin-Tate, but still *conditional* on finiteness of the Brauer group, equivalently of $\operatorname{Sha}$, not unconditional). The non-archimedean local height of Chapter VI is the number-field shadow of the *same* intersection-theoretic quantity. The detector's lesson lives here: a method that computes the rank-$\geq 2$ regulator by surface geometry has imported the Chapter III structure that the number field lacks. The book itself does not commit the mirage (it keeps the two settings distinct); it is where one learns exactly what would be borrowed.

5. **The rank $\leq 1$ (proven) vs rank $\geq 2$ (open) control pair.** This source sits *underneath* the control pair rather than on either side. It provides the height pairing that, on the proven side, Gross-Zagier evaluates at a single Heegner point ($r=1$, a $1\times 1$ determinant $\hat{h}(P) > 0$), and that, on the open side, a rank-$\geq 2$ proof must evaluate at $\geq 2$ independent points (an $r \times r$ determinant $> 0$ with $r \geq 2$). Crucially, Silverman's positive-definiteness theorem already guarantees that determinant is positive *once the points exist*; the wall is not positivity, it is **existence and analytic certification** of the points. This sharpens the program's thesis: the rank-$\geq 2$ obstruction is not in the regulator's algebra (settled here) but in constructing the lattice it is evaluated on.

6. **Which direction / experiment it feeds.** Primary: [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md) (the "$2 \times 2$ height-pairing matrix with nonzero determinant" in that spec is exactly the Chapter VI canonical pairing; this note fixes its definition, its positive-definiteness, and the sigma-function and local-height formulas that compute it). Secondary: [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md) via the Chapter II CM theory that produces elliptic units and Hecke $L$-functions, and [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md) as the archimedean counterpart against which the $p$-adic local height and $p$-adic regulator are compared. Experimentally it is the backbone of the strong-BSD quantities thread (`experiments/strong_bsd_quantities/`): the canonical-height descent $\hat{h}(P) = \lim 4^{-n} h(2^n P)$ and the $\sigma$-function archimedean local height are what every numerical $\operatorname{Reg}_E$ computation rests on, checkable already on `37a1` ($r=1$) and `389a1` ($r=2$).
