# Reading note: S. Zhang, "Heights of Heegner points on Shimura curves," Annals of Mathematics 153 (2001), 27-147.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the totally-real / quaternionic generalization of Gross-Zagier (1986) and Kolyvagin (1990). It proves a Gross-Zagier height formula for Heegner points on Shimura curves over a totally real field $F$, and uses it to deduce a rank-1 BSD-type theorem (the Mordell-Weil rank equals the analytic order of vanishing, and $\mathrm{Sha}$ is finite) for a class of $\mathrm{GL}_2$-type abelian varieties $A/F$ attached to Hilbert modular newforms, **assuming analytic order $\leq 1$**. Its importance for this project is precisely that it shows how far the one-point height machine generalizes: it removes the Heegner hypothesis (it works on a Shimura curve attached to a quaternion algebra, so the parametrization always exists once the root-number sign is right) and crosses to totally real base fields, yet the output is still a single class $z_f$, so the theorem it can prove is still capped at analytic rank $\leq 1$.
>
> **Reading depth.** Read from the arXiv PDF (`math/0101269`, 121 pp, identical content to the Annals version, pagination 27-147): the full Table of Contents, the Introduction (Theorems A, B, C stated verbatim, the construction of $z_f$, the plan of proof), Section 3.3-3.5 (newforms on $X$, $L$-functions associated to newforms, functional equation, Eisenstein/theta series), and Section 4.1-4.3 (Arakelov height pairing $\hat z = \hat\eta - h\hat\xi + Z$ and the Hecke computations $T(m)\eta$, $T(m)\hat\xi$). Sections 5 (local intersections, Clifford algebras, final formula), 6 (Rankin-Selberg derivative of the $L$-series, holomorphic projection), and 7 (proofs of Theorems C then A) were read in outline from the ToC and Introduction's plan-of-proof, not line by line.

## One-line takeaway

Zhang proves a Gross-Zagier formula on Shimura curves over a totally real field $F$ (equating the Neron-Tate height of a Heegner-point class $z_f$ to the central derivative $L_E'(f,1)$), and then, **only in analytic order $\leq 1$**, feeds it into Kolyvagin's Euler-system method to conclude $\operatorname{rank} A(F) = \operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(s,f)$ and $\#\mathrm{Sha}(A) < \infty$; the construction still produces one class, so however general the Shimura curve, the rank-$\geq 2$ regime is untouched.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Setup (Introduction).** Let $F$ be a totally real number field, $g = [F:\mathbb{Q}]$, and $N$ a nonzero ideal of $\mathcal{O}_F$. Let $f$ be a newform on $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_F)$ of parallel weight $2$, level $K_0(N)$, trivial central character. Let $\mathcal{O}_f$ be the order of $\mathbb{C}$ generated over $\mathbb{Z}$ by the Hecke eigenvalues $a(f,m)$. Under the assumption that **either $[F:\mathbb{Q}]$ is odd, or $\operatorname{ord}_v(N) = 1$ for at least one finite place $v$**, there is an abelian variety $A/F$ of dimension $[\mathcal{O}_f:\mathbb{Z}]$ with $L(s,A) = \prod_{\sigma:\mathcal{O}_f\to\mathbb{C}} L(s,f^\sigma)$ up to factors at places dividing $N$. (This parity-type hypothesis is what guarantees a quaternion algebra $B/F$, ramified at all archimedean places but one, whose Shimura curve carries the relevant newform; it is the structural analogue of the Heegner hypothesis being satisfiable.)

**Theorem A (the BSD-type conclusion; Introduction, proved in 7.2).** Assume $L(s,f)$ has order $\leq 1$ at $s=1$. Then for any such $A$:
1. $\operatorname{rank} A(F) = [\mathcal{O}_f:\mathbb{Z}]\cdot \operatorname{ord}_{s=1} L(s,f)$;
2. the Shafarevich-Tate group $\mathrm{Sha}(A)$ is finite.

This is the rank-$\leq 1$ BSD statement (weak BSD plus finite Sha) over a totally real field. It is **stated and proved only under the order $\leq 1$ hypothesis**; it says nothing in analytic order $\geq 2$.

**Theorem B (modularity / the abelian variety on the Jacobian; Introduction, proved in 3.4.4).** The curve $X/F$ (the canonical model of the Shimura curve $X_\tau(\mathbb{C}) = B_+\backslash\mathcal{H}\times\widehat B^\times/\widehat F^\times\widehat R^\times \cup\{\text{cusps}\}$) is connected but not geometrically connected; one sets $\mathrm{Jac}(X) = \operatorname{Res}_{\widetilde F/F}\operatorname{Pic}^0(X/\widetilde F)$. Then there is a **unique** abelian subvariety $A$ of $\mathrm{Jac}(X)$, defined over $F$ of dimension $[\mathcal{O}_f:\mathbb{Z}]$, with $L(s,A) = \prod_{\sigma}L(s,f^\sigma)$ up to bad factors. Proved in Section 3 via Eichler-Shimura plus a newform theory for $X$ built from Jacquet-Langlands; the supercuspidal case uses Waldspurger (Zhang credits Jacquet, and notes Tunnell-Gross obtained related results).

**Theorem C (the Gross-Zagier height formula; Introduction, proved in 7.1).** This is the heart of the paper. Construct the Heegner class as follows. Let $E$ be a totally imaginary quadratic extension of $F$, unramified over the places dividing $N$, with $\varepsilon(N) = (-1)^{g-1}$ where $\varepsilon = \otimes_v\varepsilon_v$ is the character of $\mathbb{A}_F^\times/F^\times$ attached to $E/F$. The CM-point $x$ on $X_\tau(\mathbb{C})$ (image of $\{\sqrt{-1}\}\times\{1\}$) is defined over the Hilbert class field $H$ of $E$. One defines a canonical degree-1 divisor class $\xi_i$ on each connected component $X_i$ by
$$\xi_i := \Big\{ [\Omega^1_{X_i}] + \sum_{p\in X_i}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{u_p}\big)[p] + [\text{cusps}] \Big\} \Big/ \!\int_{X_i}\frac{dx\,dy}{2\pi y^2},$$
a map $\phi: X\to\mathrm{Jac}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ by $p\mapsto p - \xi_i$, and the class
$$z = u_x^{-1}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(H/E)}\phi(x^\sigma) \in \mathrm{Jac}(X)(E)\otimes\mathbb{Q},$$
with $z_f$ its component in $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}$. Set $L_E(s,f) = L(s,f)L(s,\varepsilon,f)$ (the base change to $E$, i.e. $f$ times its $\varepsilon$-twist). Then $L_E(f,1) = 0$ (forced by the sign of the functional equation) and
$$L_E'(f,1) = \frac{(8\pi^2)^g}{d_F^2\sqrt{d_E}}\,[K_0(1):K_0(N)]\,(f,f)\,\langle z_f, z_f\rangle,$$
where $\langle z_f, z_f\rangle$ is the Neron-Tate height, $d_F$ the discriminant of $F$, $d_E$ the norm of the relative discriminant of $E/F$, and $(f,f)$ the Petersson inner product on $Z(\mathbb{A}_F)\mathrm{GL}_2(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{A}_F)$. For $F=\mathbb{Q}$ with every prime factor of $N$ split in $E$ this recovers Gross-Zagier; the new content is that primes $\wp$ dividing $2$ or with $\operatorname{ord}_\wp(N)>1$ are allowed (handled via local intersection theory at mildly singular integral models).

**How A follows from C (Introduction plan; 7.2).** If $L(s,f)$ has order $\leq 1$, then by a nonvanishing-of-twist result (the $F=\mathbb{Q}$ statement of [3] generalizes to any number field; cf. [40]) one chooses $E$ so that $L_E(f,s)$ has order exactly $1$. Theorem C then gives $\langle z_f, z_f\rangle \neq 0$, so $z_f$ has infinite order. Kolyvagin's method (Euler system from the Heegner class, refs [17],[28],[29],[30] in the paper) then applies **directly, "without any new difficulty,"** to conclude rank $1$ and finite Sha. The only added input is a correct system of CM-points, supplied at the end of the paper. This is the precise place where the proof is locked to order $\leq 1$: the Euler system is built from the single class $z_f$.

**1. Shimura curves.** Integral models $\mathcal{M}_K/\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_F$ are constructed via a moduli interpretation (1.1-1.2), using a quaternion algebra $B/F$ unramified at the chosen archimedean place $\tau$, $B\otimes\mathbb{R}\simeq M_2(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathbb{H}^{g-1}$ (eq. 1.1.1). Drinfeld-Carayol give a regular integral model when $K$ is small. Special fibers (1.3), Hecke correspondences and their reductions (1.4), and the order $R$ of type $(N,E)$ with its level structure (1.5) are set up here. The order $R$ contains $\rho(\mathcal{O}_E)$, which is what makes CM-points (hence Heegner points) exist on $X$.

**2. Heegner points.** CM-points (2.1), formal groups (2.2), endomorphisms (2.3), and liftings of distinguished points (2.4). Proposition 4.2.1 identifies positively-oriented CM-points of conductor $c$ with $E^\sharp\backslash B^{\flat,\times}/R^{\flat,\times}$ and computes $T(m)\eta = \sum_{c} r(m/c)\eta_c$ with $r(m)$ the number of integral ideals of $\mathcal{O}_E$ of norm $m$. This is the totally-real analogue of the classical CM-point / Eichler-order dictionary.

**3. Modular forms and $L$-functions.** Newforms on $X$ (3.2) via Eichler-Shimura and Jacquet-Langlands; the supercuspidal case (3.3, Prop 3.3.1) via Waldspurger. The $L$-function of a newform $\phi$ of weight 2, level $K_0(N)$, trivial character is
$$L(s,\phi) = \sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}_F}\frac{a_\phi(m)}{\mathrm{N}(m)^s} = \prod_{\wp\nmid N}\frac{1}{1-a_\phi(\wp)\mathrm{N}(\wp)^{-s}}\prod_{\wp\mid N}\frac{1}{1-a_\phi(\wp)\mathrm{N}(\wp)^{1-2s}}.$$
**Proposition 3.4.2 (functional equation).** With $L^*(s,\phi) = d_N^{s/2}d_F^s\big[\Gamma(s)/(2\pi)^s\big]^g L(s,\phi)$ one has $L^*(s,\phi) = \gamma\,L^*(s,\phi)$ via the Atkin-Lehner sign $w_N(\phi) = \gamma\phi$, $\gamma = \pm1$. The base change $L_E(s,\phi) = L(s,\phi)L(s,\varepsilon,\phi)$ (3.4.3) has, by the Rankin-Selberg analysis of Section 6, a functional equation with sign $\varepsilon(N)(-1)^g$. The choice $\varepsilon(N) = (-1)^{g-1}$ forces this sign to be $-1$, so $L_E(\cdot,f)$ vanishes at the center to odd order: this is exactly the analytic input that puts the problem in the rank-1-accessible regime. Eisenstein and theta series (3.5) supply the kernel for the Rankin-Selberg computation.

**4. Global intersections (Arakelov height).** The height pairing (4.1) is computed on a regular integral model $\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}/\mathcal{O}_F$ via arithmetic intersection theory. One forms the arithmetic class
$$\hat z := \hat\eta - h\hat\xi + Z,$$
where $\hat\eta$ is the arithmetic class of the CM-divisor, $\hat\xi$ the Hodge / Arakelov canonical class (from $\Omega^1[\text{cusps}]$ with its hermitian metric, normalized to degree-zero on each geometric component), $h$ the scalar making the generic-fiber degree zero, and $Z$ a vertical divisor enforcing degree-zero on every fiber component. The bulk of Section 4 computes the Hecke translates $T(m)\eta$ (4.2), $T(m)\hat\xi$ (4.3) and $T(m)Z$ (4.4), culminating in a uniqueness theorem (4.5). Notably, the **absence of a cuspidal-divisor representative for $\xi_i$ and the absence of Dedekind's $\eta$-function in the general (totally real) case** are the essential new difficulties; Zhang resolves them with Arakelov theory plus a strong multiplicity-one argument, and remarks that even for $X=X_0(N)$ this simplifies the original Gross-Zagier height computation.

**5. Local intersections.** Archimedean intersections (5.1) via Green's functions, nonarchimedean (5.2, the key Lemma 5.2.3 on local multiplicities at $\wp\mid N$), Clifford algebras (5.3), assembled into the final local formula (5.4). This is where the ramified / non-split-prime cases that Gross-Zagier excluded are handled.

**6. Derivatives of $L$-series.** The Rankin-Selberg method (6.1) computes the central derivative; Fourier coefficients (6.2), functional equations and derivatives (6.3, giving the sign $\varepsilon(N)(-1)^g$), and holomorphic projection (6.4) to land back in the space of weight-2 forms. The strategy (Introduction, plan of proof): construct two forms $\Psi,\Phi$ with $a(\Psi,m) = \langle z, T(m)z\rangle$ and $L_E'(f,1) = c(f,\Phi)$; Theorem C reduces to $\Phi \equiv \text{const}\cdot\Psi$ modulo oldforms, i.e. to matching Fourier coefficients (one side by Arakelov geometry, the other by Rankin-Selberg).

**7. Proof of the main theorems.** 7.1 proves Theorem C (height = derivative); 7.2 proves Theorem A from C plus Kolyvagin, supplying the CM-point system.

## Project mapping

1. **Proven vs gap.** PROVEN: a Gross-Zagier formula $\langle z_f,z_f\rangle \propto L_E'(f,1)$ on Shimura curves over totally real $F$ (Theorem C, unconditional), and the BSD-type rank equality plus finiteness of Sha (Theorem A) **conditional on analytic order $\leq 1$**. GAP: nothing here addresses analytic order $\geq 2$. The construction outputs one class $z_f$; a single class spans a rank-$\leq 1$ subgroup, so the formula certifies "rank exactly 1" ($z_f$ non-torsion) or "rank 0" ($z_f$ torsion), never two independent generators. The generalization widens the base field and drops the Heegner hypothesis but does not move the rank ceiling.

2. **Exact regime.** Analytic rank/order $\leq 1$. Archimedean (the height is the Neron-Tate / Arakelov archimedean height; the $L$-derivative is the complex-analytic central derivative). Base field: totally real $F$ (and the quadratic CM extension $E/F$), strictly generalizing $F=\mathbb{Q}$. This is the archimedean leg of research direction [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md); the $p$-adic analogue is separate later work, not this paper.

3. **Three detectors.**
   - **Parity-only:** PASSES (does not merely recover parity). It uses the sign $\varepsilon(N)(-1)^g = -1$ to place itself in the odd-order regime, but then produces the actual height and the actual rank-1 generator, going strictly beyond parity. Caveat for the program: the step that beats parity (Theorem C $\Rightarrow$ non-torsion $z_f$) is exactly the step that is confined to order $\leq 1$.
   - **Sha-finiteness-assumed:** PASSES / does not trigger. It does not assume $\mathrm{Sha}$ finite; it **proves** finiteness (Theorem A.2) via Kolyvagin, but only in the order-$\leq 1$ regime. It must be flagged that this finiteness theorem is regime-bounded and gives nothing for order $\geq 2$.
   - **Function-field mirage:** PASSES. This is genuinely the number-field (totally real) case. The geometry used is the arithmetic surface $\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}/\mathcal{O}_F$ with archimedean Arakelov contributions, not a geometric Frobenius over $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$. No function-field shortcut is imported; the hard archimedean intersection theory is done honestly.

4. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This note sits squarely on the proven side and, crucially, demonstrates that the proven side is intrinsically one-point even after maximal generalization. It sharpens [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md): the rank-2 replacement must produce two classes whose height-pairing matrix $\langle z_i, z_j\rangle$ has nonzero $2\times2$ determinant, and Zhang's machinery (a single Heegner orbit summed over $\operatorname{Gal}(H/E)$) structurally cannot do this, no matter which Shimura curve or totally real field is chosen.

5. **Feeds.** Research direction [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md) (fixes the precise object to beat), [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md) (the Kolyvagin Euler system is re-used verbatim, so it inherits the rank ceiling: a higher-rank Euler system would need a non-Heegner source), and [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md) (this is the archimedean template the $p$-adic Gross-Zagier formulae mirror). On the experimental side it underwrites the weak-BSD and strong-BSD-quantity tables ([`experiments/weak_bsd_table/`](../../../experiments/weak_bsd_table/), [`experiments/strong_bsd_quantities/`](../../../experiments/strong_bsd_quantities/)) for rank-1 curves as the ground truth, and the rank-1 vs rank-$\geq 2$ control split.
