# Reading note: J. Coates, A. Wiles, "On the conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer," Inventiones Mathematicae 39 (1977), 223-251.

> **Role in the BSD program.** This is the FIRST theorem in the direction of BSD: the historical root of the Euler-system / Iwasawa line (architectures 3 and 4 in the atlas). It proves one half of weak BSD in a restricted CM case: if $E$ has complex multiplication by the maximal order of an imaginary quadratic field $K$ of class number $1$, and $E$ is defined over $K$ or $\mathbb{Q}$, then $L(E/K, 1) \neq 0$ implies $E(F)$ is finite (Mordell-Weil rank $0$). It is the $L(1) \neq 0 \Rightarrow \mathrm{rank} = 0$ implication only. It does NOT prove the converse, does NOT compute Sha, and lives entirely in analytic rank $0$. Its method (a system of elliptic units constructed by Robert, mapped through explicit reciprocity laws to bound a Galois-cohomology / units quotient $U_0'/\bar{C}_0$) is the direct ancestor of Kolyvagin's Euler systems and of the Iwasawa main conjecture for CM curves (Rubin).

## One-line takeaway

For an elliptic curve $E$ with complex multiplication by the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}$ of an imaginary quadratic field $K$ of class number $1$, defined over $K$ or $\mathbb{Q}$, Coates and Wiles prove (Theorem 1) that if the Mordell-Weil rank $g_F \geq 1$ then $L(E/F, 1) = 0$; equivalently, $L(E/F, 1) \neq 0$ forces $E(F)$ finite. The mechanism is global, not analytic: a point of infinite order is fed into Galois extensions $H_n F_\infty / F_\infty$ that the explicit-reciprocity / formal-group machinery forces to be non-trivial and ramified, which via Robert's elliptic units (the CM analogue of cyclotomic units) forces $\Omega^{-1} L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi, 1)$ to be divisible by infinitely many distinct prime ideals, hence to be $0$. This is exactly half of weak BSD, in analytic rank $0$ only, for CM curves only, with NO claim about Sha and NO converse; it is the seed of the Euler-system line, not a proof of BSD.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Introduction and main theorem (pp. 223-224).** Let $F$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, $E/F$ an elliptic curve, $g_F$ the rank of $E(F)$ modulo torsion, and $L(E/F, s)$ the Hasse-Weil zeta function. The paper attacks the implication $g_F \geq 1 \Rightarrow L(E/F, s)$ vanishes at $s = 1$, which the authors call "the most accessible part of the conjecture." The main result:

> **Theorem 1** (p. 223). Assume $E$ has complex multiplication by the ring of integers of an imaginary quadratic field $K$ with class number $1$, and that $E$ is defined over $K$ or $\mathbb{Q}$. If $F$ is $K$ or $\mathbb{Q}$ and $g_F \geq 1$, then the Hasse-Weil zeta function $L(E/F, s)$ vanishes at $s = 1$.

The model case is $y^2 = x^3 - Dx$, the curves originally studied by Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, which have CM by the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Analytic continuation of $L(E/F, s)$ is NOT proved here; it is borrowed from Deuring, who identified the Hasse-Weil zeta function with a product of Hecke $L$-series with Grössencharacters. The class-number-$1$ hypothesis is stated to be "for technical convenience only" and the method is conjectured to extend (and was, by Arthaud, per the Note Added in Proof) to abelian base fields over $K$.

The authors flag the logical strength explicitly (p. 224): $L(E/F, 1)$ factors as a non-zero transcendental period times an algebraic number $L^*(E/F, 1)$, and the strategy (suggested by Razar) is to show a point of infinite order forces $L^*(E/F, 1)$ to be divisible by infinitely many primes, hence zero. The paper is written "in the spirit of Iwasawa's work" (ref [8]), using explicit reciprocity laws and analogues of Iwasawa's maps $\psi_n$. A crucial remark (p. 224): the authors suspect these laws "will play a vital role in future attempts to prove the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group of $E$ when $L(E/F, 1) \neq 0$," but explicitly state "We have not succeeded in proving this so far." So Sha is announced as open at the outset.

**Section 1: Notation (pp. 224-225).** $K$ imaginary quadratic, class number $1$, ring of integers $\mathcal{O}$; $\mathfrak{R}_\mathfrak{m}$ the ray class field of $K$ modulo $\mathfrak{m}$. $E/K$ has $\mathrm{End}(E) \cong \mathcal{O}$, Weierstrass model $y^2 = 4x^3 - g_2 x - g_3$ (1) with $g_2, g_3 \in \mathcal{O}$, period lattice $L = \Omega \mathcal{O}$. $\psi$ is the Grössencharacter of $E$ (Shimura, ref [16], 7.8) with conductor $\bar{\mathfrak{f}}$; $\mathfrak{f}$ is a fixed generator of $\bar{\mathfrak{f}}$. A rational prime $p \notin S$ ($S = \{2,3,\text{bad primes}\}$) splits in $K$ as $(p) = \mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$; $\pi = \psi(\mathfrak{p})$ is the Frobenius endomorphism mod $\mathfrak{p}$. $E_{\pi^{n+1}}$ are the $\pi^{n+1}$-division points, $F_n = K(E_{\pi^{n+1}})$, $G_n = \mathrm{Gal}(F_n/K) \cong (\mathcal{O}/\pi^{n+1})^\times$. The canonical character $\chi: G_0 \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ gives the Galois action on $E_\pi$. The eigenspace decomposition $A = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{p-1} A^{(k)}$ (2) for $\mathbb{Z}_p[G_0]$-modules is used throughout.

**Section 2: Fields of Division Points (pp. 225-227).**

> **Theorem 2** (p. 226). The elliptic curve $E$ has good reduction everywhere over $F_0 = K(E_\pi)$.

Proof via a variant of the Néron-Ogg-Shafarevich criterion (Ribet). This is what later lets the formal-group and ramification arguments run cleanly.

> **Lemma 3** (p. 226). The field $H = K(E_{\bar f})$ (division points by the conductor) coincides with the ray class field of $K$ modulo $\bar{\mathfrak{f}}$.

> **Lemma 4** (p. 227). The conductor of $F_n = K(E_{\pi^{n+1}})$ over $K$ is $\bar{\mathfrak{f}}_n = \bar{\mathfrak{f}} \mathfrak{p}^{n+1}$, and the ray class field $\mathfrak{R}_{\bar f_n}$ is the compositum $\mathfrak{R}_{\bar f_n} = F_n H$, with $F_n \cap H = K$.

**Section 3: Formal Groups (pp. 227-229).** $\hat{E}$ is the formal group of the reduction mod $\mathfrak{p}$, with local parameter $t = -2x/y$ and expansions $x = t^{-2} a(t)$, $y = -2 t^{-3} b(t)$ (3). The key move: $\hat{E}$ is a Lubin-Tate formal group over $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ (since $[\pi](t) \equiv t^p \bmod p\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$), so by Lubin-Tate (ref [10]) there is a simpler formal group $\mathscr{E}$ with $[\pi](w) = \pi w + w^p$, isomorphic to $\hat{E}$ over $\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ via $w = t + \sum_{k \geq 2} e_k t^k$ (5). The division points $\mathscr{E}_{\pi^{n+1}}$ replace $E_{\pi^{n+1}}$.

> **Lemma 5** (p. 228). $\Phi_n = K_\mathfrak{p}(E_{\pi^{n+1}}) = K_\mathfrak{p}(\mathscr{E}_{\pi^{n+1}})$ is a totally ramified extension of $K_\mathfrak{p}$ of degree $p^n(p-1)$; if $u_n$ generates $\mathscr{E}_{\pi^{n+1}}$ then its norm to $K_\mathfrak{p}$ is $\pi$, and $u_n$ is a local parameter for $\Phi_n$.

The logarithm $\lambda: \mathscr{E} \xrightarrow{\sim} G_a$ (6) over $K_\mathfrak{p}$ has $\lambda(w) = w + \sum_{n \geq 2} a_n w^n$ with $n a_n \in \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}$ and $a_n = 0$ unless $n \equiv 1 \bmod (p-1)$ (Lemma 7). The action of $G_0$ on $\mathscr{E}_\pi$ is $u^\sigma = [\chi(\sigma)](u) = \chi(\sigma) u$ (7).

**Section 4: Local Theory and the explicit reciprocity computation (pp. 229-238).** This is the technical engine. $U_0$ = units of $\Phi_0$ that are $\equiv 1 \bmod \mathfrak{p}_0$, a $\mathbb{Z}_p[G_0]$-module.

> **Lemma 9** (p. 230). For each $1 \leq k \leq p-1$ there exist $\eta_k \in U_0$ with $\eta_k \in U_0^{(k)}$ and $\eta_k \equiv 1 - u_0^k \bmod \mathfrak{p}_0^{k+1}$. If $\Phi_0$ contains no non-trivial $p$-th root of unity, the residue classes of $\eta_1, \dots, \eta_{p-1}$ form a basis of $U_0$ modulo $p$-th powers.

These $\eta_k$ are the local units that Robert's global elliptic units will hit. Homomorphisms $\varphi_k: U_0 \to \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}$ (8) (originating in Kummer's work and used by Robert) extract the relevant coefficient of $T(d/dT)\log f(T)$ from a power series $f$ representing a unit; they satisfy $\varphi_k(\alpha^\sigma) = \chi^k(\sigma)\varphi_k(\alpha)$ (9). Lemma 10 computes $\varphi_k(\eta_j)$: $= -k$ if $j = k$, else $0$.

> **Theorem 11** (p. 231). Let $\alpha$ be a non-zero element of $\mathscr{E}(\mathfrak{p})$, and for each $n \geq 0$ choose $\alpha_n$ in the maximal ideal of $\bar{K}_\mathfrak{p}$ with $[\pi^{n+1}](\alpha_n) = \alpha$. Then for all sufficiently large $n$, the extension $\Phi_\infty(\alpha_n)/\Phi_\infty$ is non-trivial and ramified.

This non-triviality-and-ramification statement is the local heart that the global proof of Theorem 1 (Lemma 35) will mirror. A simpler proof (pointed out by Lang and Mazur, replacing the original explicit-reciprocity argument) uses a cohomological lemma of Sah on $H^1(G_\infty, \mathscr{E}_{\pi^{n+1}})$.

> **Definition** (p. 231). $p$ is *anomalous* for $E$ if $\pi + \bar\pi \equiv 1 \bmod \mathfrak{p}$ (trace of Frobenius $\equiv 1$).

> **Lemma 12** (p. 231). For $p > 5$, $p$ is anomalous for $E$ iff $\Phi_0$ contains a non-trivial $p$-th root of unity. If $2$ ramifies or splits in $K$, anomalous primes do not exist.

This matters because the cleanest arguments need $\Phi_0$ to have no non-trivial $p$-th root of unity, i.e. $p$ non-anomalous. Crucially (p. 232), Dirichlet's theorem gives infinitely many primes $p$ that split in $K$ and are non-anomalous for $E$. That infinitude is what powers the final "divisible by infinitely many primes" conclusion.

The generalized norm residue symbol $(\ ,\ )_n: \mathscr{E}(\mathfrak{p}_n) \times \Phi_n^\times \to \mathscr{E}_{\pi^{n+1}}$ (Lemmas 13-16) is a non-degenerate pairing (Lemma 16, eq. (12)). The maps $\psi_n: \Phi_n'' \to \mathscr{X}_n / \pi^{n+1}\mathscr{X}_n$ (Lemma 14, eq. (11)) are the elliptic analogues of Iwasawa's $\psi_n$, satisfying $(\alpha, \beta)_n = [T_n(\lambda(\alpha)\psi_n(\beta))](u_n)$. The computation culminates in:

> **Theorem 18** (p. 236). With $u_0$ a generator of $\mathscr{E}_\pi$ and $\eta_1, \dots, \eta_{p-1}$ as in Lemma 9, assuming $\Phi_0$ contains no non-trivial $p$-th root of unity: for each $\alpha \in \mathscr{E}(\mathfrak{p}_0)$, $(\alpha, u_0)_0 = [\pi^{-1} T_0(u_0^{-1}\lambda(\alpha))](u_0)$ and $(\alpha, \eta_k)_0 = [-k\pi^{-1} T_0(u_0^{-1}\lambda(\alpha))](u_0)$ for $1 \leq k \leq p-2$.

> **Corollary 19** (p. 237). $\psi_0(u_0) = \pi^{-1} u_0^{-1}$ and $\psi_0(\eta_k) = -k \pi^{-1} u_0^{k-1}$ ($1 \leq k \leq p-2$) mod $p X_0$.

This explicit value of $\psi_0$ on the local units is what connects, in Section 5, to the $\mathfrak{p}_0$-adic logarithmic derivative of the global elliptic units and hence to the special $L$-value.

**Section 5: Elliptic Units (pp. 238-249).** Following Robert (ref [12]), one builds global units that are the CM analogues of cyclotomic units. From the Weierstrass $\sigma$-function and a twelfth-power theta function $\theta(z, \mathscr{L})$, define $\Theta(z, \mathfrak{a}) = \theta(z, L)^{N\mathfrak{a}} / \theta(z, \mathfrak{a}^{-1}L)$ (23). For admissible pairs $(A, \mathcal{N})$ with $\sum n_j(N\mathfrak{a}_j - 1) = 0$, the products $\Theta(\rho_n, A, \mathcal{N})$ (25) generate the group $\mathscr{C}_n$ of elliptic units of $\mathfrak{R}_n$ (Lemma 20: $\mathscr{C}_n$ is Galois-stable and independent of the chosen division point $\rho_n$). $C_n$ = the elliptic units of $F_n$ = norms from $\mathfrak{R}_n$.

The bridge to $L$-values is the $\mathfrak{p}_0$-adic logarithmic derivative. Defining $\Lambda(z, \mathfrak{a}) = \prod_{b \in B}\Theta(z + \psi(b)\rho, \mathfrak{a})$ (30):

> **Lemma 21** (p. 241). $\Lambda(z, \mathfrak{a})$ is a rational function of $\wp(z), \wp'(z)$ with coefficients in $K$, and $z \frac{d}{dz}\log\Lambda(z, \mathfrak{a}) = \sum_{k \geq 1} c_k(\mathfrak{a}) z^k$ where $c_k(\mathfrak{a}) = 12(-1)^{k-1}\rho^{-k}(N\mathfrak{a} - \bar\psi^k(\mathfrak{a})) L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi^k, k)$ (31).

This is the formula that makes special values of the Hecke $L$-function $L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi^k, k)$ literally the Taylor coefficients of the log-derivative of an elliptic unit. Corollary 22 (a case of Damerell): $\Omega^{-k} L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi^k, k) \in K$. Expanding $\Lambda$ in the Lubin-Tate parameter $w$ (Lemma 23, $\Lambda(z, \mathfrak{a}) = \sum h_k(\mathfrak{a}) t^k$ with $h_0$ a unit) and matching coefficients gives, after the norm map $N = N_{\mathfrak{R}_0/F_0}$:

> **Theorem 27** (p. 246). For $1 \leq k \leq p-1$, $\varphi_k\big(N\Theta(\tau + \rho, A, \mathcal{N})\big) = \lambda_k L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi^k, k)\big(\sum_j n_j(N\mathfrak{a}_j - \bar\psi^k(\mathfrak{a}_j))\big) \bmod \mathfrak{p}$, with $\lambda_k = 12(-1)^{k-1}\rho^{-k}$.

> **Lemma 28** (p. 246). For $1 \leq k < p-1$ there exists an admissible $(A, \mathcal{N})$ with $\sum_j n_j(N\mathfrak{a}_j - \bar\psi^k(\mathfrak{a}_j)) \not\equiv 0 \bmod \mathfrak{p}$.

These combine into the main structural theorem of the section, which bounds the Iwasawa-style quotient by the $L$-value:

> **Theorem 29** (p. 246). Assume $p > 5$ satisfies (i) $p \notin S$, (ii) $p$ splits in $K$, (iii) $p$ non-anomalous for $E$. Let $\bar{C}_0$ be the $\mathfrak{p}_0$-adic closure of the elliptic units $C_0$. Then for $1 \leq k \leq p-2$, the eigenspace $(U_0'/\bar{C}_0)^{(k)}$ is non-trivial if and only if $\Omega^{-k} L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi^k, k) \equiv 0 \bmod \mathfrak{p}$.

That iff is the Euler-system-style bound in embryo: a units-modulo-elliptic-units quotient (the local incarnation of a Selmer/class-group object) is controlled exactly by a special $L$-value mod $\mathfrak{p}$. Theorem 31 (p. 248) and Corollary 32 (p. 249) pass to the Iwasawa limit $X_\infty = \varprojlim \Phi_n'/\Phi_n'^{p^t}$, giving $Y_\infty/(\gamma^{p^n} - 1)Y_\infty \cong U_n'/\bar{C}_n^*$ and the statement that $(U_0'/\bar{C}_0)^{(k)}$ is non-trivial iff $(U_\infty'/\bar{C}_\infty)^{(k)}$ is.

**Section 6: Proof of Theorem 1 (pp. 249-251).** Now the global input. Suppose for contradiction $E(K)$ has a point $P$ of infinite order. For each $n$, pick $Q_n \in E(\bar{K})$ with $[\pi^{n+1}]Q_n = P$, and form $H_n = F_n(Q_n)$, a cyclic extension of $F_n$ of degree dividing $p^{n+1}$, unramified outside $\mathfrak{p}_n$ (Lemma 33), with $x^\sigma = \chi(\sigma) x$ for $x \in \mathrm{Gal}(H_n/F_n)$ (48).

> **Lemma 35** (p. 250). For all sufficiently large $n$, the extension $H_n F_\infty / F_\infty$ is non-trivial and ramified.

This is the global mirror of Theorem 11. The point of infinite order is exactly what guarantees $\beta P$ is non-torsion in the formal group, forcing the extension to be non-trivial. Combined with the explicit description of $\mathrm{Gal}(M_n/L_n F_n)$ (Theorem 34, via Artin map to units $U_n'/\bar{E}_n$), the non-triviality of $H_n L_n F_\infty / L_n F_\infty$ forces (eq. (49)):
$$(U_n'/\bar{E}_n)^{(1)} \neq 0, \qquad \text{hence} \qquad (U_n'/\bar{C}_n)^{(1)} \neq 0 \ \text{(since } C_n \subseteq E_n\text{)}.$$
By Corollary 32, this means $\Omega^{-1} L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi, 1)$ is divisible by $\mathfrak{p}$. But there are infinitely many primes $p$ splitting in $K$, non-anomalous for $E$, and outside $S$ (the Dirichlet argument from Section 4). Each such $p$ contributes a distinct prime $\mathfrak{p}$ dividing $\Omega^{-1} L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi, 1)$. A non-zero algebraic number cannot be divisible by infinitely many distinct primes, so
$$\Omega^{-1} L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi, 1) = 0.$$
By Shimura (ref [16], Theorem 7.42), $L(E/K, s) = L_{\bar f}(\psi, s) L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi, s)$, so $L(E/K, 1) = 0$, proving Theorem 1 for $F = K$. For $F = \mathbb{Q}$, $L_f(\psi, s) = L_{\bar f}(\bar\psi, s)$ and the Hasse-Weil function of $E/\mathbb{Q}$ equals $L_f(\psi, s)$ up to finitely many non-vanishing Euler factors. This completes the proof of Theorem 1 (p. 251).

**Note Added in Proof (p. 251).** Theorem 1 was generalized by Nicole Arthaud to CM curves over an abelian extension $F$ of an arbitrary imaginary quadratic field $K$ (dropping class number $1$), under two hypotheses: $F$ abelian over $K$, and the torsion points generate over $F$ an abelian extension of $K$.

## Project mapping

1. **Proven, exactly.** One implication of weak BSD, in a restricted setting: $E$ has CM by $\mathcal{O}_K$ with $h_K = 1$, $E/K$ or $E/\mathbb{Q}$, and $g_F \geq 1 \Rightarrow L(E/F, 1) = 0$ (Theorem 1). Contrapositive: $L(E/F, 1) \neq 0 \Rightarrow E(F)$ has rank $0$. This is genuine, unconditional (given Deuring's analytic continuation and Robert's elliptic units), and over a number field.

2. **The gap (what is NOT proven).** (a) No converse: this does NOT show $L(E/F, 1) = 0 \Rightarrow g_F \geq 1$. (b) No Sha: the authors explicitly say (p. 224) they could not prove finiteness of Sha when $L(1) \neq 0$, and announce it as a hope, not a result. (c) No leading-coefficient / strong-BSD formula. (d) No rank $\geq 1$ analytic content: the argument is purely "rank $\geq 1$ algebraic $\Rightarrow L = 0$ analytic," which only certifies analytic rank $0$ on the curves where $L(1) \neq 0$. (e) CM only: the entire method depends on the CM structure (Grössencharacter, elliptic units, Lubin-Tate formal group). There is no non-CM analogue here.

3. **Exact regime.** Analytic rank $0$, and only the "$L(1) \neq 0$" side. The arithmetic is heavily $p$-adic (Lubin-Tate formal groups, $\mathfrak{p}$-adic units, Iwasawa limits over the cyclotomic-type tower $F_\infty$), but the conclusion is about the archimedean / complex special value $L(E/F, 1)$, reached through the $\mathfrak{p}_0$-adic logarithmic derivative (Lemma 21, Theorem 27) that ties special values of Hecke $L$-functions to elliptic-unit coefficients. So it is the archetype of "$p$-adic machinery proving an archimedean non-vanishing consequence," which is exactly the tension catalogued in [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md).

4. **Detector scorecard.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES (does not trigger).** The conclusion is honest non-vanishing of $L(1)$, equivalently exact rank $0$ on the $L(1) \neq 0$ locus, not a statement about $\mathrm{rank} \bmod 2$. The root number plays no role; nothing here reduces to parity. It is strictly stronger than parity on the curves it covers (it gives rank $= 0$, not just rank even), but it is also strictly weaker than full BSD (no converse, no Sha).
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES (clean), and is informative.** The argument never assumes $\#\mathrm{Sha}$ finite; it bounds a units quotient $U_n'/\bar{C}_n$ by an $L$-value and derives rank $0$ by an infinitude-of-primes contradiction. This is precisely why it is unconditional. The authors flag the OPEN direction (finiteness of Sha) as the thing they could not do, which is the right honest posture: the later CM main conjecture (Rubin) is what eventually controls Sha here, and it is downstream, not in this paper.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): PASSES (clean).** The proof uses no geometric Frobenius of a surface and no function-field structure. The only Frobenius is the arithmetic Frobenius endomorphism $\pi = \psi(\mathfrak{p})$ of the CM curve, an honestly number-field object. The elliptic units are genuine global units of ray class fields of $K$. There is no $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ shortcut hiding here; the method does not transport verbatim to function fields and claims nothing there.

5. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This paper sits at the easy end of the proven arm: it handles only rank $0$ (the $L(1) \neq 0$ side) and only for CM curves. It is BELOW the Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin rank-$0$-and-$1$ ceiling in generality (CM only) but is its historical ancestor. Critically, the method is structurally rank-$0$-bound: the argument turns a single point of infinite order into a contradiction with non-vanishing. It produces no points and bounds no rank-$\geq 2$ structure. The "one missing object" of the atlas (a rank-$\geq 2$ construction or an unconditional Sha bound in the open regime) is entirely untouched; this is a non-existence (rank $0$) argument, the opposite move from constructing independent points.

6. **What it feeds.** Directly the Euler-system / Iwasawa architectures (atlas 3, 4) and [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md): Theorem 29's "units-mod-elliptic-units quotient is controlled by an $L$-value mod $\mathfrak{p}$" is the prototype of an Euler-system bound on a Selmer-type object, the pattern Kolyvagin (Heegner points), Kato (zeta elements), and Rubin (CM main conjecture, which closes Sha here) all instantiate. The explicit reciprocity laws (Section 4, Wiles ref [18]) and the $\mathfrak{p}_0$-adic logarithmic derivative (Lemma 21) feed [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md) as the cleanest historical example of the $p$-adic-to-archimedean bridge. For [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md) it is a cautionary boundary marker: this entire line, however deep, is a one-point / rank-$0$ machine and gives no purchase on the rank-$\geq 2$ object. No current experiment in [`experiments/PLAN.md`](../../../experiments/PLAN.md) reproduces this; the natural numerical hook is a check on $y^2 = x^3 - Dx$ (Gaussian-integer CM) that $L(E, 1) \neq 0$ coincides with rank $0$, the family Coates-Wiles and Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer originally tabulated.
