# Reading note: J. Coates, "Lectures on the Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture," Notices of the ICCM 1 (2013), no. 2, 29-46.

> **Role in the BSD program.** A modern expository survey written from inside the Iwasawa-theoretic / CM tradition. It does three things: (1) it states the conjecture cleanly in all three strengths (weak, parity, full exact formula) and pins down exactly which is proven and which is open; (2) it gives an honest census of the proven regime (Kolyvagin plus Gross-Zagier for analytic rank $\leq 1$, plus $p$-part results via Iwasawa theory for $r_E = 0$); and (3) it walks through, in self-contained detail, the CM machinery (Grossencharacters, Kronecker-Eisenstein series, elliptic units) used by Zhao and by Tian to attack the $2$-part of BSD and to produce the first explicit infinite family of curves with a simple zero at $s=1$. ## One-line takeaway

Coates surveys BSD as three nested conjectures (weak $r_E = g_E$; parity $r_E \equiv g_E \bmod 2$; full exact formula for $\mathscr{L}_E$), states with care that only the weak and full forms are proven, and only for analytic rank $r_E \leq 1$ (Theorem 24, Kolyvagin plus Gross-Zagier, which also gives $\mathrm{Sha}$ finite there), stresses that for $r_E \geq 2$ (in particular $g_E \geq 2$) "it has never been proven for a single elliptic curve $E$" that even the rational-number prediction $\mathscr{L}_E / (c_\infty(E) R_\infty(E)) \in \mathbb{Q}$ holds, and then spends the second half developing the CM technology (Grossencharacters, Kronecker-Eisenstein series, elliptic-unit averaging) by which Zhao attacks the $2$-part for $r_E = 0$ twists and Tian produces the first explicit infinite family of quadratic twists of $X_0(49)$ with a genuine simple zero at $s=1$ (rank $1$ and finite $\mathrm{Sha}$ of odd order). It is a clean statement of the coordinates: everything lives at rank $\leq 1$, the $2$-part and rank $\geq 2$ are the open core, and the CM case is where the only new ground is currently being won.

## Technical content (section by section)

**Introduction (p. 29).** Frames BSD as the modern analogue of Dirichlet's 1839 use of $L$-functions to get an exact class-number formula (Theorem 1, Dirichlet, p. 29: $h(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})) = -\frac{1}{p}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1} a\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)$ for $p \equiv 3 \bmod 4$). Notes that BSD is "still largely unproven" and flags the live front: Ye Tian's recent generalization of Heegner's old work to one family of quadratic twists of $y^2 = x^3 - x$, and the joint work of Coates-Li-Tian-Zhai on $E = X_0(49)$, $y^2 + xy = x^3 - x^2 - 2x - 1$.

**Classical Descent Theory (pp. 29-30).** Recalls Mordell's theorem (Theorem 2: $E(\mathbb{Q})$ finitely generated) and stresses the algorithmic gap: "theoretically no algorithm for calculating $E(\mathbb{Q})$ in a finite number of steps has ever been proven." Definitions: $g_E = \dim_\mathbb{Q}(E(\mathbb{Q}) \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Q})$ the rank (Def. 3); $\mathrm{Sha}(E) = \ker(H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E) \to \bigoplus_v H^1(\mathbb{Q}_v, E))$ the Tate-Shafarevich group (Def. 5); $\mathcal{S}el_m(E) = j_m^{-1}(\mathrm{Sha}(E)[m])$ the $m$-Selmer group (Def. 6), fitting in $0 \to E(\mathbb{Q})/mE(\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathcal{S}el_m(E) \to \mathrm{Sha}(E)[m] \to 0$. Theorem 7: $\mathcal{S}el_m(E)$ is finite for all $m > 1$ (the one thing always computable, in principle). Theorem 8 (Cassels-Tate): there is a canonical non-degenerate alternating bilinear form on $\mathrm{Sha}(E)/\mathrm{Sha}(E)_{\mathrm{div}}$, whence (Cor. 9) $(\mathrm{Sha}(E)/\mathrm{Sha}(E)_{\mathrm{div}})[p]$ has even $\mathbb{F}_p$-dimension and (Cor. 10) if $\mathrm{Sha}(E)_{\mathrm{div}}(p) = 0$ then $\#(\mathrm{Sha}(E)(p))$ is a square.

> **Conjecture (finiteness of Sha)** (p. 30). The group $\mathrm{Sha}(E)$ is finite. Coates: "To settle this conjecture is unquestionably one of the major problems of number theory. However, it has never been proven so far for a single elliptic curve with $g_E \geq 2$."

This is the survey's first explicit naming of the open core. He records (p. 30, after Theorem 11, T. and V. Dokchitser: the parity of $t_{E,p} = \mathrm{ord}$ of the divisible part does not depend on $p$) the conditional Lemma 12: if $\mathcal{S}el_2(E)/\mathrm{Im}(E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}})$ has order $2$, then either (i) $g_E = 1$ and $\mathrm{Sha}(E)(2) = 0$, or (ii) $g_E = 0$, $\mathrm{Sha}(E)(2) = \mathbb{Q}_2/\mathbb{Z}_2$, and $\mathrm{Sha}(E)(p) \supset \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ for every odd prime $p$ (so case (ii) forces $\mathrm{Sha}(E)$ infinite, which is exactly the possibility one wants to rule out). The largest prime $p$ for which $\mathrm{Sha}(E)(p) \neq 0$ is currently known to occur is $p = 1627$ (Z. Liang and D. Wei, for $E: y^2 = x^3 - (7173305747)^2 x$, with $\#\mathrm{Sha}(E)(1627) = 1627^2$).

**$L$-Functions (pp. 30-31).** Defines $L(E,s) = \prod_{p \mid \Delta}(1 - t_p p^{-s})^{-1} \prod_{(p,\Delta)=1}(1 - t_p p^{-s} + p^{1-2s})^{-1}$ with $t_p = p + 1 - A_p$, converging for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 3/2$. Theorem 14 (Wiles et al.): the function $f_E(\tau) = \sum c_n q^n$ is a primitive cusp form of weight $2$ for $\Gamma_0(C(E))$. Cor. 15: $\Lambda(E,s) = C(E)^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(E,s)$ analytically continues to an entire function with functional equation $\Lambda(E,s) = w_E \Lambda(E, 2-s)$, $w_E = \pm 1$. The root number $w_E$ gives the parity of the order of vanishing immediately. Cor. 16: there is a non-constant rational map $\varphi: X_0(C(E)) \to E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Example 18: $X_0(49) \to E$ is an isomorphism). Coates remarks (p. 31) that for CM curves there is a totally different, 19th-century (Eisenstein-Kronecker) route to continuation and functional equation, and that "There seems to be no analogue of this method for elliptic curves without complex multiplication."

**The Conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (pp. 31-32).** Def. 19: $r_E = \mathrm{ord}_{s=1} L(E,s)$ (analytic rank).

> **Weak Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture** (p. 31). For all $E/\mathbb{Q}$, $r_E = g_E$.
>
> **Strong Parity Conjecture** (p. 31). For all $E/\mathbb{Q}$, $r_E \equiv g_E \bmod 2$. Implied by weak BSD; since the functional equation gives $w_E = (-1)^{r_E}$, this would force $E(\mathbb{Q})$ infinite whenever $w_E = -1$, "of which very little in general is still known."

The best unconditional result toward parity is recorded as the **Weak Parity Theorem** (T. and V. Dokchitser [5], p. 32): for all $E/\mathbb{Q}$ and all primes $p$, $r_E \equiv t_{E,p} + g_E \bmod 2$. Cor. 22: $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\mathfrak{S}_p(E)$ is even iff $w_E = +1$. The survey is careful that this is parity only, not rank.

> **Theorem 24** (Kolyvagin [9], Gross-Zagier [7]) (p. 32). Assume $r_E \leq 1$. Then $r_E = g_E$ and $\mathrm{Sha}(E)$ is finite.

Coates immediately stresses the boundary (p. 32): "while it is easy to decide in numerical examples whether or not $r_E \leq 1$, very little is still known about establishing this assertion theoretically for large families of elliptic curves." This is the proven ceiling, named as a ceiling.

**The Exact Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer Formula (pp. 32-33).** Defines $\mathscr{L}_E$ by $L(E,s) = \mathscr{L}_E (s-1)^{r_E} + \text{higher order}$. Regulator $R_\infty(E) = \det\langle P_i, P_j \rangle$ (Néron-Tate height pairing, Defs. 25-26), Tamagawa factors $c_p(E) = [E(\mathbb{Q}_p) : E_0(\mathbb{Q}_p)]$ (Def. 28; Lemma 29: $= \mathrm{ord}_p(\Delta)$ for split multiplicative reduction, $\leq 4$ otherwise), and $c_\infty(E) = \delta_E \Omega_E$ (Def. 27).

> **Full Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture** (p. 32). $r_E = g_E$, $\mathrm{Sha}(E)$ finite, and
> $$\frac{\mathscr{L}_E}{c_\infty(E)} = \frac{\#(\mathrm{Sha}(E)) R_\infty(E)}{\#(E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}})^2} \prod_{p \mid C(E)} c_p(E).$$

The rationality consequence (p. 33): the full conjecture predicts $\mathscr{L}_E / (c_\infty(E) R_\infty(E)) \in \mathbb{Q}$. Theorem 31: if $r_E \leq 1$, then $\mathscr{L}_E / (c_\infty(E) R_\infty(E)) \in \mathbb{Q}$ (the hard case $r_E = 1$ is Gross-Zagier). Coates is careful (p. 33) that the hypothesis is the ANALYTIC rank $r_E \leq 1$, and that the reverse direction is genuinely open: "it is unknown at present how to prove that $g_E \leq 1$ implies that $r_E = g_E$." Under the strictly stronger hypothesis $g_E \leq 1$ AND $\mathrm{Sha}(E)$ finite, some $r_E = g_E$ results follow from Iwasawa theory (for instance when $E$ has CM). That added finiteness hypothesis is precisely the open input flagged by detector 2, and the analytic-versus-Mordell-Weil asymmetry is why the proven regime is keyed to $r_E$, not $g_E$.

> Coates, verbatim (p. 33): "When $r_E \geq 2$ (in particular, when $g_E \geq 2$), we stress that it has never been proven for a single elliptic curve $E$ that $\mathscr{L}_E / c_\infty(E) \in \mathbb{Q}$. Of course, numerically it seems always to be true, but no proof is known for a single $E$."

This is the cleanest possible statement of the rank $\geq 2$ wall: not even the rationality shadow of the exact formula is known for one curve of rank $\geq 2$. For $r_E = 0$, define $L^{(\mathrm{alg})}(E,1) = L(E,1)/c_\infty(E) \in \mathbb{Q}$ (Kolyvagin gives finiteness here). The $p$-part of full BSD for $r_E = 0$ (p. 33, with $\mathrm{Tam}(E) = \prod_{q \mid C(E)} c_q(E)$): $\mathrm{ord}_p(L^{(\mathrm{alg})}(E,1)) = \mathrm{ord}_p(\mathrm{Tam}(E)) - 2\,\mathrm{ord}_p(\#E(\mathbb{Q})) + \mathrm{ord}_p(\#\mathrm{Sha}(E))$.

> **Theorem 32** (Rubin) (p. 33). Assume $L(E,1) \neq 0$ and $E$ has CM. Then the $p$-part of BSD holds for all $p \neq 2$ (and $p \neq 3$ if CM by $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$).
>
> **Theorem 33** (Kato, Skinner-Urban) (p. 33). Assume $L(E,1) \neq 0$. Then the $p$-part of BSD holds for all good ordinary primes outside some specified list, which certainly includes $p = 2$.

So the $2$-part is "largely unknown," even at $r_E = 0$, even with CM. Coates then sets up the motivation for the rest of the lectures: knowing the $2$-part is vital to generalize Tian's argument and prove, for infinite families of twists with $w_E = -1$, that the $L$-series has a simple zero (i.e., to manufacture rank $1$ and feed Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin).

**Quadratic Twists of an Elliptic Curve (pp. 33-35).** $E^{(N)}$ the twist by $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N})/\mathbb{Q}$; root numbers satisfy $w_{E^{(N)}} = \chi_N(-C(E)) w_E$. For $E = X_0(49)$, Example 34 records $L^{(\mathrm{alg})}(E^{(N)},1) = 1/2$ when nonzero, and a table (p. 34) of $L^{(\mathrm{alg})}(E^{(N)},1)$ values from Z. Liang's MAGMA computation, mostly powers of $2$. Behavior of Tamagawa factors under twisting (pp. 34-35): Lemmas 36-40 compute $\mathrm{ord}_2(c_p(E^{(N)}))$ via reduction type, isolating that the interesting variation for $p \mid N$ depends only on the splitting of $p$ in the CM field $K$ and on $t_p \bmod 2$.

**Basic Facts About a Class of CM Elliptic Curves (Following Deuring) (pp. 35-37).** $K$ imaginary quadratic, class number $1$, $\mathcal{O}_K \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{End}_K(E)$. Lemma 41: for $v \notin S_E$ there is a unique $\pi_v \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with $\bar{\pi}_v = \varphi_v$ (Frobenius). Def. 42 builds the Grossencharacter $\psi_E$; Theorem 43 (Deuring): $\psi_E$ is a Hecke Grossencharacter with conductor $\mathfrak{f}$ whose prime divisors are exactly the bad primes of $E/K$. Theorem 45: $L(E/K,s) = L(\psi_E, s) L(\overline{\psi}_E, s)$, and over $\mathbb{Q}$, $L(\psi_E, s) = L(E,s)$. Prop. 50: $C(E) = |d_K| N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{f}$.

**Expression for the $L$-Series in Terms of Eisenstein Series (pp. 36-38).** The 19th-century Kronecker-Eisenstein route. Def. 52: $H(z,s,L) = \sum_{w \in L} \frac{\overline{z+w}}{|z+w|^{2s}}$ (converges $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 3/2$, continues to all of $\mathbb{C}$). Theorem 53 expresses the imprimitive Hecke $L$-function $L_S(\overline{\psi}_E, s)$ as a finite sum of $H$ values. Def. 54: $\mathscr{E}_1^*(z,L) = H(z,1,L)$ (value of the analytic continuation at $s=1$). Theorem 55 (Eisenstein): $\mathscr{E}_1^*(z,L) = \zeta(z,L) - z s_2(L) - \bar{z} A(L)^{-1}$ (note: not holomorphic in $z$). The functions $B_m(z,L)$ (Def., p. 37) connect $\mathscr{E}_1^*(mz,L)$ to $\wp, \wp'$; Lemmas 56-57 establish the recursion and Swinnerton-Dyer's identity $\mathscr{E}_1^*(u,L) = -B_{m-1}(u,L)/m$ for $u$ of exact order $m \geq 3$ in $\mathbb{C}/L$. The upshot (Theorem 59, p. 38): $L_S(\overline{\psi}_E, 1)/\Omega_\infty = g^{-1} \sum_{\mathfrak{b}} \mathscr{E}_1^*(\psi_E(\mathfrak{b})\Omega_\infty/g, \mathscr{L})$, an algebraic special-value formula.

**Behaviour of Tamagawa Factors / CM Curves with Good Reduction at 2 (pp. 38-42).** Restricts to CM curves with $\mathrm{End}_K(E) = \mathcal{O}_K$ and good reduction at all $v \mid 2$ (Example 69: $X_0(49)$, $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$, $2$ splits, good ordinary reduction at $2$). The key averaging lemma (Theorem 67, p. 39) sums $L_{S_n}(\overline{\psi}_M, 1)/\Omega_\infty$ over divisors $M$ of $\mathfrak{g}_n$ and equals $2^n \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{R}_n/\mathscr{I}_n}(g_n^{-1}\mathscr{E}_1^*(\Omega_\infty/g_n, \mathscr{L}))$. Theorem 72 (p. 39): $\mathrm{ord}_v(\Lambda_n(E)) \geq -1$ for all $v \mid 2$, with $\geq 0$ if $2 \mid a_1$; this integrality (best possible, e.g. $L(\overline{\psi},1)/\Omega_\infty = 1/2$ for $X_0(49)$) is the engine for the $2$-part weak results. "Special split primes" $p = \pi\bar{\pi}$ with $\pi \equiv 1 \bmod 4$ (Def. 84, Lemma 87: positive density) play the role of admissible Heegner primes; by Lemma 39, $c_p(E^{(N)}) = 4$ at such primes.

**On the $2$-part of BSD (pp. 42-43).** The weak $2$-part results for admissible twists $Ad(E)$ (Def., $N$ squarefree, $(N,C(E))=1$, $w_{E^{(N)}} = +1$, $N$ a product of split primes $\equiv 1 \bmod 4$): Theorem 88: if $\mathrm{ord}_2(L(E,1)/\Omega_\infty) = -1$, then $\mathrm{ord}_2(L(E^{(N)},1)/\Omega_N) \geq n(N) - 1$ for all $N \in Ad(E)$. Theorem 89: with $N$ a product of $k$ distinct primes splitting completely in $\mathbb{Q}(E[4])$, $\mathrm{ord}_2(L(E^{(N)},1)/\Omega_N) \geq 2k$. These match the BSD prediction (3): $\mathrm{ord}_2(L^{(\mathrm{alg})}(E^{(N)},1)) = n(N) - 1 + \mathrm{ord}_2(\#\mathrm{Sha}(E^{(N)}))$ when $L(E^{(N)},1) \neq 0$. Coates flags this is exactly Zhao's CM method ([15]-[18]), which "really requires $E$ to have complex multiplication."

**Tian's Argument for $E = X_0(49)$ (pp. 43-46).** The new content. General Problem (p. 43): find a large explicit infinite family $\mathcal{J}(E)$ of squarefree $N$ with $L(E^{(N)}, s)$ having a simple zero at $s=1$. Heegner points on $X_0(49)$ via CM by $\mathcal{O}_K$, $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$. For $M \in \mathscr{D}(N)$ define non-primitive Heegner points $u_{N,M} = \sum_{\sigma \in G}\mathcal{X}_M(\sigma)\sigma(w_N)$ (Def. 94). The Gross-Zagier formula is invoked as Theorem 95 (p. 44): $L'(E/K_N, \mathcal{X}_M, 1) = 16\lambda(E) N^{-1/2}\hat{h}(u_{N,M})$. Combined with the factorization Lemma 96 and Theorem 95, Corollary 97 (p. 44): if $u_{N,M}$ has infinite order, then $u_M$ has infinite order, $E(K_M)$ has rank $1$, and $L(E^{(N/M)},1) \neq 0$. The arithmetic criterion that forces infinite order is the ideal class group condition (no element of order $4$). The capstone:

> **Theorem 91** (p. 43, full proof on pp. 45-46). Let $E = X_0(49)$ and $N = p_0 p_1 \cdots p_k$ with (i) $p_0 \equiv 3 \bmod 4$, $p_0 \neq 7$, $p_0$ a quadratic non-residue mod $7$, (ii) $p_1, \dots, p_k$ split completely in $\mathbb{Q}(E[4])$, and (iii) the ideal class group $C_N$ of $F_N = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-N})$ has no element of order $4$. Then $L(E^{(-N)}, s)$ has a simple zero at $s = 1$, $E^{(-N)}(\mathbb{Q})$ has rank $1$, and $\mathrm{Sha}(E^{(-N)})$ is finite of odd order.

Coates is explicit (p. 43) about the logic: once $L(E^{(-N)}, s)$ is shown to have a simple zero, Kolyvagin's Theorem 24 gives rank $1$ and finite $\mathrm{Sha}$; an additional classical $2$-descent argument (the $2$-part work of the earlier sections) shows the $2$-primary part of $\mathrm{Sha}$ is zero, hence $\mathrm{Sha}$ has odd order. Theorem 98 (p. 45) gives the exact ratio of canonical heights $\hat{h}(u_{N,M})/\hat{h}(u_M) = L^{(\mathrm{alg})}(E^{(N/M)},1)/L^{(\mathrm{alg})}(E^{(1)},1)$, and Theorem 99 the lower bound $\mathrm{ord}_2(L^{(\mathrm{alg})}(E^{(N/M)},1)) \geq k(N) - k(M)$ when $L(E^{(N/M)},1) \neq 0$. The proof is by induction on $k(N)$ over a commutative diagram of Selmer/cohomology groups and a Lubin-Tate ramification argument at $2$ (Lemma 102: $E(\mathscr{I}_N)[2^\infty] \subseteq E[2](\mathbb{Q})$).

## Project mapping

1. **What is PROVEN, stated by the survey.** (a) Theorem 24: $r_E \leq 1 \Rightarrow r_E = g_E$ and $\mathrm{Sha}(E)$ finite (Kolyvagin + Gross-Zagier). (b) Theorem 31: the rationality shadow $\mathscr{L}_E/(c_\infty R_\infty) \in \mathbb{Q}$ for $r_E \leq 1$. (c) Theorem 32 (Rubin) and Theorem 33 (Kato, Skinner-Urban): the $p$-part of full BSD for $p$ outside specified lists, when $L(E,1) \neq 0$ (so $r_E = 0$). (d) Theorem 91 (Coates-Li-Tian-Zhai): a concrete infinite family of twists of $X_0(49)$ with simple zero, rank $1$, odd finite $\mathrm{Sha}$. Every one of these is rank $\leq 1$, and the survey says so plainly.

2. **The GAP, named in the source's own words.** $r_E \geq 2$ (in particular $g_E \geq 2$): "it has never been proven for a single elliptic curve $E$ that $\mathscr{L}_E/c_\infty(E) \in \mathbb{Q}$" (p. 33). Finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$: "never been proven so far for a single elliptic curve with $g_E \geq 2$" (p. 30). The $2$-part of BSD: "largely unknown, even though it has been verified numerically in vast numbers of cases" (p. 33). This survey is, for our purposes, a high-authority external confirmation of the exact coordinates the repo's stance is built on.

3. **EXACT regime.** Analytic rank $0$ and $1$ for the rank/Sha theorems (Theorem 24). The CM $2$-part material (Theorems 88-91) is rank $0$ ($w = +1$ twists, $L(1) \neq 0$) and rank $1$ ($w = -1$ twists, simple zero), never rank $\geq 2$. Mixed archimedean/$p$-adic: the special-value formulas (Theorems 53, 59) and Gross-Zagier (Theorem 95) are archimedean; the $2$-part integrality (Theorem 72) and the Iwasawa $p$-part results (Theorems 32, 33) are $p$-adic. The survey is an explicit catalogue of the $p$-adic-to-archimedean bridge, directly feeding [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md).

4. **Detector scorecard.**
   - **Detector 1 (parity-only): PASSES, and is exemplary about the distinction.** The survey separates the Strong Parity Conjecture and the Weak Parity Theorem (Dokchitser-Dokchitser, $r_E \equiv t_{E,p} + g_E \bmod 2$) from the full rank equality, and states clearly that parity "would force $E(\mathbb{Q})$ infinite when $w_E = -1$" but that this is open in general. Theorem 91's content is a genuine simple zero (analytic rank exactly $1$), not a parity statement: it produces an actual point of infinite order via Heegner, then Kolyvagin. No claim here collapses to parity.
   - **Detector 2 (Sha-finiteness assumed): PASSES (clean), and is a model of honesty.** Finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ is never assumed; it is stated as the open Conjecture (p. 30) and is a CONCLUSION (Theorem 24 in rank $\leq 1$, Theorem 91 with odd order) reached by Euler-system / descent input. The survey flags the rank $\geq 2$ failure of any $\mathrm{Sha}$ result explicitly. This is the correct posture and a useful citation anchor for the project's discipline.
   - **Detector 3 (function-field mirage): PASSES (clean).** Everything is over $\mathbb{Q}$ and imaginary quadratic $K$. The only Frobenius is the arithmetic Frobenius endomorphism $\pi_v$ of the CM curve (Lemma 41), an honest number-field object, and the Grossencharacter is a Hecke character of $K$, not a geometric Frobenius of a surface. No function-field shortcut is imported; the CM mechanism is the number-field substitute for the geometry the general case lacks, and the survey says (p. 31) there is "no analogue of this method for elliptic curves without complex multiplication."

5. **Control pair (rank $\leq 1$ proven vs rank $\geq 2$ open).** This survey IS the control pair, written out by an authority. The entire proven body (Theorem 24, Theorem 31, Theorems 32-33, Theorem 91) is rank $\leq 1$; the entire open body (finiteness of $\mathrm{Sha}$ in general, $\mathscr{L}_E$ rationality for $g_E \geq 2$, the $2$-part) is rank $\geq 2$ or the $2$-adic completion of rank $0$. Tian's method is structurally a rank-$1$ machine: it manufactures a single Heegner point of infinite order in a twist with $w = -1$, then hands off to Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin. It produces one point and so, like every Heegner construction, cannot reach rank $2$. This is exactly the "one missing object" framing of [`01_rank_two_object.md`](../research_directions/01_rank_two_object.md): the best current new work still lives below the rank-$2$ wall.

6. **What it feeds.** Primarily [`03_padic_archimedean.md`](../research_directions/03_padic_archimedean.md) (the survey is a compact tour of the Kronecker-Eisenstein special-value bridge and the $2$-adic integrality that powers the $2$-part) and [`02_higher_rank_euler_system.md`](../research_directions/02_higher_rank_euler_system.md) (the averaging-lemma / induction structure of Tian's argument is an Euler-system-flavored bound on twisted $L$-values, and Theorems 32-33 are the main-conjecture inputs).
